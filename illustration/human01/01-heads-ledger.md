    identical at 32, 60 — and, one generation up, at 80.
22. Repetition pays immediately: both repeats (Carmen, stool reader)
    improved by applying logged lessons, not by more effort.

## Session 4 lessons

16. The bowed and upward heads are solved as ARMATURES, not bent
    frontals: upward = eyes above midline / ears low / underside plane
    drawn; bowed = crown dominant / eyes below midline / ears high.
    Both need their planes weighted heavier than instinct.
17. Occlusion is a tool: the newspaper cropping a bowed face makes the
    bow legible (h039, h040). What hides part of the head explains the
    rest of it.
18. What survives photographs (the session's question): Jennifer = the
    smile structure, never the hair. Jim = grin asymmetry + squint +
    jaw. Jordan = brow-gaze relationship + the nose. Jensen = face
    length + brow weight. In every case the survivor is a RELATIONSHIP,
    not a feature.
19. Family resemblance is drawable: shared pale-eye treatment and lean
    cheeks across four heads, with identity living in the proportions
    between them.

## Session 3 lessons

12. The bowed head is its own subject. Frontal rules (eye midline,

13. Long hair keeps defaulting to a hood. Crown lift + S-waves + end

14. A beard is hair: silhouette first transfers perfectly (h023).
15. Likeness of the young lives in three decisions — hairline behavior,
    brow attitude, iris treatment — because everything else must stay
    unmarked.

## Session 2 lessons

7. Hair mass: silhouette first, heavy line, then at most three interior

8. Eye variety is built from the LID, not the iris: fold height, opening

11. Profile heads need MORE horizontal room for the face than instinct

## Lessons forced by this batch

1. Eyes sit at the vertical midpoint of the skull. Every first attempt put
   them high and grew a snout.
2. No hairline arc = bald. The hairline is a feature, not an implication.
3. The jaw has a corner. A 45° taper from cheek to chin is the default face
   trying to come back.
4. Tilt the whole head. Nobody holds their skull plumb; symmetry is the
   mannequin.
5. Age = interior mark count. In economy line, every crease, dash, and
   groove is ten years. Draw a young face by leaving marks out.
6. Spline scallops invert: midpoints must belly DOWN for curls or you get
   thorns. Two lazy arcs beat four sharp ones.
