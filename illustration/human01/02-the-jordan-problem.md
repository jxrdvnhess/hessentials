# THE JORDAN PROBLEM
## Human Studies Vol. 1 — research report. Observation only; nothing drawn.

Corpus reviewed: 46 photographs in `Personal Photos/Jordan Hess`, the
17-photo packet provided in session 3, the Instagram and Facebook
profile observations, and the leaf/patio/couch candids — roughly 70
distinct exposures across an estimated 6–8 years, four facial-hair
states, at least six hair states, two body-weight states, indoor/
outdoor/flash/golden-hour/overcast light, selfie, mirror, and
other-photographer framings.

Subject data (provided by the subject for this study): b. May 19, 1994 —
32 at the time of writing, so the archive spans roughly ages 24–32.
5'11", ~150 lbs; the whole family thin and active. This recalibrates
section 1: what I read as two "body-weight states" sits inside a 150 lb
frame — the variation is cheek fullness within leanness, never build.
Build is therefore a sixth constant: the long light frame and the long
neck under every head, and it is a FAMILY constant (one more reason the
roster reads as related).

Likeness frame applied (per the study requirement, applied from the
shared core of the Bauman / Proko / Court Jones tradition, full course
study deferred and flagged): likeness is not features rendered well; it
is the measured DEVIATION of this head's relationships from the average
head, and recognition rides on the few deviations that persist.

## 1. What changes most often — evidence

HAIR STATE changes most. Six distinct states in the corpus: cropped
sun-spiked (beach with Greg); tall textured quiff (black-tank kitchen);
full swept-back volume (glasses kitchen selfie, firepit); flattened
against a seat (bus window); product-slick night (white-shirt dinner);
under-cap/messy (car shots). FACIAL HAIR cycles through four states:
clean (couch portrait), light stubble (patio, candle birthday), short
beard (IG profile pic era), shadow (several mirror fits). EYEWEAR is
four systems: none (~half), clear half-rims (kitchen, firepit), black
rectangles (cruise cabin), aviators (Spain street). SKIN VALUE swings
two full steps between winter picnic and the Spain/beach tan. CAMERA
GEOMETRY varies hugely: phone-below car selfies (jaw underside, brow
compression), arm's-length mirror shots (level, slight wide-angle
stretch), and other-photographer candids (true proportions — the couch
portrait is the least distorted document in the archive).

## 2. What survives most consistently — evidence

a. THE BROW–GAZE UNIT. Straight, low-set brows with a SHORT vertical
gap to deep-set pale eyes. Present in every exposure: the 3/4 picnic
stare, the frontal kitchen lean-in, the from-below car shots (where the
gap compresses to nearly nothing), the couch laugh (where everything
else changes and this doesn't). Even in the eyes-closed pout, the brow
height and straightness hold the face together.

b. THE NOSE. Long straight dorsum, defined tip. It leads every 3/4
view and survives the from-below foreshortening as visible nostril
geometry. No photograph contradicts it.

c. THE MIDFACE INTERVAL. Brow-line to nose-base is the longest
vertical interval of the face — a long midface over lean cheeks with
high bone. Constant across both weight states (the fuller-cheek photos
change the CONTOUR, not the interval).

d. HAIR AS VECTOR, NOT STYLE. In all six hair states the hair moves UP
and/or BACK off the forehead. It is never down onto it. The hairline
itself — strong center, two shallow temple notches — is identical from
the earliest photo to the newest.

e. MOUTH AT REST. Level parting line, corners that refuse commitment,
fuller lower lip. The composed set recurs in suit, tank, transit, and
grocery aisle; the deadpan head (h061) is this set weaponized.

## 3. What appeared important but was not

Hairstyle (six states; recognition held through all of them — Test 01
used a sun-spiked crop never drawn before). Facial hair (four states;
the beard in h008 misled the first drawing toward age, not identity).
Glasses (worn in under a third of exposures; never required for
recognition). Tan. Clothing register — tank to suit changes the
photograph's class, not the person. And most instructively: THE SMILE.
I assumed early that likeness would ride on expression; the corpus
shows Jordan recognized in pout, deadpan, slack transit, and joy alike.
Expression is weather. The brow-gaze unit is climate.

## 4. The five recognition anchors, ranked

1. Brow–gaze unit (straight low brows + deep-set pale outlined irises
   + short brow-to-eye gap). Evidence: only element present and
   unchanged in all ~70 exposures; the Test 01 head shared no hair, no
   mouth state, and no lighting with prior heads — the judge still
   answered instantly.
2. Nose (long straight dorsum, defined tip). Evidence: carries the 3/4
   and from-below views where the eyes foreshorten away.
3. Midface interval + lean high-boned cheek. Evidence: the family
   separator — Jensen's face is longer overall with heavier brows, Jim's
   midface is shorter; this interval is what makes the four roster heads
   four different people while visibly related.
4. Hair vector up/back + temple-notched hairline. Evidence: a behavior
   that survived six styles; drawn as direction, it reads as him at any
   length.
5. Mouth at rest (level, uncommitted corners, fuller lower lip).
   Evidence: the deadpan reads as comedy only because this set is his
   baseline; on another face the same mouth would be unremarkable.

Family note: the pale-eye treatment is shared with Jim and Jensen, the
smile structure with Jennifer. None of the five anchors above is
inherited whole — recognition of Jordan specifically lives in their
combination, chiefly 1 and 3.

## If I had only ten lines

1. The far face contour in one stroke — temple, high cheekbone, lean
   cheek, jaw corner, chin. This single line carries anchor 3 and
   implies the chin and jaw without spending lines on them.
2. The skull-and-hair mass in one sweep, rising off the forehead and
   breaking back — anchor 4 as a vector, not a hairstyle.
3. The hairline with its two shallow temple notches.
4. Left brow — straight, low. 5. Right brow — straight, low, one pixel
   of extra mobility. Together they are half of anchor 1; they set the
   gaze before any eye exists.
6. Left lash line with the slight outer hood.
7. One outlined iris, pale, never filled — the other eye stays implied;
   the pallor only needs to be stated once.
8. The nose: dorsum and tip as a single line — anchor 2 entire.
9. The mouth parting line: level, corners uncommitted — anchor 5's
   load-bearing line.
10. The lower-lip fullness stroke beneath it.

Dropped without loss: ears, neck, chin shelf (implied by line 1),
philtrum, second iris, nostril wings, every interior cheek mark. The
ten lines hold all five anchors; everything else in my Jordan heads has
been, by this account, commentary.

Why these ten: each one states a RELATIONSHIP (interval, vector, gap,
set) rather than rendering a feature. That is also why the volume's
promoted doctrine predicted this list before the analysis confirmed it.
