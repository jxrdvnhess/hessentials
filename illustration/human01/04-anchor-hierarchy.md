# ANCHOR HIERARCHY STUDY
## Human Studies Vol. 1, Session 11 — observation only; nothing drawn.

Method: for each roster subject, a ranked hierarchy with the removal
test run downward until recognition is predicted to fail. Evidence
cited from the corpus and from the two Recognition Tests (RT-01 passed
4/4 with eyes available; RT-02 passed 4/4 with eye regions withheld).
Predictions are stated so the next test can break them.

## JORDAN — hierarchy

1. Brow-gaze unit (level low brows, deep-set pale eyes, short gap)
2. Midface interval + lean high-boned cheek (the within-family separator)
3. Nose (long straight dorsum, defined tip)
4. Hair vector (up/back, temple-notched hairline)
5. Mouth at rest (level, uncommitted corners, fuller lower lip)

Removal test. Remove 1: SURVIVES — empirically proven (RT-02 aviators;
identified third, called "iconic," carried by 2+3+4). Remove 1+3 (eyes
AND nose hidden — goggles-and-scarf condition): predicted DEGRADATION
to near-type; hair vector and build alone are family-generic. Remove
1+3+4 (add a hat): predicted FAILURE. Jordan's identity is a
CO-DOMINANT PAIR — brow-gaze × midface interval — with the nose as the
strongest single backup. No single point of failure; two removals
required before collapse.

## JENNIFER — hierarchy

1. Smile structure ENTIRE (crescent crush + cheek apples + upper teeth
   + the folds that frame it)
2. The crescents alone (when the eye region is visible)
3. Hair mass (long blonde waves)
4. Slim, slightly pointed chin
5. Cheekbone height

Removal test. Remove 2 (eyes): SURVIVES — proven (RT-02 sunglasses;
the smile carried her alone). Remove 1 — a NEUTRAL Jennifer: this
condition is UNOBSERVED. Every photograph in the corpus smiles; the
neutral Jennifer is this roster's "worried Jordan" — it cannot be drawn
honestly and cannot yet be tested. PREDICTION: without the smile,
recognition falls to hair + chin and weakens severely. Jennifer is the
roster's CONCENTRATED case — identity distributed most unequally, one
anchor carrying a dominant share. Corroborating scar: the volume's
three hood failures were all Jennifer-hair failures, and they nearly
destroyed likeness despite a correct smile — when one anchor carries
this much, the second-rank anchors punch above their weight.

## JIM — hierarchy

1. Grin asymmetry (left corner leads; open, dug-in folds)
2. Squint + crow rays (the eye region's own signature)
3. Lean weathered jaw
4. Short neat side-combed hair / high forehead
5. Lean athletic build (shared family channel)

Removal test. Remove 2 (eyes): SURVIVES — proven (RT-02 wraparounds;

opened eyes carried him. Jim is the roster's REDUNDANT case: two
strong anchors, either sufficient. Removing both (neutral Jim, eyes
hidden) is predicted to collapse him to "lean older athlete" — but
neither single removal harms him. Most robust subject on the roster.

## THE JENSEN PROBLEM — why stance defeated facial information

Description forbidden; explanation attempted, in two parts.

1. RECOGNITION IS DISCRIMINATION WITHIN A CANDIDATE SET. The judge
   was not finding Jensen among all humans; he was finding him among
   four Hesses. Within that set, facial channels carry LOW
   between-subject variance — the family resemblance the volume
   documented (shared pale eyes, lean cheeks, lean frames) actively
   suppresses facial diagnosticity. Age-coded body signal is the
   channel where the four differ MOST: Jensen is twenty years younger
   than Jim, postured differently from Jordan, built differently from
   Jennifer. The faceless figure was not the weakest stimulus; within
   this roster it was the single highest-contrast stimulus on the
   sheet. My prediction failed because I ranked anchors by information
   CONTENT when I should have ranked them by information CONTRAST
   against the live alternatives.

2. THE JUDGE'S EXPOSURE HISTORY WEIGHTS THE CHANNELS. A family member
   has a lifetime of non-facial training data — brothers seen at
   distance, from behind, across yards and parking lots. The judge's
   recognition system is over-trained on exactly the channel I called
   weakest. A stranger-judge given the same sheet is predicted to fail
   the distance figure first and the sunglasses heads last — the exact
   inverse of this judge.

CONSEQUENCE, stated as the session's finding: ANCHOR DOMINANCE IS NOT
A PROPERTY OF THE SUBJECT. It is a property of the triple — subject ×
candidate set × judge. Hierarchies are conditional. The volume's
earlier rankings were not wrong; they were rankings for the condition
"this subject among strangers, judged by anyone." RT-02 was the
condition "this subject among kin, judged by kin." Both hierarchies
are real; neither is absolute.

Falsifiable prediction for the next test: seat ONE roster head among
four stranger heads of similar age and build, judge = Jordan. The
roster member will be found instantly (kin-channel advantage). Then
show the same sheet to anyone else: the stranger-judge will rank the
roster head no easier than its neighbors. If the stranger-judge ALSO
finds the roster head easily, the conditional-dominance claim is
wrong and something in my drawings is privileging the roster (a style
tell, not a likeness) — which would be the most useful failure the
volume could produce.
