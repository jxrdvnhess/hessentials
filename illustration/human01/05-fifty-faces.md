# ASSIGNMENT 2 — FIFTY FACES
## Opened June 11, 2026. Expression, not identity.

Standing principle (faculty, accepted without amendment): Assignment 1
taught that observation defeats assumption; this assignment is
teaching that TIME defeats assumption. A still hides the mechanics; a
sequence reveals them. The subject of study is never what worry looks
like — it is how worry ARRIVES. Transitions, not outcomes. Every
collection decision and every eventual drawing answers to that
sentence.

exhausted 0/10 · ambiguous 0/10

## The rule this assignment inherits

Assignment 1 ended by proving that the default human returns the
moment observation is delegated. Assignment 2 begins under the same
law, applied to feeling: NO FACE IS DRAWN FROM MEMORY OF AN EMOTION.
Every face here must be observed in a photograph first — especially
the worried ten, because worry is the state this volume has never
once observed in 105 heads. A worried face drawn without observation
would be a symbol, and the ledger of the last hundred heads is a
list of what symbols cost.

## Observation plan (sources, to be walked next session)

Worried — candid documentary photography: sideline coaches mid-play,
waiting rooms, departure boards during delays, owners at vet clinics.
The face managing uncertainty, not reacting to event.
Neutral — passport/archival ID photography, people mid-task unaware
(readers, machinists, passengers); reject every trace of pose.
Amused — audiences BEFORE the laugh: pre-punchline crowd photographs,
listeners at readings; the mouth not yet moved, the eyes already gone.
Exhausted — end-of-shift industrial photography, marathon finish
lines, night-transit riders; observe whether physical, mental, and
emotional exhaustion sit in different parts of the face.
Ambiguous — Magnum-register candids: listeners, rememberers, people
holding something back; faces that resist captioning. Family archive
permitted where states are honest and unperformed.

## The edges rule (faculty amendment, accepted before production)

Collect the moments BEFORE and AFTER emotions, not the peaks. The face
before the laugh over the laugh; the face after the news over the
grief; the face trying not to show worry over worry shown. Emotions
have default faces exactly as people do, and the defaults live at the
peaks — symbols grow where expression is loudest. People are most
themselves at the edges of their states. Observe there. Every source
in the plan above is re-aimed accordingly: not the sideline coach
screaming, but the one watching the kick in the air; not the finisher
crossing, but the finisher ten steps after.

## ASSIGNMENT 2C — THE REDUCTION TEST (2026-06-12)

Five strongest 2B events reduced A→B→C→D until death. Kill

contact: out/_contact_2c.png. All five predictions held.

R1 · THE CRADLE (i08). Removed: A eye-line and leg; B closing
forearm, jaw, body-line, hand; C the sprawl; D the bowed head.
SURVIVED: the hold, down to THREE strokes — arc, bowed head,
small head. The trust died at C with the sprawl; the hold lived
on without it. KILLED BY: removing the bow. At two strokes the
drawing is a bowl with an egg. Attention is the hold.

R2 · THE KISS (i01). Removed: A hair, second eye, throat; B jaw
and chin; C smile and cheek-line; D the lip-notch and the closed
eye. SURVIVED: affection, at four strokes — two meeting contours,
a lip-notch, one closed eye. KILLED BY: removing attitude.
Contact without the notch and the closed eye is collision: two
balloons touching. The kiss is not the contact; it is the
attitude at the contact.

R3 · THE GREETING (i09). Removed: A shoulders and two finger
ticks; B remaining ticks and grin-depth; C the eyes; D the palms.
SURVIVED: to about six strokes, weakening. KILLED BY: removing
the palms — without them it is a smiling face and the second
person vanishes. SECONDARY OBSERVATION, unplanned: at B and C the
tickless palms began reading as EARS. My own hands failed the
same way the foreign hands fail — the finger ticks were
load-bearing. The standing hand-failure surfaced inside my own
figure. The minimum hand is not an oval; it is an oval plus
fingers, and nothing less.

R4 · TELLING AND RECEIVING (i03). Removed: A teller's eyes and
lids; B receiver's second brow and mouth-depth; C brows and
smile; D the receiver's irises. SURVIVED: the between, at FIVE
strokes — two contours, one open mouth, two aimed irises.
Reception survives on irises alone. KILLED BY: removing them —
a person talking beside an egg is not an interaction. The gaze
is the between.

R5 · THE EMBRACE (i02). Removed: A eyes, chest-line, arm detail;
B mouth and one arm edge; C the torso; D the head. SURVIVED: to
six strokes. KILLED BY: removing the torso, exactly as 2B taught
and the pre-registration predicted — the arm over nothing became
the railing again, on schedule. D (arm alone) rendered as the
past-death control: a stick.

REDUCTION RESULT, recorded as observation: in all five events the
kill was the same kind of removal. The event died when the
ATTENDING element went (the bow, the closed eye, the aimed
irises, the addressed palms) or when the RECEIVING BODY went (the
torso under the arm). Contact, objects, and gestures never died
first and never carried the event alone. Floors observed: hold 3
strokes · kiss 4 · between 5 · embrace 6 · greeting 6. The
minimum amount of drawing required for a human event to remain
human is, on this page, the attention plus whatever receives it.
