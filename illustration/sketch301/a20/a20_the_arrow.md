# Sketch 301 — Assignment 20: The Arrow

As assigned: the missing baseline beneath A17–A19. Is the instance channel weak, or
merely unused? One visual element — directional arrows — with no system, no labels, no
familiar grammar around them.

## Materials

- **A — single:** one solid arrow, pointing right. Nothing else.
- **B — competing:** two arrows, opposite directions, equal weight.
- **C — interrupted:** one arrow, its mid-shaft occluded by a grey rounded bar; head
  and tail visible, alignment recoverable.
- **D — ambiguous:** ambiguity inside the form: a shaft with a solid head on the
  right and a notched block on the left that reads as fletching (pointing right) or
  as an open head (pointing left).

Fresh viewers: 8 sessions (2 per image, Sonnet/Haiku marked). "What is it?" only.

### The responses, condensed

- **A:** "a simple black arrow pointing to the right... the kind you'd typically see
  as a UI element, navigation indicator..." (Haiku) — and, from the Sonnet: "It is a
  simple, bold right-pointing arrow — a horizontal line with a triangular arrowhead
  on the right end — rendered in dark near-black against a light off-white
  background. **Clean, minimal, no decoration.**" Full stop. No use, no story, no
  audience.
- **B:** "two horizontal arrows pointing in opposite directions... commonly used to
  represent a **transfer, exchange, swap, or two-way transaction**" / "back-and-forth
  navigation, exchange, reversal... **balance or opposition**."
- **C:** "a thick horizontal arrow **passing through — or behind — a small rounded
  rectangle**... something **moving through or exiting a container**" / "input on the
  left, **some kind of transformation or action happening inside the box**, and
  output emerging on the right... how something moves through a system or gets
  processed."
- **D:** "a **double-headed arrow**... arrowheads pointing in both directions
  simultaneously... suggests bidirectionality, width, or **a span between two
  points**" / "a pair of chevron-style arrows... **bidirectional movement... swap
  function... push-pull or balance**."

## Observations

1. **The channel is not weak.** Direction was reported accurately in 8 of 8 sessions
   — including through occlusion, where both viewers recovered the arrow's
   continuation behind the bar without effort. At the floor, instance-reading is
   fast, correct, and unanimous. A19's zero-instance result cannot be explained by a
   broken channel.

2. **The channel never appears alone — except once.** Seven of eight responses
   recruited purpose, system, or story within the same breath as the direction: UI,
   navigation, exchange, flowchart, container, span. The bare reading exists and is
   then immediately built upon. Read against A19: in system-rich images the building
   (template) REPLACES the reading; here, with nothing to recruit, the building is
   thin enough that the reading stays visible underneath.

3. **The exception is the most interesting response in the assignment.** One viewer,
   on the single arrow, produced geometry, palette, and "no decoration" — and
   stopped. The closest thing to a systemless report in twenty assignments and
   ~150 sessions arrived NOT at the ceiling of corruption (A14's engineered boundary
   case) but at the FLOOR of simplicity. Abandonment, hunted by making images
   harder, may live where images become too simple to need a system. ("Arrow" is
   itself a category-word; this is recruitment-minimal, not recruitment-free.
   Stated precisely.)

4. **Competition was resolved by synthesis, not choice.** The two opposing arrows
   produced no tension, no preference, no uncertainty in either viewer — they were
   unified into a single concept (exchange, balance). The course's longest-running
   pattern (conflict becomes design: A8 occluders, A9 gaps, A18 convergences) holds
   at two arrows on an empty ground.

5. **Ambiguity was resolved by the lexicon, convergently.** Both D viewers
   regularized the asymmetric form into the SAME known symbol — the bidirectional
   arrow — which legalizes both directions at once, and reported zero hedges. Where
   A4-C's mode-conflict produced scatter (head/lollipop/tree), the icon vocabulary
   is apparently shared tightly enough that regularization converges. The designed
   instability never reached any report; the nearest sign absorbed it.

6. **Zero hedges anywhere.** A19's violation cards leaked disjunctions; A20's
   genuinely ambiguous FORM leaked none. The hedge-instrument apparently fires on
   system-level mismatch, not form-level ambiguity — when the lexicon can absorb a
   form, grammar stays fluent. (Sharpens the instrument's domain; carried.)

7. **The occluder was hired** (C): "container," "processing unit," "transformation
   happening inside the box." A8's occluder-becomes-second-object, replicated at
   icon scale — but with a difference worth keeping: the arrow's continuity SURVIVED
   (direction recovered), while the occluder was promoted into the story. At the
   floor, completion and recruitment cooperate instead of competing.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The baseline reframes A17–A19: the instance channel is high-fidelity but
  LOW-PRIORITY. It is consulted when nothing richer is available and overwritten
  the moment a system, template, or lexeme can claim the marks. On this account the
  gradient template→fog→forced-template is a priority stack, not a capacity limit —
  and the fog cards of A19 sat at a hideous middle where the template half-claimed
  the marks and the instance was already deprioritized.
- Obs 3 suggests recruitment has a THRESHOLD, not just a hierarchy: below some
  complexity, there is nothing for a system to do, and description can surface
  bare. If so, the long-sought "systemless report" was never going to appear under
  corruption — corruption ADDS work for systems. It appears under poverty.

## Open questions

- Where exactly is the recruitment threshold? A ladder from one arrow to
  two-arrows-plus-a-dot to three elements would locate the complexity at which
  purpose-talk becomes obligatory. (One viewer at n=1 element already abstained.)
- Is the single-arrow abstention viewer-typical or stochastic? (One Sonnet; the
  paired Haiku recruited UI immediately.)
- Does the lexicon-absorption of D generalize: is form-ambiguity ALWAYS absorbed by
  the nearest shared symbol when one exists, and scattered (A4-C-style) only when
  none does?
- The hedge-instrument's domain is now half-mapped (fires on system-mismatch, silent
  on form-ambiguity). What about lexicon-mismatch — a form equidistant from two
  shared symbols?

*No findings. The channel underneath is sound; what stands on top of it almost never
lets it speak — and the one time it spoke, the image had given the systems nothing
to do.*
