# Sketch 301 — Assignment 23: Perceived Configuration

As assigned: what determines whether a configuration survives perception? Ring and
row, each at equal and aggressively unequal sizes. Same blob family, five elements,
nothing else. The question is whether arrangement survives distortion.

## Materials

- **a — equal ring:** five identical blobs (r=40) on an exact pentagon. A22's missing
  control.
- **b — unequal ring:** same pentagon, sizes 58/22/44/16/50 — stronger variation than
  A22's.
- **c — equal row:** five identical blobs, even spacing, one line.
- **d — unequal row:** same row, same aggressive sizes as b.

Fresh viewers, 1 Sonnet + 1 Haiku per image = 8 sessions. "What is it?" only.

## Observations

1. **Size variation destroyed the ring and spared the row.** Same five sizes, same
   distortion: the unequal ring died for both viewers ("arc or cluster";
   "asymmetrically... no obvious geometric order" — an explicit DENIAL of the order
   that is geometrically present), while the unequal row was reported as a row by
   both, with its size-structure described correctly. The assignment's question has
   a configuration-dependent answer: arrangement does not have one robustness;
   ROWS survive what RINGS cannot.

2. **The rival-grouping mechanism is visible in the data.** The unequal-ring Haiku
   grouped the elements BY SIZE — "three larger ones of varying sizes and two
   smaller ones" — abandoning position-grouping entirely. Size variation does not
   merely add noise; it offers a competing organization, and the pentagon lost to
   it. The row, whose spatial grouping is anchored by the shared line, kept its
   organization and let size become a property INSIDE it ("alternate in size")
   rather than a rival to it.

3. **The census tracked configuration coherence again.** Equal cells: counted
   correctly 4/4. Unequal ring: miscounted six-for-five by the Sonnet — the third
   census failure in the course, all three on incoherent/centre-periphery
   arrangements (A21/A22 scatter ×2, unequal ring ×1). Rows have never been
   miscounted. Counting continues to behave like an operation the arrangement
   affords or withholds (A22 Obs 3, second independent support).

4. **The series-recital quantizes to the nearest simple pattern.** A22's
   near-monotonic row sizes were falsely reported as "progressively smaller"; A23's
   strongly alternating sizes were correctly reported as alternating. The recital
   does not always overwrite measurement — it rounds it to the closest available
   order. Wrong when the truth is irregular, right when the truth happens to BE a
   pattern. (Two rows, two outcomes; held lightly.)

5. **The equal row went straight to the interface lexicon:** "a loading indicator, a
   pagination pattern," an ellipsis. Perfectly regular configurations appear to be
   OWNED, like A20-D's bidirectional glyph — regularity is itself a recruitment cue,
   claimed by the systems that traffic in regular dots. The unequal row, unclaimed
   by the lexicon, stayed a "study."

6. **The equal ring half-survived.** Sonnet named "a pentagonal ring"; Haiku gave
   correct positions but never said ring or circle — pattern without figure. Even
   the clean control transmits unevenly across viewers (A21's cohort effect again,
   now at the perception layer rather than the recruitment layer).

7. Both Haiku sessions cited ambient brand material [†] — contamination noted,
   confined to register-talk.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- A configuration survives distortion to the extent that its organizing feature is
  orthogonal to the distorted property. The row is organized by collinearity —
  untouched by size. The ring is organized by an implied contour through the
  elements' centers, and that contour's support depends on visual weight — exactly
  what size variation disturbs; worse, variation supplies a rival grouping (big vs
  small) that bids for the same elements. Robustness = orthogonality plus absence
  of rivals. (Fits all four cells; untested beyond them.)
- The implied-contour account predicts: an unequal ring with elements CONNECTED by
  a faint line would survive (the contour made explicit); an unequal ring with
  EQUAL spacing but more elements (8–10) might also survive (more samples of the
  contour). Both cheap; neither run.

## Open questions

- The two predictions above — the explicit-contour rescue and the dense ring.
- Is there a size-variation level that kills the row? (Aggressive variation didn't;
  what about 10:1?)
- The quantization observation (Obs 4): does the recital round arbitrary magnitudes
  to nearest-pattern in other registers — three slightly-unequal columns reported
  as equal, a near-square reported as square?
- The census: is over-counting specifically a centre-periphery artifact (the
  "orbiting" reading multiplying satellites), now three-for-three on such
  arrangements, or general to any unclaimed scatter?

*No findings. The ring died of its sizes, the row didn't notice them, the census
obeyed the survivor — and regularity itself turned out to be a recruitment cue.*
