# Sketch 301 — Assignment 7 (self-assigned): The Wordless Promise

Per the A6 recommendation: stop investigating announcements; return to images.
Construct an image that appears to promise additional information without any verbal
instruction. The preserved question, in its craft form.

## Method

Two withholding mechanisms, each with a matched control. No announcements anywhere; one
fixed question ("What is it?"); fresh viewers (2 Sonnet + 1 Haiku per image), single
viewings, neutral filenames.

- **Pair 1 — content withheld.** A domestic note ("Monday — buy lemons / return the
  ladder / call about the gate / water the fig").
  P1: text at threshold size (13px, soft). C1: same note fully legible (30px).
  P1b (added mid-run): text pushed further down (9px, softer) after P1's threshold
  proved to sit below Sonnet acuity — calibration failure documented, not hidden.
- **Pair 2 — identity withheld.** A still life: dark cup sharp in the foreground;
  behind it, a jar holding scissors. P2: background defocused (9px blur on that layer
  only). C2: everything sharp.
- Scoring: does the viewer report information as PRESENT-BUT-WITHHELD (the image
  successfully communicated that more exists), hedge its assertion, fill the withheld
  slot, or read the withholding as style?

## Responses (condensed to the relevant behavior)

**P1 (threshold note, 13px):** All three viewers simply read the list. Sonnet ×2
verbatim and complete. Haiku read it as legible but misread one line — "four farmers"
for "buy lemons" — without flagging any difficulty. No withholding registered;
P1 behaved as C1. (The misreading wears the voice of reading — A1's invention pattern
at the letter level.)

**C1 (legible note):** All three read it fully, committed, no hedges.

**P1b (deep-threshold note, 9px):**
- Sonnet 1: "It reads **something like** 'Monday —' followed by... 'buy lemons,'
  'return the ladder'..." — content recovered, but assertion status dropped to hedged.
- Sonnet 2: "five short lines that **read like** a to-do list... (something like 'buy
  lemons'...)" — same: recovered through a marked veil.
- Haiku: "...several short lines, **possibly a few words or a brief note, though the
  text is too small to read clearly in this image.**" — the target behavior, verbatim:
  information reported as present and inaccessible. No invention, no filling. The
  image made the promise and the viewer registered it — wordlessly.

**P2 (defocused background):**
- Sonnet 1: "...a blurred, out-of-focus **bottle or decanter** sitting on a shelf...
  The depth-of-field effect... gives it a quiet, composed, almost photographic
  sensibility."
- Sonnet 2: "...**a bottle or decanter with a stopper — possibly a perfume bottle or
  small carafe**..."
- Haiku: "...a translucent **glass bottle** blurred in the background... recessive and
  dreamlike."

The background is a jar of scissors. Nobody reported something-they-couldn't-make-out.
All three named the withheld object — same wrong family (bottle/decanter), the
scissors' loop handles read as a **stopper**.

**C2 (sharp control):** All three correctly: a cup/holder with scissors in it, beside
a dark mug. The background object reads instantly when given.

## Observations

1. **The promise was achieved once, wordlessly — by withheld WRITING, and only past
   the viewer's acuity.** P1b/Haiku reported present-but-unreadable text with no
   invention: the image alone communicated "there is more here than you are getting."
   The Sonnets at the same level read through the veil but dropped to hedged assertion
   ("something like") — the withholding registered as reduced certainty even where it
   failed to withhold.

2. **The promise is viewer-relative.** One image, three viewers, three positions:
   read-plainly (P1 for everyone), read-with-hedges (P1b for Sonnet),
   reported-as-withheld (P1b for Haiku). "Does this image promise more?" has no
   viewer-independent answer in this data; the same construction whispers to one
   acuity and speaks plainly to another.

3. **Defocus did not withhold — it assigned.** P2's blurred jar-of-scissors was not
   reported as unresolvable; it was named, by all three viewers, as a member of the
   blur-compatible category (bottle/decanter/carafe), and the one feature that should
   have resisted (scissors handles) was renamed to fit ("a stopper"). Blur functioned
   as evidence, not absence. A5's heavy-blur behavior (confident wrong verdicts)
   replicated inside a multi-object scene.

4. **The scissors became a stopper.** A rendering treatment alone (defocus) renamed a
   feature — second object, second mode, no card present. Carried toward A4 Obs 4
   (mode renames features), which was awaiting replication: this is one more instance,
   in a different mechanism (defocus rather than tonal/diagram mode-mixing). Adjacent,
   not merged: A4-C mixed representational grammars; this is optical. The pattern is
   accumulating, not yet earned.

5. **The withholding itself was read as style.** P2 viewers attributed the blur to
   photography ("depth-of-field," "almost photographic sensibility," "dreamlike") —
   in a flat graphic illustration. The withholding device was absorbed as an aesthetic
   mode, and the mode then licensed the bottle reading (bottles are what sit
   photogenically defocused behind cups). Nothing about the blur said "look again";
   everything about it said "this is that kind of picture."

6. **Asymmetry between the two mechanisms** (the assignment's core result, stated as
   observation): the withheld region that DECLARED ITS FORMAT (writing — instantly
   classified as writing since A1) produced withholding reports and hedges; the
   withheld region with no declared format produced confident filling. Where viewers
   knew WHAT they were missing (words), they could miss it; where they didn't, they
   didn't miss anything — they had an answer.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- A wordless promise of more information may require the image to specify the FORM of
  the missing information while withholding its content. Writing does this natively:
  its format announces "content exists here." An unformatted blur specifies nothing,
  so the candidate-filling machinery (A1 Obs 9, A4 Obs 3) supplies a complete object
  and no absence is ever experienced. If this holds, the craft rule would be: to
  promise more, withhold content inside a declared container — never withhold the
  container itself. Untested as a rule; one pair each.
- P2's style-absorption suggests a second route to failure: a withholding device that
  matches a known aesthetic convention gets classified AS the convention (re-leveling,
  A1 Obs 1) and stops being read as withholding at all.

## Open questions

- Other format-declaring containers besides writing: a map legend too small to read, a
  labeled diagram with the labels withheld, a photograph-within-the-image turned
  half away? Does the container/content split hold beyond text?
- Is there a defocus level that stops assigning and starts withholding — or does an
  unformatted blur ALWAYS get filled at every level (A5's ladder suggests filling
  persists to extreme blur)?
- The hedged middle state ("something like...") — is that the usable craft register?
  A viewer who reads the content but feels the veil may be the one who looks again.
  The full promise (Haiku's report) ends the look; the hedge might extend it. Which
  state does editorial illustration actually want?
- The promise was demonstrated for content (words), not identity. Can an image promise
  more IDENTITY — "you will know what this is if you stay" — without text? (A4-B's
  serration is a candidate: present, consultable, late. But A4/A5 showed viewers who
  stop early never feel its absence.)
- Viewer-relativity (Obs 2) cuts at every brand decision: threshold tuned for whom?
