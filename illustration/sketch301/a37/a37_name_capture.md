# Sketch 301 — Assignment 37: Name Capture

As assigned, resolving the A36 open: the COUNT moved with context, but the NAME stuck
at "eighth." Can a stronger context flip the NAME too? Same ambiguous-flag target, now
in heavy uniform contexts, plus a beamed-sixteenth positive control to check whether
"sixteenth" is even reachable for these viewers.

## Materials

- **a — alone:** the ambiguous target (1 full + 1 partial flag).
- **b — strong sixteenth:** target among SEVEN clear sixteenth notes (2 flags each).
- **c — strong eighth:** target among seven clear eighth notes (1 flag each).
- **d — beamed sixteenth (positive control):** four notes joined by a DOUBLE BEAM —
  constitutively a sixteenth group; no flag ambiguity.

Fresh viewers, 1 Sonnet + 1 Haiku per image = 8 sessions. "What is it?" only.

## Observations

1. **The name did NOT flip under strong flagged context — but DID under beaming.**
   Seven clearly double-flagged sixteenth notes were still called "eighth notes" by
   the Sonnet (and "quarter notes" by the Haiku); strengthening the flagged context
   from three neighbors (A36) to seven did not capture the name, and here even
   collapsed the COUNT back to "single flag." Yet the beamed double-beam group was
   named "sixteenth notes" correctly and confidently. So the answer to "can context
   change the name" is conditional: **soft context (flagged neighbors) cannot flip the
   name; a constitutive structural cue (beaming) can.** The name is reachable — it
   just answers to a different kind of context than the count does.

2. **The name/count dissociation from A36 sharpens into a rule of DIFFERENT
   sensitivities.** Count is recruited by SOFT context — neighbor-homogenization and
   sequence (A36: 1/2/1/3). Name is prototype-locked against soft context and moves
   only under a CONSTITUTIVE cue that IS the identity (a double beam is, by definition,
   a sixteenth; flagged neighbors merely resemble one). Two outputs, two different
   recruiters: the count follows the company; the name follows the grammar.

3. **The positive control did its job — "sixteenth" is in the vocabulary.** D rules
   out the trivial explanation ("these viewers just don't say sixteenth"). The Sonnet
   said it the instant beaming forced it. So B's failure-to-flip is a real property of
   flagged context, not a vocabulary gap. (Clean control; the assignment's load-bearing
   cell.)

4. **Strong UNIFORM context pulled the count toward the PROTOTYPE, not the neighbors.**
   The surprise vs A36: three sixteenth-neighbors gave the target "double flag"
   (count → neighbors), but seven gave "single flag each" (count → eighth prototype).
   A uniform field of many same notes seems to recruit "a row of [prototype] notes" as
   a single gestalt and recite the prototype's count across all of them — overriding
   even the visible double flags. Quantity past a threshold flips the recruiter from
   "match the neighbors" to "name the whole row as the prototype kind." (Noted, not
   over-read; it complicates the simple "count follows context" of A36 — context can
   recruit the PROTOTYPE when the field is uniform enough.)

5. **The Haiku named DOWN to "quarter" in the dense rows** — its perception of small
   flags degraded further at seven-note density (b: "quarter notes," d: "quarter notes
   on a staff," missing both the flags and the beams). At this scale the Haiku is not
   a clean tally cohort; it falls to the plainest prototype (quarter = no flag). A37's
   cohort data is therefore weak (same limitation owned in A36); the load-bearing
   results are the Sonnet's name-stick (b) vs name-flip (d).

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The two channels now have distinct recruiters: **COUNT is recited from the recruited
  identity, and the identity for count can be recruited softly (neighbors, sequence,
  or — when the field is uniform and large — the prototype). NAME is recited from a
  CONSTITUTIVE structural reading and defaults to the prototype ("eighth," the
  canonical flagged note) unless a cue that constitutes a different identity (beaming)
  is present.** This explains every note cell A34–A37: flags route through the
  eighth-prototype name while their count flexes with context; beams constitute the
  name directly.
- Why beaming captures the name where flags don't: a double beam has no eighth-note
  reading at all — it is unambiguous and constitutive, like the die-five's pip count.
  Flags are read through a prototype that tolerates "extra flags on an eighth." The
  name flips only when the cue leaves no prototype reading available. (Mirrors A33:
  enforcement/identity needs a cue where the count IS the name; here the NAME flips
  only under such a cue.)

## Open questions

- The uniform-field prototype pull (Obs 4): dose the neighbor count (3 → 5 → 7
  sixteenths) and find where the target's reported count flips from "double" (match)
  to "single" (prototype). Locates the match→prototype threshold.
- Can a SOFT cue ever flip the name — e.g., an explicit ascending beam-count run
  (1 beam, 2 beams, 3 beams, target) where the rule, not a constitutive single cue,
  implies "sixteenth+"? (A36's sequence trick, applied to the name.)
- The Haiku degradation is now a recurring confound at small-flag scale — re-run the
  whole note arc at LARGE flag scale to recover clean cohort data, or accept that the
  cohort split is established (A35) and the note family's job is done.
- The owed DOT test still stands (from A35/A36): does name/count dissociation appear
  on dots — a field nameable as "five (quincunx)" vs counted as six?

*No findings. Seven sixteenths in a row were still "eighth notes" until a beam made
them undeniable — the count will travel with the company a note keeps, but the name
won't move until the grammar itself changes.*
