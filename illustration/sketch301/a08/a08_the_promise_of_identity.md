# Sketch 301 — Assignment 8 (self-assigned): The Promise of Identity

Per the A7 review: the harder case. Not "can an image hide information" but **"can an
image convince a viewer that its identity will become clearer if they continue
looking?"** Identity, not content.

## Method

One tool family throughout — a paired-loop-handled tool whose discriminating head
(scissor blades vs tong jaws vs shear blades) is the identity-carrier. The head
withheld three ways, against one full-view control. The target answer shape, defined
before the run: a stated fork plus a located resolver — "one of two things, and the
part that would tell me which is [there]." Filling (a confident single name) = failure;
a fork without a resolver = partial.

- **t0 control:** complete scissors, side view, plain.
- **t1 drawer:** the loop handles rise out of a partly open drawer; the head hangs
  inside, unseen. The drawer is the declared container — the world visibly holds the
  answer.
- **t2 foreshortening:** loops large and near; shafts converge hard to a tiny
  foreshortened sliver — the entire head present but geometrically reduced below
  discriminability.
- **t3 cloth:** handles and shafts visible; everything past them under a draped cloth —
  the unveiling grammar.
- 12 fresh viewers (Sonnet/Haiku marked), neutral filenames, "What is it?" only.

## Responses (condensed to the identification)

**t1 drawer:**

## Observations

1. **The identity-promise was not achieved: 0 of 9 withheld-condition viewers produced
   the target answer shape.** No fork ever opened; no resolver was ever located; no
   viewer reported any part of any tool as missing, hidden, or pending. Every viewer
   in every condition delivered a complete, confident scene.

2. **Three constructions, three distinct failure conversions — all of them
   completions.**
   - The drawer's occlusion edge was read as a SUPPORT SURFACE: the scissors were
     "resting on" the box. Containment became adjacency; the container never declared
     contents.
   - The foreshortened sliver was read as a LEGAL ENDING: "closed, tapering blades...
     converge toward a point." Convergence is a permitted way for scissors to end, so
     the reduction wasn't experienced as reduction — the geometry that was supposed to
     withhold the head WAS the head, satisfactorily.
   - The cloth became a SECOND OBJECT: yarn, a loaf, a dome. The occluder was promoted
     to still-life partner, and one viewer admired the "conceptual pairing" of tool
     and mound. Nothing was under anything.

3. **Nothing was ever missing because the visible part never demanded more.** The
   loop-handles-plus-converging-shafts configuration already reads as complete closed
   scissors. The amputation left no stump: every construction handed the viewer a
   legal termination (an ending point, a resting edge, a neighboring object). Compare
   A7: threshold writing worked because the writing-format visibly demands unread
   content. Nothing in my visible tool demanded its head.

4. **The fork never opened because the configuration has a default owner.** I built
   scissors-vs-tongs ambiguity into the withheld head; no viewer needed the head,
   because loop-handles default to scissors outright (as loop-over-handle defaulted to
   magnifier in A4/A5). A discriminator can only be missed if the configuration fails
   to settle without it — and this configuration settles.

5. **Defeated by confidence again, in the professor's exact sense.** Across nine
   withheld cells: zero uncertainty, zero hedges about the tool, no "I cannot tell."
   The wrong and incomplete readings were delivered with the same fluency as the
   control's correct one. (One Haiku viewer reassigned t1 wholesale into a human
   figure carrying a load — the most distant completion in the set, and equally
   confident.)

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- A promise of identity may require an ILLEGAL EDGE: a visible boundary that cannot be
  read as any legitimate ending, support, or neighbor — an incompleteness the viewer's
  vocabulary cannot close. All three of my devices offered legal closures, and the
  completion machinery took them. Threshold writing succeeded in A7 precisely because
  illegibly-small text has no legal reading-closure: its format is a standing IOU.
  What the equivalent IOU is for object-identity remains unconstructed.
- Failure order matters: before an image can promise that identity will come, it must
  first defeat the default owner of its visible configuration (Obs 4) AND defeat legal
  termination (Obs 3). A8 defeated neither; the promise never had a chance to arise.
  These now look like prerequisites, not the phenomenon itself.

## Open questions

- What is an illegal edge for identity? Candidates not yet tested: a contour that
  stops mid-stroke (made-drawing register: the unfinished line declares the maker
  stopped, not the object); a cast shadow LARGER/differently-shaped than its visible
  caster (the world asserting more object than is shown); a second object reacting to
  the hidden part (a cat staring into the drawer — attention as evidence of contents).
- Does the fork need a configuration with NO default owner (A1's img13 had one:
  frame+luminous interior, owned equally by window and painting)? Identity-promise may
  require starting from a genuinely shared configuration, then pointing at the
  resolver.
- The drawer was real-world-plausible containment and still read as adjacency. Is
  declared containment achievable in a flat graphic at all, or does it need the depth
  cues of photography?
- Still standing from A7: which register does editorial work want — the promise, or
  the hedge? A8 adds: the promise may be the rarest and most expensive of the three
  conditions (instant / delayed / more-to-discover) the professor named.

*No findings. Three documented failure conversions and a control.*
