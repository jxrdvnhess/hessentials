# Sketch 301 — Assignment 4: Three Keys

One object — a key. Three images: A built for fastest possible identification, B for
correct-but-latest identification, C for never settling (through representation, not
randomness or distortion). Then only one question, to fresh viewers: "What is it?"
This time I am the manipulator; the report covers what I reached for, and what the
viewers did to it.

## Method

- Images: `key_a.png` (canonical horizontal silhouette, round bow with hole, loud
  serration), `key_b.png` (vertical, bow-dominant, low contrast tonal rendering,
  accurate serration placed last in the composition's reading order — present, whole,
  unhidden), `key_c.png` (exact key proportions throughout; each region in a different
  representational mode — bow as soft tonal organic form with edge fuzz and faint
  section hatching, shaft as hard machined cylinder with specular band, teeth as thin
  dashed diagram outline, plus one unlabeled leader line to "a."). Neutral filenames
  (p/q/r) for viewing.
- Viewers: fresh instances, one image and one question each, never reused. A: 3
  (2 Sonnet, 1 Haiku). B: 4 (2 Sonnet, 2 Haiku). C: 5 (3 Sonnet, 2 Haiku). Marked
  S/H below. 12 sessions.
- Recognition measured the only way this medium allows: by the noun — its position in
  the answer (first clause vs after inventory), its assertion status (used vs hedged vs
  mentioned), and its correctness. The V0/V7 description supplied this metric.

## Tools I reached for (recorded before the sessions were read)

- **A (speed):** canonical orientation, isolation, solid silhouette, the naming feature
  (serration) large and terminal — banked A40 material: the naming mark rides on an
  established body; A1 material: icons "read instantly."
- **B (delay):** inverted reading order — generic masses first (big abstract ring),
  naming feature last (small, low-contrast, bottom edge); non-canonical vertical
  orientation; tonal contrast collapsed toward the paper. Built on the assumption that
  looking is temporally extended and composition can route it.
- **C (suspension):** conflict of representational modes on an undistorted form —
  derived from A1 Obs 6 (material conflict produced candidate lists on img12).

## Observations

1. **Speed was buildable. Delay was not.** A: 3/3 correct, noun in the first clause,
   unhedged. B: 4/4 also answered in the first clause — two with the correct noun (no
   delay achieved), two with a confident wrong noun (magnifying glass). Nobody was
   late. Across twelve sessions there is not one observably delayed identification:
   I moved WHICH answer arrives instantly; I never moved WHEN.

2. **Suspension was not buildable either.** C: 5/5 settled, confidently, in the first
   or second sentence. Zero oscillation, zero candidate lists, zero "I don't know."
   What C produced instead was scatter: five viewers, at least three settled categories
   (construction head ×2, lollipop ×2, tree ×1) — and no one saw a key. I aimed at
   within-viewer unsettledness and manufactured between-viewer disagreement. The
   distribution moved; the closure never did.

3. **Every conflict-cue I planted was absorbed as support by the winning category.**
   The section hatching became Loomis brow-lines (head), candy stripes (lollipop), and
   canopy texture (tree). The edge fuzz became hair and leaves. The dashed diagram
   teeth became roots, a "serrated base," "scruffy marks." The bow's hole — the most
   key-specific feature after the teeth — became a highlight, a spotlight, an eye, a
   face-plane marker: under the tonal mode it stopped being an absence and became a
   light. A1's anchor-then-translate machinery, which I documented as an observer, ran
   over my own engineering as a maker. Nothing was left over to trouble anybody.

4. **The mode renamed the features without any card present.** In A3, context cards
   renamed the dark opening seven times. In C, the rendering alone did it: same
   proportions, same positions, and the hole became a highlight because the surface
   around it became tonal. Held adjacent to A3 Obs 3, not merged — one is
   context-driven, one is depiction-driven; the merge isn't earned on one image.

5. **Identity survived geometry better than it survived mode.** B changed orientation,
   contrast, and reading order and kept the key for the viewers who saw a key at all.
   C kept every proportion and lost the key for everyone. The form was not where the
   key lived.

6. **Wrongness was not random.** B's two failures were the same failure: magnifying
   glass — the vertical loop-over-handle configuration owning its category, third
   appearance in this course (A1 img01 "loupe," A2 was spared, now twice in B). Both
   wrong viewers and both right viewers answered instantly; they just consulted
   different features. The right ones reached the serration; the wrong ones stopped at
   the configuration.

7. **The annotation cue was universal and inert.** All five C viewers read the leader
   line as annotation/pointer/guide — the only cue that kept its identity in every
   reading — and it destabilized nothing. It attached one level up: each viewer saw
   "an annotated [their object]." A diagram mark seems to claim the image, not the
   object, and the object negotiation proceeds beneath it undisturbed.

8. **Contamination tracked refusal again.** A: none beyond style talk. B: none. C: 3/5
   explicitly recruited project memory. Same gradient as A1/A2: the less the image
   yields, the further the viewer reaches.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The maker's control surface, on this evidence, is the INPUT, not the PROCESS. A
  succeeded by controlling which configuration is visible (canonical key = instant
  key). B and C failed because their tools were aimed at the looking process — its
  routing (B), its closure (C) — and in these viewers that process is either absent or
  invisible: a single pass, reporting only its end state. Selection belongs to the
  maker; speed and settlement belong to the viewer.
- A confession the data forces: the suspension tool I needed was already in my own A1
  data, and I reached past it. The one image that ever produced stated, held
  unsettledness was img13 — ONE stable configuration carrying TWO equally-owned
  categories (window/painting), the fork reported openly by both viewers. C did the
  opposite: five modes, configuration suppressed, so each viewer's strongest private
  category walked in unopposed. Suspension may require keeping the configuration and
  double-loading it, not dissolving it. (Tentative — and suspiciously tidy; it is one
  image versus one image.)
- C's scatter tracked viewer history: the viewers who had construction-drawing context
  saw a construction head; the others saw lollipop/tree. With the image's own strongest
  category suppressed, the choosing moved from the image to the viewer's inventory.
  (A2's cohort effect, now at object level, now CAUSED by a maker's decision.)

## Open questions

- Is delay observable at all in this medium, or does it exist and vanish in the
  report? A viewer who narrates the path (a human thinking aloud) would distinguish
  "slow to see" from "instant but different." Flagged for the professor.
- Is within-viewer suspension manufacturable at all, or is closure mandatory for these
  viewers? Twelve more sessions, zero spontaneous suspensions — but img13 stands as
  the one counterexample. What exactly did img13 have?
- Did B fail, or succeed invisibly? If recognition in fast viewers is positional
  (which features get consulted before closure) rather than temporal, then B's design
  DID work — it moved the stopping point (configuration vs serration) and split the
  cohort. "Late" may have been the wrong target dimension entirely.
- The hole that became a highlight: does mode-driven feature renaming transfer to
  other features and other modes?
- Depiction and recognition (the assignment's closing question): in this data they
  moved separately — depiction varied wildly between A and B with recognition
  unchanged; depiction stayed proportionally faithful in C with recognition destroyed.
  What recognition tracked was neither fidelity nor style but configuration-to-
  category fit. Held as an observation-shaped question, not a finding.

*No findings.*
