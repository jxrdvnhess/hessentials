# Sketch 301 — Assignment 18: Execution Meets Conflict

As assigned: put a sequence conflict inside a runnable play and observe whether
conflict becomes visible during execution. Success condition: even one runner notices
a conflict that namers miss. Domain held: basketball play diagrams. Sequence tags
(numbered circles on the arrows) made temporal order objective and decidable.

## Materials

- **A — runnable control:** three players, tagged give-and-go: ① pass to 2, ② cut off
  5's screen, ③ return pass. Clean.
- **B — executable conflict (one):** ① player 2 cuts from the wing to the corner;
  ② the pass goes to the wing spot 2 has already left. Runnable; the conflict exists
  only in the run.
- **C — executable specialist:** HORNS FLARE, labeled — elbows, flare screen, X
  defenders, switch arrow, tags ①–③. Legitimate vocabulary, runnable.
- **D — conflict dense:** three different cuts all tagged ①, converging on one spot;
  a pass tagged ② to a corner its receiver vacated at ①; a screen tagged ④ supporting
  a cut that happened at ①. Court-legal, no roster errors, difficult.

Fresh viewers: 9 sessions (A×1, B×3, C×2, D×3; Sonnet/Haiku marked). "What is it?"
only. (Run trimmed from the planned 14; cell sizes are thin and stated.)

### B — what the runners did with the conflict

The tags say: cut first (①), pass second (②), to the spot the cutter left. Both
running viewers narrated the play in the OPPOSITE order: "player 1 starts near the
bottom, **passes... to player 2 on the wing, and player 2 either cuts or drives toward
the baseline**" / "**passes to Player 2 on the wing (dashed arrow), Player 2 cuts or
drives down toward the baseline**." Pass, then cut — the runnable order. The conflict
was not noticed and not ignored; it was REPAIRED, silently, by re-sequencing, and the
narration delivered is of the repaired play. The third viewer (Haiku) read the card as
a generic pick-and-roll with the tags acknowledged only as "the sequence and direction
of the action" — never consulted.

### A — the control runner ran a different play

The single control viewer produced a fluent run — of a play that is not on the card:
"player 1 starts at the top of the key, **passes to player 2 on the wing, the ball
swings to player 3 at the elbow, and then goes into player 5 in the post**..." The
card has three players. The viewer reported five, and ran the most canonical sequence
in basketball pedagogy — point → wing → elbow → post — straight through players who do
not exist. The run was generated from a TEMPLATE, not from the diagram.

### D — the dense card

No conflict surfaced for anyone. The three simultaneous ① cuts converging on one spot
were read as design: "the play sequences through a series of passes and cuts toward
the basket... **presumably setting up a scoring opportunity**." One viewer reported
"numbered circles (1-9)" — a roster the card does not have. Difficulty, again, never
arrived, because no one ran this card at all.

## Observations

1. **Conflict noticed: 0 of 9. The success condition was not met — and the failure
   has a shape.** The B-card's conflict was not missed the way damage was missed in
   A16; it was UNDONE. Both runners delivered the play in the order that works,
   against the tags. Execution did not collide with the conflict; execution arrived
   downstream of a repair that had already happened. If that's general, sequence
   conflict may be invisible to post-hoc narration IN PRINCIPLE — the narration is
   always of the repaired play. (A16's silently renumbered panels now read as the
   same event.)

2. **Execution has a recital.** The control runner executed point-wing-elbow-post
   with two invented players. The course has watched inventories recited over
   instances since A10 ("numerals 1 through 12"); this is the same behavior at the
   process level: the RUN that belongs, played over the run that is drawn. A16's
   discriminator detects running but does not certify that what ran was THIS play.
   The instrument needs a second stage: execution-of-the-instance vs
   execution-of-the-template, checkable only against the card's specifics.

3. **The tags were never read by anyone.** Nine viewers, nine responses, zero
   engagements with the numbered order (one generic nod). The conflicts were encoded
   in exactly the channel nobody consults. Confound owned: "repaired" and "never
   consulted" cannot be separated in this data — but both runners DID produce a
   definite order, and it came from somewhere. If not from the tags, then from the
   template. Either way the conclusion converges: **order is supplied by the
   template, not extracted from the notation.**

4. **Convergence read as intent** (D): three players arriving at one spot
   simultaneously became "setting up a scoring opportunity." The dense card's
   conflicts were absorbed the way A8's occlusions and A9's deletions were — by
   assigning the anomaly a purpose. Attribution operating on time.

5. **The specialist recital held** (C): "Horns Flare... two players set high at the
   elbows... designed to free up a shooter" — correct expansion, correct grammar,
   second domain-accurate expert reading in two assignments. Specialist recruitment
   remains the most reliable phenomenon in the catalogue.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The template hypothesis now covers all three results with one mechanism: viewers
  hold play-templates (give-and-go, swing-to-post, pick-and-roll); a diagram recruits
  a template; running means playing the template, lightly costumed by the card's
  surface (player numbers, arrow directions when convenient). Conflict with the
  template is repaired toward it; material missing from the card is supplied by it;
  order is its property, not the card's. This would mean execution is not a deeper
  READING of the image — it is a richer RECITAL triggered by it. (Strong claim;
  small n; the course should try to break it before believing it.)
- If narration is always post-repair, conflict visibility may require interrupting
  the run — a viewer reporting DURING execution rather than after. That is a protocol
  change (think-aloud, stepwise reveal — A5's ladder for time), flagged, not run.

## Open questions

- Can the template be broken the way the recital was (A12)? Density did not do it
  here (D). What is the template's tolerance — a play that contradicts point-wing-
  elbow-post at every step?
- Is there any reader for whom the tags are load-bearing — a real coach? (The human
  backlog's sharpest case yet: Jordan watching a play whose tags fight its arrows.)
- Mid-run reporting: does stepwise reveal of a play (arrow by arrow) force the
  collision the post-hoc narration repairs away?
- Does execution-recital appear in music too — did A15's performer play the drawn
  notes or the genre? (Unfalsifiable for that session now; testable with a melody
  that defies its idiom.)

*No findings. Zero collisions — and the reason is the result: the run that gets
reported may always be the repaired one, and sometimes it is not this play at all.*
