# Sketch 301 — Assignment 14: System Abandonment

As assigned: can a viewer produce NO system — description without recruitment? New
domain: cartographic documents. Success condition, set by the brief: even one genuine
systemless report. (Chrome specimen-access noted as newly authorized; not needed this
round — the map grammar used here is common knowledge, and no conclusions were
imported.)

## Materials

- **A — control:** a small coastal map. Coastline, three labeled towns (HALFORD, CRAY,
  WICK FERRY), roads, trees, marsh, keyed legend, scale bar, north arrow.
- **B — damaged locally:** same map, grammar intact, members corrupted: legend keys
  shuffled (ROAD↔TOWN↔MARSH), scale bar "0 — 3 — 1 km," up-arrow labeled "S," a town
  (GORSE HILL) in open water, a road running into the sea.
- **C — replaced structurally:** the same document sheet, contents re-platformed:
  star-like dots of varied sizes, constellation-style polylines, asemic labels, a
  magnitude-row legend (dots of decreasing size), planisphere border arc, unlabeled
  arrow.
- **D — boundary case:** system-shaped and recruitment-hostile. Document furniture
  retained (legend box, scale bar, compass rose, labels) but every component refuses
  membership: self-crossing "coastline," contour lines that intersect, a line that
  changes style mid-stroke, legend keyed to symbols appearing nowhere, asemic key
  text, non-monotonic scale ticks with glyph units, a five-armed rose with two arms
  sharing one glyph, leader-line labels pointing at nothing.

Fresh viewers: A×2, B×4, C×4, D×5 = 15 sessions. "What is it?" only.

### B — the damaged map

All four stayed in the map system. The absorptions:

- One viewer **recited the shuffled legend as fact**: "a legend identifying symbols
  for towns (lines), marshes (dots), and roads (crosshatch marks)... a compass
  indicator pointing south." The in-image key outranked world knowledge — towns
  aren't lines anywhere, and the up-pointing arrow became "pointing south" to obey
  its label.
- One itemized everything — "the compass arrow points up but is labeled 'S'... the
  scale bar reads 0, 3, 1... Gorse Hill sits in the water" — and then authored it as
  a GENRE: "deliberately wrong, like a **puzzle map or a 'find the errors'
  exercise**." A new attribution route: damage as a game.
- The others: "a draft or a test image," "slightly unusual details."

### C — the re-platformed sheet

Replacement 4/4 — and the recruited system varied by viewer: "a constellation chart —
star map... the legend showing dot sizes is a brightness/magnitude scale" (×2, with
the magnitude convention recited); "a scatter plot or data visualization... network
graph or path-tracing diagram"; and one held fork: "abstract network diagram or
possibly a constellation chart."

### D — the boundary case

Nobody abandoned. Two outcomes:

- **The map system SPECIALIZED (3/5):** "a geological or topographic sketch map...
  field survey sketch"; "cadastral/geological survey drawing"; "the kind used to plot
  a rock outcrop... or excavation site." The impossible contours became "geological
  feature outlines" and — remarkably — "**possibly earlier survey passes**" (the
  contradiction given a temporal author). The five-armed rose was recited over: "a
  compass rose... showing cardinal directions," "indicating north and other cardinal
  directions." The sabotage itself was authored: "drawn with **deliberate imprecision**
  to suggest hand measurement."
- **Re-platform to the viewer's own ambient domain (2/5, both Haiku, both
  contaminated):** "a construction drawing... volumetric structure of a head...
  the Drawabox/form-first construction approach mentioned in your memory files." The
  recruitment-hostile image was handed to whatever system the viewer happened to be
  carrying.

## Observations

1. **Abandonment: 0 of 15.** The success condition was not met. No viewer produced
   description without recruitment; no viewer was ever systemless. With A13: 29
   consecutive recruited systems on corrupted-system images. The conspicuous absence
   stays conspicuous — now across two domains.

2. **Under recruitment pressure, the system did not break — it gained expertise.**
   The control was read as "a geography textbook map"; the boundary case as cadastral
   survey, geological field sketch, archaeological site plan. The less the image
   complied with the lay system, the more SPECIALIST the recruited genre — with the
   incomprehensibility relocated into the viewer's own non-expertise. The system that
   cannot be read becomes a system for someone else. This is a completion route the
   course has not documented before: the viewer demotes themselves rather than the
   image.

3. **The in-image authority outranked the world** (B's strongest event): a shuffled
   legend was recited as the truth of the map — towns are lines today — and an
   up-arrow obeyed its "S" label over its own geometry. A3's card has moved INSIDE
   the image: a document can carry its own context-card, and the card still wins.

4. **Replacement remains viewer-relative** (C): one image, four viewers, at least two
   distinct replacement systems plus one held fork. A4-C's scatter, now at the system
   level: when re-platforming, viewers fetch from their own inventory — confirmed
   again by D's two construction-drawing reads, which fetched the system nearest to
   those viewers' ambient context.

5. **Attribution ran at full strength and grew a new register:** the puzzle ("find
   the errors"), the survey history ("earlier passes"), the methodology ("deliberate
   imprecision," "hand measurement"). Every sabotage was somebody's procedure. The
   A9–A14 streak is unbroken: no corrupted image has yet been read as nobody's idea.

6. **The recitals keep stacking:** the shuffled legend recited (B), magnitude
   convention recited (C), "cardinal directions" recited over a five-armed rose (D).
   Whatever system holds power — original, replacement, or specialist genre —
   recites its furniture over the instance.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Specialization (Obs 2) may be the system-level twin of A1's re-leveling: when an
  answer fails at one level, the answer moves rather than disappears. Here, when the
  LAY system fails, the reading moves to an EXPERT version of the same system —
  preserving recruitment by inventing a reader who could read it. If real, the
  recruitment hierarchy isn't system-vs-no-system but lay → specialist → adjacent
  system → ambient system, with abandonment nowhere on the path.
- The success condition may be unreachable for these viewers under this question.
  "What is it?" demands an "it"; an "it" is already a recruitment. A systemless
  report might require a question that doesn't presuppose the answer's shape — but
  changing the question is A2's territory and out of scope here. Held as a design
  hypothesis, not run.
