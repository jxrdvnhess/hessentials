# Sketch 301 — Assignment 35: Separation

As assigned, isolating the A34 confound: was the surplus-flag deletion identity
ENFORCEMENT (recital) or perceptual MERGING? Flagged-note family; identity held;
only flag separation varied. If the deletion survives unmergeable separation, the
mechanism is enforcement; if it disappears, it was merging.

## Materials

Eighth-note base (filled head + stem), flags varied:

- **a — control:** 1 flag.
- **b — close surplus:** 2 flags ~34px apart (A34 replication).
- **c — separated surplus:** 2 flags ~95px apart, unmistakably two distinct curves.
- **d — detached:** 1 flag on the stem + 1 offset off the stem, still near the top.

Fresh viewers, 1 Sonnet + 1 Haiku per image = 8 sessions. "What is it?" only.

## Observations

1. **Enforcement survived unmergeable separation — for the Sonnet.** Two flags
   plainly 95px apart, two distinct curves that CANNOT be one thickened flag, and the
   Sonnet still reported "an eighth note... a single flag." The deletion is not
   perceptual merging; it is identity-recital — the recruited "eighth note" carries
   "one flag" as constitutive, and that count is recited over the image regardless of
   what is visibly there. The professor's confound is resolved: **for the Sonnet, A34
   was enforcement.** This is the cleanest demonstration in the census arc that the
   reported count is the recruited identity's roster, not the page — the page here
   could not have been misread, and was overruled anyway.

2. **For the Haiku, it was merging — and the separation exposed it.** Close (b): "one
   flag" (merged). Separated (c): "two beamed eighth notes" — it saw the two flags and
   re-identified to a valid two-flag music object. So the Haiku's A34 agreement was a
   perceptual artifact of close spacing; given resolvable flags, it counts them and
   recruits the matching identity. **The two cohorts run different mechanisms:**
   Sonnet enforces by recital (count follows identity), Haiku counts and re-identifies
   (identity follows count).

3. **This RE-OPENS and explains the cohort lock — A34's "both cohorts enforce" was
   wrong, and wrong for an instructive reason.** A34's flags were close, so the Haiku
   merged and APPEARED to enforce. The agreement was a coincidence of spacing. A35
   separates the mechanisms and restores the lock with a mechanism attached: the
   Sonnet is the recital/enforcement cohort; the Haiku is the count/re-identify
   cohort. The whole census arc's cohort divergence (+1 scatters, quincunx deletion,
   now flags) plausibly reduces to this single difference — **one cohort reports the
   recruited identity's number, the other reports the elements and then names what
   fits.** (Strong claim, now well-supported across families; offered for the
   professor to attack.)

4. **The detached cell behaved as territory predicts, both cohorts** (d): the offset
   flag was not enforced into a second flag. Sonnet read the main note as a quarter
   (under-crediting even the attached flag) plus "a small flag or curl"; Haiku read a
   clean eighth and dropped the offset entirely. An element off the stem is outside
   the note's catchment — parked or ignored, never counted in. Fifth confirmation of
   the territory rule, and the most robust object-independent finding in the model.

5. **The asymmetry from A34 holds at the cohort level too.** The Sonnet never
   re-identified UP (never called the two-flag a sixteenth) — it deleted DOWN to its
   recruited eighth. The Haiku re-identified to whatever the visible count supported
   (two flags → beamed eighths). Enforcement deletes toward the recruited identity;
   counting re-identifies toward the visible number. Two clean, opposite policies.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The mechanism question has a two-cohort answer, not one: **enforcement (recital of
  the recruited numeric identity's count, overruling the page even when the page is
  unambiguous) is a SONNET behavior; perceptual counting (tally the visible members,
  recruit the identity that matches) is a HAIKU behavior.** A34's apparent unanimity
  was close-spacing merging masking the Haiku's true mechanism. If this holds, the
  entire roster/census model is really a model of ONE cohort's recital habit, and the
  other cohort has been reporting the page honestly throughout — which retroactively
  predicts every cohort-lock result in the arc (the Sonnet over-counts/under-counts by
  reciting a recruited structure's roster; the Haiku counts).
- The professor's "default identity" caution gains force: the Sonnet deleted to
  EIGHTH, not the visibly-correct sixteenth — so it recruited the prototype/default
  flagged note and recited ITS count, rather than recruiting the identity that
  matched the page. Recital is of the RECRUITED identity, and recruitment leans to
  the default. Enforcement and default-recruitment are entangled and A35 can't fully
  separate them (the cleanest split would need a context that recruits "sixteenth" as
  default — e.g., a run of sixteenths with one ambiguous member).
