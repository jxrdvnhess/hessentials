# Sketch 301 — Assignment 19: Template Versus Instance

As assigned: separate execution-of-the-template from execution-of-the-instance. The
target is the SOURCE of execution. Domain held: basketball. The violations live in
arrow geometry — the channel A18 showed viewers actually consult — not in tags.

## Materials

- **A — canonical template:** a textbook pick-and-roll: 5 screens, 1 drives off it,
  5 rolls to the rim, pocket pass to the roller. Two players, clean.
- **B — template violation (one step):** identical setup; after screening, 5 departs
  AWAY from the basket to the deep corner, and the pass follows the diagram out
  there. Legal, runnable, anti-template at the critical step.
- **C — strong violation:** the screen is refused (1 drives away from it), 5 walks
  outward to the far wing, and the final pass goes BACKWARD to a trailing 2. Legal.
- **D — instance only:** no recognizable pattern: 1 zigzag-dribbles up from the deep
  corner, 4 drifts to the short corner, the pass goes corner-slot to short-corner,
  3 loops out to the wing. No screens, no canonical spots. Coherent, unfamiliar.

Fresh viewers: 7 sessions (A×2, B×2, C×1, D×2; Sonnet/Haiku marked). Cells are thin
and stated as such — this run reads as a third consecutive probe of one domain, with
~26 play-card sessions now accumulated across A17–A19. "What is it?" only.

## Observations

1. **Instance evidence: zero occurrences in seven sessions.** Not one viewer
   correctly narrated an anti-template detail (the outward roll, the refused screen,
   the backward pass) or any distinctive specific of the pattern-free card. The
   diagram's specifics were never the source of anything anyone said. Whatever
   execution and description drew on, it was not the instance.

2. **The gradient is the result.** As template fit decreases, the response does not
   shift toward instance-reading — it degrades:
   - **Canonical (A):** template-runs — including an IMPORTED WRONG template: "player
     1 passes to player 5" on a card where no such pass exists; the give-and-go
     schema overwrote the pick-and-roll geometry.
   - **Violation (B, C):** fog. No template name, no violation report — instead
     coordinate-talk with garbled directions (a pass arrow read backwards), hedged
     disjunctions, and in one case loss of the sport ("basketball or soccer").
   - **Pattern-free (D):** the template imposed by force — "pick-and-roll or
     drive-and-kick" named on a screenless card, with a handoff invented and a cut
     re-aimed "toward the basket" that travels upcourt.
   When the template fits, it runs; when it half-fits, narration stammers; when it
   doesn't fit, it is installed anyway. At no point does the instance take over.

3. **Violations surfaced as HEDGES, not as reports.** The first sustained hedge
   syntax in three play-assignments ("screen or cutting," "receiving or initiating,"
   "passes or moves") appeared exactly on the cards where template grip weakens.
   Template mismatch was never noticed as content; it leaked out as grammar. (A1
   Obs 3 — hesitation has a syntax — resurfacing as a mismatch detector more
   sensitive than the viewers' own reports.)

4. **Template names are badges, not analyses.** A17's viewers named "give-and-go"
   on a give-and-go; this run's viewers failed to name "pick-and-roll" on a textbook
   pick-and-roll but DID name it on a screenless pattern-free card. The name attaches
   to genre-feel, not to verified structure — consistent with every recital since
   "numerals 1 through 12."

5. **Repair continued at the member level:** arrows reversed into runnable
   directions, a dashed pass re-typed as a dribble, a backward pass smoothed into
   wing motion. No viewer reported anything wrong on any card. The
   anomaly-never-reported streak in this domain is now 26 sessions.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The A18 template hypothesis survived its first designed attack, in a modified
  form. The prediction "diagram vs template forces a choice" was wrong in an
  informative way: there was no choice, because the instance was never a candidate.
  The amended candidate: viewers narrate FROM the template when it grips and produce
  degraded surface-description when it doesn't — instance-faithful reading is not on
  the menu at all, for these viewers, under this question. (The professor's warning
  stands: ignored tags, unreadable geometry, and template priority remain
  unseparated. B/C's garbled arrow-directions suggest the instance channel itself is
  low-fidelity — the template may win partly because the competition is noise.)
- The hedge-leak (Obs 3) offers a usable instrument: template mismatch may be
  measurable by disjunction-density in the response, without any viewer ever
  reporting a violation. If it replicates, the course gains a second indirect
  detector (after A16's output test) — reading the grammar instead of the claims.

## Open questions

- Is the instance channel weak or unused? A card whose ONLY content is one arrow
  (no template available, no complexity) would measure raw arrow-reading fidelity —
  the missing baseline under all three play-assignments.
- Does hedge-density track violation-strength quantitatively? (A: 2 disjunctions;
  B/C: garble; D: fluent. Order suggestive, n tiny.)
- Would a viewer with REAL basketball knowledge narrate B's outward roll as the
  story it is ("short roll refused — that's a pop, but to the CORNER?") — i.e., is
  instance-reading an expert behavior? Sharpest human-backlog case yet, third
  assignment running.
- Does the badge-not-analysis behavior of template names hold outside sport (was
  "Horns Flare" in A18 verified or badged)?

*No findings. Zero instance-evidence in seven sessions, a gradient that runs
template → fog → forced template, and a hedge-leak that may be worth more than the
reports it leaks from.*
