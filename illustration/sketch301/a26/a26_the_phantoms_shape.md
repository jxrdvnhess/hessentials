# Sketch 301 — Assignment 26: The Phantom's Shape

As assigned: which organizational reading creates the phantom? Five elements always;
four organizations: center+satellites, chain, two groups (2+3), and a scatter built
to be as hard to claim as possible. Success condition: different relational
structures produce different census behavior.

## Materials

- **a — radial:** one large central blob, four satellites at varied angles and
  distances.
- **b — chain:** a curving directional progression, lower-left to upper-right, sizes
  deliberately non-monotonic (30/38/34/40/32).
- **c — two groups:** 2 upper-left, 3 lower-right, clear separation.
- **d — ambiguous:** near-uniform sizes, no center dominance, no alignments, no
  pairs.

Fresh viewers, 1 Sonnet + 1 Haiku per image = 8 sessions. "What is it?" only.

## Observations

1. **The phantom changed sign — the success condition is met.** The 2+3 grouping
   produced the course's first richness-driven UNDER-count: the Sonnet read "two
   clusters, each a larger circle paired with a smaller one" — two pairs, four
   members — deleting the fifth blob to satisfy the pair-structure. Census error is
   not a fixed +1; it took the value the claimed structure required: pairs → −1,
   the loose orbit of A21/A25 → +1, A24's 4+4 pattern → −1 from nine. **Error
   follows the arithmetic of the claim.**

2. **The clean radial counted itself — 2/2 correct.** "One large + four small" is a
   pre-counted structure, like the rows and the 4+1 outlier. This separates two
   things A25 had fused: A25's over-counting scatters were read with a LOOSE radial
   ("largest near center, others around it") imposed on a field that only half
   supports it; A26's radial genuinely IS 1+4, and enumerated perfectly. The
   phantom does not come from radial reading — it comes from a radial reading that
   doesn't quite fit.

3. **The hardest-to-claim scatter counted correctly — 2/2.** Near-uniform sizes, no
   center: the Sonnet explicitly declined structure ("no obvious grid or pattern")
   and counted five. This is the decisive cell against the A25 three-regime map as
   drawn: the "estimate regime" may not exist. No claim, no phantom. The census
   errors live at the BOUNDARY — where a structure is half-imposed on a field that
   underdetermines it — not in the genuinely unclaimed.

4. **The chain protected its count and taxed its members instead.** Both cohorts
   counted five; both recited a false size-gradient over deliberately non-monotonic
   sizes ("each slightly larger than the one before," "progress from smaller to
   larger" — plus "evenly spaced and perfectly round," neither true). Third
   instance of order-quantization, and the first to take both cohorts. Where the
   claim fits the count but not the properties, the properties pay. Something is
   always smoothed; the claim chooses what.

5. **The cohort lock held and sharpened.** Haiku: 8/8 lifetime correct counts,
   including this assignment's four. Every census error in the course — the four
   +1s, A24's two −1s (both cohorts, the one exception, under an overwhelming
   pattern), and today's −1 — comes from structure-constructing readings. The
   genre-filing cohort, which builds no member-level structure, has never
   miscounted. The richer reading keeps paying; today it paid in the other
   direction.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- One mechanism now covers every census event on record: **the reported count is
  the membership of the claimed structure, not the tally of the elements.** Clean
  fit (rows, 1+4, 4+1, even the uniform scatter's null-claim): structure membership
  equals element count, census "correct." Misfit claim: membership diverges in the
  direction of the structure's needs — an orbit short one satellite invents it
  (+1); a pair-scheme with a leftover deletes it (−1); a near-symmetric ring rounds
  to its symmetry (9→8). The viewer reports the structure's roster. This subsumes
  A25's three regimes into two: claimed-and-fitting, claimed-and-misfitting — with
  "unclaimed" collapsing into the first (a null claim fits trivially).
- The chain shows the same mechanism operating on properties when the count slot is
  already satisfied: the sequence-claim's roster matched, so its GRADIENT got
  enforced instead. The claim always collects its dues; count is just the most
  countable currency.

## Open questions

- The −1 event is n=1. Does the pair-deletion replicate — and does 2+2+1 at seven
  elements delete to "three pairs" (6)?
- Can a misfit be DOSED? A radial field at increasing center-offset: does the +1
  appear exactly when the misfit does?
- The chain's both-cohort gradient recital vs the cohort-locked census errors:
  property-smoothing apparently has no cohort lock. Why does the census discriminate
  cohorts and the gradient not?
- The structure's-roster account predicts the located-phantom experiment's outcome
  (the viewer should point to the structure's slots, not the page's blobs) — still
  blocked on the protocol question, which remains the professor's call.

*No findings. The phantom is not a number — it is the signed difference between the
page's tally and the claimed structure's roster, and today it went negative on
schedule.*
