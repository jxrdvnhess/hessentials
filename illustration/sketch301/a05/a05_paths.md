# Sketch 301 — Assignment 5 (self-assigned): Paths

Per the A4 recommendation: stop measuring destinations, observe recognition in motion.
Aimed at the preserved question — **did B fail, or succeed invisibly?**

## Method (and its honesty)

A blur ladder stretches the input over steps so a path becomes observable. Owned up
front: this MAKES a path rather than proving an internal one exists in a single
viewing. What it earns is narrower — how identification behaves as information arrives
in stages, and what a viewer's own history does to that.

- Stimuli: `key_a` (A4's instant key) and `key_b` (A4's delayed key), each at six
  Gaussian blur levels (28, 18, 12, 7, 3, 0 px on 640 px), neutral filenames m1–m6 /
  n1–n6. Contact sheet: `_ladder.png`.
- **Profile** (per image): six independent fresh Sonnet viewers, one per level, each
  seeing only its own level, asked "What is it?" Shows what each information level
  supports, with no history anywhere.
- **Path** (per image): one transcript-continuity viewer (the A3 method) walked up the
  same ladder, told only: "the same image six times, each time with less blur." Its
  prior answers replayed verbatim. Shows what history does.
- The announcement ("you will see it six times") is itself an intervention. Its effect
  turned out to be one of the results (Obs 4). One thread per image — single-run
  caveats throughout.

## Observations

1. **The two images carry their identity at different depths.** key_a's identity is in
   its configuration — blur-proof, present at 28px. key_b's identity is in its
   serration — absent until ~3px; at every shallower depth the configuration actively
   supports OTHER categories (magnifier, mirror, Q). A4-B did not weaken the key; it
   relocated the key deeper into the image.

2. **On B's preserved question:** the A4 "magnifying glass" viewers gave, at full
   resolution, exactly the verdict this ladder shows belongs to LOW information
   (n1–n2). The serration, when consulted, flips it (n5). Read against this ladder, B
   built a path with a late naming feature, and the A4 cohort split by how far along
   that path each viewer stopped. "Late" exists — as a position in information-space,
   not in time. (Observation-shaped; the inference about A4 viewers' stopping depth is
   explanation-grade and held there.)

3. **Path ≠ profile.** At n2, the fresh viewer said hand mirror; the path viewer,
   carrying its own step-1 candidate list, said "leaning toward a key" — and argued
   from evidence against the magnifier. The path viewer reached the right answer three
   levels before fresh viewers did, and never visited the profile's intermediate
   categories (mirror, Q, ankh). History accelerated here. Held directly against A3,
   where history blinded: same mechanism, opposite valence.

4. **The announcement licensed suspension.** Both path viewers, at step 1, produced
   hedged candidate lists — "impossible to be more specific than that" — while fresh
   viewers at the SAME blur level committed confidently (m1 "clearly key-shaped," n1
   "a magnifying glass"). These are the first spontaneous suspensions in five
   assignments, and they were produced not by any image but by the promise of more
   viewings. A4's correction lands precisely: suspension wasn't shown unbuildable —
   it was unbuilt IN IMAGES by my tools. It appeared immediately when the protocol
   made the viewing non-final.

5. **Confidence was not monotonic in information** (n-profile): high at the most
   degraded levels (wrong), collapsing to a candidate list mid-ladder, high again at
   the end (right). The most confident wrong answers occurred where information was
   scarcest; uncertainty appeared only where two category-verdicts changed hands.

6. **Settling was one-way.** In both paths, once the category settled (m at step 2,
   n at step 4), no later step re-opened it; everything after was detail refinement.
   No revision-after-settling occurred anywhere in this run.

7. **The path viewer rewrote its own history.** Final step, n-path: "Every prior
   viewing was correct." Viewing 1 was a four-way tie; viewing 2 was a lean. The
   record was smoothed toward the conclusion — the viewer's epistemic history edited
   the same way A9/A10 found memory edits toward the residue. Invention in the same
   voice as recall, now applied to one's own past answers.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- What "recognition in motion" looks like under this method: a cheap, blur-proof
  configuration verdict first; expensive, blur-fragile detail verdicts later; the
  naming detail can overrule the configuration only if it gets consulted. A maker can
  choose the depth at which identity sits (A's surface vs B's depth); a viewer chooses
  — or has chosen for them — how deep to go. The A4 split would then be a split in
  stopping depth, not in seeing. One object; not promoted.
- Closure looks like a policy, not a capacity. Told the viewing is one of six, the
  viewer holds candidates; believing the viewing final, it closes. If that holds, every
  "nobody ever says I don't know" result since A1 is a fact about assumed-final
  viewings, not about viewers.
- Why history accelerated here and blinded in A3: these priors were the viewer's own,
  held as provisional hypotheses; A3's were external, authoritative, and serially
  victorious. Ownership and modality of the prior may decide whether history is a lens
  or a cage. (Speculative; flagged as such.)
