### B — the damaged sheet

All three viewers read "a 4/4 time signature." The signature reads 4/7. The
doubled-stem note and the half-height barline were never mentioned by anyone. Three
corruptions, zero reports — and the register moved DOWN: "a beginner's sheet music
book," "a teaching context," "basic musical phrase." The damaged sheet read as more
elementary, not less correct.

### C — the genuine specialist sheet

All three moved register UP. The full version: "written for a low-register
instrument... in 7/8 time... *pp*... a 5:4 tuplet... 8va... fermata... a brief passage
from a **contemporary or 20th-century classical score**... for a cello, bassoon, or
trombone." One viewer garbled the members (3/4, "sfrz," treble) and STILL placed it in
the specialist register: "a study or exercise... hand-annotated working quality." And
one viewer, unprompted, PERFORMED it: "it starts quietly, accelerates through a burst
of fast notes in a high register, then lands on a held, accented note" — the recruited
system didn't just name the image; it ran it.

### D — the boundary sheet

No nonsense verdicts, no lay stop, no abandonment. All three handed it to a specialist
system, two of them to the same one: "**drum notation**... x-shaped noteheads
(indicating cymbal hits)... hi-hat hits... a bass drum accent (the solid black
wedge)... a cross-stick or rim shot... a personal transcription or study exercise" /
"percussion sheet music... rhythm syllables or counting marks" / "an instructional
music book... a technical study of how to play specific passages." The cluster wedge
became a bass drum accent; the stacked seconds became a rim shot — expert member-facts,
fluent and false, asserted inside the recruited subsystem.

## Observations

1. **Specialization replicated in a second domain. The success condition is met.**
   C: 3/3 escalated register within the system (contemporary classical, instrument
   speculation). D: 3/3 assigned a specialist subsystem or expert genre. Eleven
   sessions, eleven recruitments, zero abandonments — and the two non-control,
   non-damaged conditions both moved lay → specialist, as the brief targeted.

2. **Specialization came in two distinguishable moves.** REGISTER ESCALATION (C): the
   same system read at higher expertise — genre, era, instrument, performer inferred.
   SUBSYSTEM ASSIGNMENT (D): the marks handed to a sibling specialist system that
   genuinely OWNS some of them — x-noteheads really are percussion convention, so the
   viewers found a real expert system whose vocabulary claims the strange marks, then
   let it claim all of them (wedge→bass drum). The map assignment's "someone else can
   read this" has a more precise shape here: "THIS specialist can read this."

3. **The recital normalized the impossible time signature, three for three.** "4/4"
   over 4/7 — and the other two corruptions invisible with it. Music's recital
   tolerated three mixed-type corruptions where A12's clock recital broke at three
   same-type substitutions. Tolerance is not a constant; it varies by domain, by
   corruption type, or by the viewer's member-level knowledge — undetermined which.
   (Kept comparative and observation-grade.)

4. **Damage moved the register down; expertise moved it up.** Every response located
   the image's intended USER: beginner, student, teacher, percussionist, chamber
   musician, transcriber. Audience-assignment looks like a mandatory output of the
   reading, running in both directions — A14's self-demotion ("for experts") is one
   end of a scale whose other end is "for beginners," and corrupted-but-recitable
   images slid DOWN it.

5. **The recruited system executed the image** (C, strongest single event): a viewer
   converted the notation into a performance in time — dynamics, acceleration,
   arrival — under "What is it?" alone. Recruitment didn't stop at naming or even at
   member-recital; the system ran the instance like a program.

6. **Expert recitals carry expert errors** (A13's replacement-recital, now inside
   subsystems): the bass-drum wedge and rim-shot cluster are confident member-facts
   the percussion system supplied about marks it doesn't actually own. The deeper the
   recruited expertise, the more authoritative the invented detail sounds.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The two specialization moves may be one mechanism — recruit the system whose
  vocabulary claims the most marks — with two outcomes depending on whether such a
  system actually exists nearby (percussion for x-heads) or must be projected
  (geological surveying for impossible contours). If real, "apparent expertise" is
  readable exactly to the extent that some genuine expertise shares its vocabulary.
- Audience-assignment (Obs 4) may be the general form of A14's self-demotion: every
  reading files the image on an expertise ladder relative to the viewer, and
  unreadability is resolved by moving the image up the ladder rather than by
  reporting failure. Down-ladder movement (the damaged sheet as "beginner material")
  would then be the same mechanism with the recital doing the smoothing first.

## Open questions

- The tolerance contrast (Obs 3): same count, different fate across domains. Is the
  variable corruption TYPE (same-member substitutions vs mixed), inventory size, or
  viewer member-knowledge? A matched ladder would discriminate; not run.
- Does the execution behavior (Obs 5) appear in other runnable systems — circuit
  read as current-flow, recipe read as cooking sequence? Naming vs running may be a
  real division in what recruitment delivers.
- D's false expert member-facts: when apparent expertise meets a viewer with REAL
  expertise (a percussionist), does the wedge-as-bass-drum read collapse — or does
  expertise deepen the confabulation? Human specialist viewer would answer this; the
  human backlog grows.
- Register-down under damage: does lay-ification replicate, or was the damaged sheet
  simply simpler-looking? (Confound owned: B's corruptions don't add visual density;
  C and D do.)
