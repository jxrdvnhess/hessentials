# Sketch 301 — Assignment 1: What Is It?

Twenty images built to invite classification while resisting stable classification.
For each, two naive viewers — fresh instances with no conversation context, no knowledge
of the generator, and no contact with each other — were shown the image file and asked
exactly one question: **"What is it?"** Nothing was defended, explained, or guided.
Responses recorded verbatim below.

## Method (stated plainly)

- I built the images, so I cannot be a viewer — I know what each is made from. The maker
  is excluded from the data.
- Viewer A = a fresh Sonnet instance; Viewer B = a fresh Haiku instance. Two different
  eyes per image, 40 sessions total, all independent.
- Hesitation in a text viewer is visible as: hedges ("or at least," "possibly"),
  candidate lists, mid-answer revisions, and either/or constructions. These are marked,
  not interpreted.
- **Method flaw, recorded honestly:** in 4 of 40 sessions (all Viewer B) the viewer used
  the file path or ambient memory as evidence ("sketch301 course materials," "Jordan").
  The lab door wasn't fully shut. Next run: neutral filenames in a neutral directory.
  The flaw is itself data — see Observation 8.
- The "built from" line under each image states the instability mechanism I constructed.
  It was written before the sessions and is not a claim about what the image IS — the
  viewers got no version of it.

### img01 — built from: one oval, two conflicting attachments (curved stem to a bough; taut vertical string with loop)

**Viewer A:** "A cherry — or at least something that reads as one. There's a solid black
oval at the bottom (the fruit body), a thin curved line rising from it (the stem), and a
straight thin line dropping from above and off to the right with a small loop or knot
where it meets the stem area. The off-screen anchor of that hanging line gives the whole
thing a suspended, pendulum-like quality — as if the cherry is hanging by a thread from
something outside the frame."

**Viewer B:** "This is a minimalist drawing of a magnifying glass or loupe. It shows a
black circular lens at the bottom center, with a curved handle extending upward and to
the left, and a straight vertical stem or support line on the right side connecting to
the lens. The composition is simple and geometric, using solid black shapes against a
light beige background. It's the kind of stripped-down, essential representation you
might see in interface design or conceptual illustration — the idea of 'looking closer'
or 'investigation' reduced to its most basic visual components."

*Notes:* Confident disagreement (cherry vs magnifying glass). A hedges once ("or at
least something that reads as one") then commits and absorbs the second attachment into
the first reading (string becomes thread the cherry hangs by). B never hedges and
absorbs the stem as handle. Each viewer anchored on a different attachment and the
anchor's category re-described everything else.

*Notes:* Confident disagreement (vest vs lungs). Both classified the DARK shapes;

## Observations

1. **Nobody ever said "I don't know."** Forty sessions, zero refusals, zero blank
   answers. Every response produced a classification at SOME level. When the object
   level failed, the answer moved up a level and stabilized there: "it's a diagram"
   (06), "it's a study" (03B, 16, 19B), "it's a render" (12B, 20B), "it's writing in a
   script I can't read" (10). Identification failure did not produce uncertainty in the
   answer; it produced a different question being answered.

2. **Confident disagreement was the commonest outcome** (01, 02, 04, 05, 07, 09, 11,
   15, 18). From inside either response, nothing marks it as contested: each viewer is
   fluent, specific, and unhedged. The instability is visible only from the seat that
   can see both answers. A single viewer's confidence carries no information about
   stability.

3. **Hesitation has a syntax.** Where uncertainty did surface, it took exactly three
   forms: the candidate list ("black hole, whirlpool, or attractive force"; "dandelion,
   urchin, seed pod, chestnut husk, lint"), the hedge ("or at least something that reads
   as one"), and the held either/or ("fishbone diagram... reads equally well as
   botanical"; "window — or possibly a framed picture"). No viewer ever revised an
   answer mid-response and abandoned it; candidates were accumulated, never retracted.

4. **A viewer invented a feature.** B on img05 reported "the rounded head with an eye."
   There is no eye. The fish category arrived and supplied the anatomy the category
   required. Same shape as 201-A9's invented "knees together," now operating in
   classification: invention wears the same voice as perception.

5. **Different levels classify at different speeds and stabilities.** img10: the format
   (writing) classified instantly and irreversibly for both viewers while the content
   failed totally — and the format verdict was untouched by the content failure. img14:
   PLANT was instant and shared; the species stayed openly plural. img13: frame-plus-
   luminous was stable; window-vs-painting was stated as unresolved by both. Whatever
   "classification" is, it ran at several levels in the same glance, with independent
   outcomes.

6. **Built instabilities sorted into three observable fates.** (a) Split the viewers
   exactly as built: img11 (mic/brush — the same gridded dome named "wire bristles" and
   "mic mesh"). (b) Split the viewers on an axis I didn't build: img09 (built
   teapot/bird; split teapot/oil-lamp — the bird never existed for anyone). (c) Vanished
   entirely: img17's self-contradicting shading was reported as "consistent roundness
   throughout"; img02's and img16's negative-space figures were never entered. Some
   conflicts reach the report and are carried (img13); some are repaired before the
   report exists (img17). Where the repair happens is not visible in the data.

7. **Failed classification was repeatedly attributed to the maker.** "The restraint is
   deliberate" (05B), "the line refuses to declare what this is" (18B), "not trying to
   depict anything recognizable" (19B), "could be something or nothing... the restraint
   is deliberate" (16B). When the image refused to answer, viewers converted their
   failure into the image's intention — the unnameable became the intentionally
   unnamed, and THAT classification was delivered with confidence. (201's third finding
   — relations read natural or intentional — appearing inside classification behavior:
   intentionality served as a terminal category when object categories failed.)

8. **When the image underdetermines, classification recruits anything available.** Four
   sessions (all Viewer B) reached outside the image — file path, ambient memory — and
   wove it into the answer ("the kind of private notation Jordan sometimes uses"). A
   method flaw, recorded as such, and an observation: the question "What is it?" was
   answered with whatever evidence existed, with no apparent boundary at the image's
   edge.

9. **The anchor picks the category; the category re-describes the rest.** img01: A
   anchored the curved stem (organic → cherry) and the string became "a thread it hangs
   by"; B anchored the loop-and-circle (instrument → loupe) and the stem became "a
   handle." img09: the neck-tip became "flame or smoking wick" under the lamp reading.
   img05: shadow-prongs became "fins" under the fish reading. After the first
   commitment, no feature was ever reported as not fitting — every leftover was
   translated into the chosen category's vocabulary.

10. **Answers arrived with unearned furniture.** Material ("limestone or travertine"),
    provenance ("shot from very close," "created in 3D modeling software"), era and use
    ("mid-century icon," "dryer vent hose"). The category came as a bundle: naming the
    object also asserted how the image was made, of what, and what for — none of it
    shown.

11. **Even refusal borrows object names.** The viewer who declined to identify img15
    described it as "a bean or kidney shape" — shape-talk conducted entirely in object
    vocabulary. And the same viewer reported the deliberately open contour as "a single
    CLOSED line." The gap was repaired in the telling.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The re-leveling behavior (Obs 1, 5) would explain why "a viewer cannot confidently
  determine what it is" almost never produced a felt failure: confidence is preserved by
  quietly changing which question "What is it?" is taken to be. If that's right, the
  assignment's target condition was met constantly and invisibly — instability lived
  between answers and between levels, almost never inside one answer.
- Anchor-then-translate (Obs 9) reads like 201's sequence effects (A19, A25) transferred
  from meaning to naming: whatever is seen first installs the frame the rest is read in.
  Here the installed frame additionally GENERATED content (the eye, the wick, the fins).
- Obs 5 is the first direct pressure on "is classification one thing": format, category
  level, species level, and artifact-of-process level behaved like separable events with
  separate stabilities in the same forty answers. Not promoted to a finding — one
  assignment, one stimulus set, two viewer types.

## Open questions

- When a viewer answers "it's a study / an exercise / a render," is that a
  classification of the image or a refusal wearing one? "What is it?" was treated as at
  least four different questions across the sessions (what object / what format / what
  kind of image / what process made it). Is the question itself plural?
- Where does the repair happen? img17's contradiction never reached either report;
  img13's fork reached both and was carried openly. What sorts a conflict into
  "silently normalized" versus "stated and held"?
- The invented eye: does the category generate the feature, or does a half-feature
  recruit the category and then get promoted to fact? Order invisible in this data.
- Attributed intention acted as a terminal category — "deliberately ambiguous" ENDED
  inquiry as effectively as "teapot" did. Is authorship a classification stopper, and is
  that the same event as naming an object?
- Two confident viewers disagreeing vs one viewer oscillating: are these the same
  instability differently distributed, or two phenomena? The images that produced one
  rarely produced the other.
- Nobody entered the negative space (02, 16). Is figure/ground prior to classification,
  or a classification already made?
