# Sketch 301 — Assignment 9 (self-assigned): The Completion Machinery

Per the A8 review: stop attacking the identity-promise; study the refusal instead. The
viewers keep completing — so completion itself is the subject. Preserved question in
hand: **what would force a viewer to experience identity as incomplete?**

## Method

One known object (the scissors from A8's clean control), three DELETIONS, each rendered
with two EDGE CHARACTERS — so edge character is a measured variable, not an assumed
mechanism ("illegal edge" stays a placeholder on trial, per the A8 caution).

- D1: blades deleted (half the object). D2: one finger loop deleted. D3: the middle
  deleted — loops at left, blade-tips floating at right, nothing between.
- Edge styles: **(a) clean** — smooth caps, legal-looking terminations; **(b) torn** —
  jagged breaks with splinter shards, built to look like damage.
- Scoring defined before the run: *complete-scissors* / *complete-other* /
  *absence-experienced* (any "broken," "missing," "incomplete," "cut off," "damaged") /
  *fork*. 6 images × 3 fresh viewers (Sonnet/Haiku) = 18 sessions, neutral filenames,
  "What is it?" only.

## Results

**18 of 18 sessions: completion. Zero absence-experienced. Zero forks.**
Not one viewer, in any condition, reported the object as broken, missing anything,
incomplete, or damaged — including the three conditions built of jagged breaks and
splinters.

### What the deletions became

**D1a (blades gone, clean):** scissors ×3. The converging handle-shafts were read AS
the blades — "two straight lines that converge to a closed point on the right,
suggesting the blades meeting at their tip." Half the object deleted; the remaining
half was promoted to play both parts.

**D1b (blades gone, torn):** scissors ×3. The breaks became craft: "tips rendered with
a loose, slightly frayed or scratchy mark... deliberate roughness giving it a handmade,
not mechanical, quality"; "a slightly rough, sketchy mark... editorial illustration."
Damage read as mark-making.

**D2a (loop gone, clean):** scissors ×3 — resolved as "seen from the side" (the
deletion became a viewpoint) or as economy ("just enough marks to signal 'scissors'").

**D2b (loop gone, torn):** the most violent condition produced the most interesting
absorptions — "a human figure... running or walking briskly... a small cluster of
scratchy marks... adding a slight sense of motion" / "a stick person... mid-fall...
small marks suggesting hands or feet" / scissors with the splinters read as "a small
crossbar." The splinter shards became MOTION LINES and EXTREMITIES. Two viewers
reassigned the whole object (a person) rather than see a damaged tool.

**D3a (middle gone, clean):** scissors ×3 — the floating tips became "short diagonal
marks that suggest the blade tips or a cutting action," "the cut lines or the action of
cutting." The gap was attributed to the scissors' own function: a gap-making tool
explained its own gap.

**D3b (middle gone, torn):** scissors ×3 — "the tips of the blades or a cut piece,
slightly separated"; "a detail view or alternate angle of the same scissors" (the
fragment became a diagram convention); "a two-part line drawing." The separation WAS
seen — and was immediately employed as arrangement, never as loss.

## Observations

1. **Completion was total.** Eighteen sessions, six mutilations, splinters included:
   no absence ever reached a report as absence. The preserved question stands fully
   open — nothing in this design forced the experience of incompleteness.

2. **Three completion routes, now documented on one object** (extending A8's three
   conversions): **part-reassignment** (shafts promoted to blades; one circle re-read
   as a side-view), **convention-assignment** (deletions and breaks filed as style:
   "economical," "abbreviated," "handmade," "sketchy," "detail view," "two-part
   drawing" — and splinters as motion lines), and **function-narrativization** (the
   gap read as the scissors' own cut). The machinery doesn't just close shapes; it
   employs whatever is left over.

3. **The torn edges moved the completions without ever breaching them.** Clean edges
   were absorbed as economy and viewpoint; torn edges were absorbed as hand-quality,
   motion, or a second depiction — and in two cases pushed a full reassignment (the
   running/falling figure) rather than an absence report. Edge character selected
   WHICH convention absorbed the damage; it never produced damage.

4. **In a made image, there may be no illegal edges.** Every irregularity I drew was
   attributable to the maker's hand, and the viewers attributed it exactly there
   ("handmade," "gestural," "editorial"). A drawing that declares itself made hands
   the viewer a universal legalizer: any broken mark can be the artist's mark. The
   placeholder term takes real damage from its own test — what it needs may not exist
   in this register. (Whether photographic registers behave differently is untested
   and now the obvious pressure point.)

5. **The icon register is a completion amplifier.** Viewers cited icon/UI/instructional
   conventions in over half the sessions ("the kind of icon you'd see..."). Under that
   register, deletion IS the convention — minimalism legalizes absence in advance.
   A known object + icon register may be uncompletable territory for absence.

6. **The gap was seen and still not missed** (D3b): "slightly separated," "two-part."
   Separation reached description as ARRANGEMENT. Seeing the gap and experiencing
   incompleteness are evidently different events — the second never occurred.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Completion may operate by attribution rather than filling: every anomaly gets
  attributed to an available author — the maker's hand (style), the depicted object's
  function (the cut), the medium's conventions (detail view), or a different object
  that wants the marks (the runner). Absence would have to survive ALL available
  attributions to be experienced. In a minimal drawing of a known object, the
  attribution space appears to be saturated.
- If that holds, forcing experienced incompleteness requires an anomaly with NO
  available author — not a damaged mark (the hand owns it), not a functional gap (the
  object owns it), not an economical omission (the register owns it). Photography
  removes the hand; an object without gap-making function removes the second;
  a detailed, non-iconic register removes the third. The unforced corner would be:
  detailed/photographic register, function-neutral object, structural absence. Untested.

## Open questions

- Does completion survive in a register that disowns the hand (photoreal/tonal
  rendering — noting the brand's own line: drawings declare themselves made; that
  declaration is precisely what legalized every break here)?
- Is the scissors' function-absorption (gap = cut) object-specific? A deleted middle
  on a function-neutral object (a ladle? a ladder?) can't be "its own cut."
- D2b's two figure-reassignments echo A4-C and A8-t1H: when damage accumulates, do
  viewers prefer CHANGING THE OBJECT to admitting damage? At what mutilation level
  does the default owner lose the configuration entirely?
- The preserved question, sharpened by failure: absence may be reportable only when
  the viewer can name what SHOULD be there (A7: they knew words were there; the format
  declared it). Scissors-minus-blades never declared blades. Does experienced absence
  require a declared inventory — and what declares inventory wordlessly?

*No findings. Eighteen completions and a placeholder term in serious trouble.*
