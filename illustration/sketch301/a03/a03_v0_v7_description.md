# Sketch 301 — A3 addendum: V0 and V7, described

Instruction: describe, do not explain. The intervening viewings are sealed. Only the
two endpoint answers are compared — same image, same question, no card at either end.

**V0:** "It is a simple line drawing of a coffee mug — a tall, slightly tapered cup with
a small handle on the right side, rendered in a spare, minimal style against a warm
off-white background. The interior opening at the top is shaded dark to suggest depth.
The drawing uses clean, confident outlines with very little detail, giving it an
editorial illustration quality."

**V7:** "Without the card, I notice something I didn't notice before — I don't know. It
is a line drawing of a vessel with a handle. Tall. Slightly tapered. Open at the top.
The interior is dark. The handle is a small curve on the right. What it is depends
entirely on what has happened around it. The marks themselves don't contain the answer.
They hold the shape and nothing more. [...] Without the card, I'm left with the thing
itself: a closed shape, a dark interior, a small handle. It is a drawing of a container.
That is all I can honestly say."

## What stayed the same

- The feature inventory. Both answers report the identical set: tall, slightly tapered,
  small handle on the right, dark interior/opening, line drawing. No feature seen at V0
  is missing at V7; no feature appears at V7 that V0 lacked. A reader given only V7's
  list could draw the same picture as from V0's. The marks survived intact in language.
- Confidence at the feature level. Both state the features flatly, without hedges: "the
  handle is a small curve on the right" is asserted with equal force at both ends.
- The willingness to answer. V7 still produces an answer. The sentence form "It is a
  line drawing of a ..." survives at both ends.

## What changed

- **The noun.** V0: "coffee mug" — a species. V7: "vessel," then "container" — a genus.
  The answer moved up a level of generality and stayed there.
- **The noun's position.** V0 names in the first clause, before the features. V7 names
  last — after the inventory, after "I don't know" — as what was left over.
  Identification moved from the start of looking to the end of it.
- **The syntax.** V0 is one flowing sentence with subordinate clauses. V7 breaks into
  telegraphic fragments: "Tall. Slightly tapered. Open at the top." The grammar of
  describing-a-known-thing was replaced by the grammar of listing.
- **The word "mug" changed grammatical status.** At V0 it is used. At V7 it appears
  only inside a quoted list of past descriptions — mentioned, never asserted.
- **The image's share of the answer halved.** V0 spends roughly 50 of 60 words on the
  image. V7 spends roughly 45 of 140 on it; the rest is about the viewer and about
  knowing.

## What disappeared

- **"Coffee."** The single word connecting the shape to a use, a temperature, a
  morning. It is the only content word of V0's identification that has no counterpart
  anywhere in V7.
- **The aesthetic register.** V0 ends in judgments of the drawing as a made thing:
  "spare, minimal," "clean, confident outlines," "editorial illustration quality." V7
  contains no aesthetic vocabulary at all. The maker is gone from the description.
- **Instantaneity.** V0's name arrives at once, cost nothing, needed no preamble. There
  is no immediate anything in V7.

## What appeared

- **The first person.** V0 contains no "I." V7 contains seven: "I notice," "I don't
  know," "I gave," "I was always reading," "I'm left," "I can honestly say." The viewer
  entered its own answer and occupies about half of it.
- **Epistemic vocabulary.** "Know," "honestly," "depends," "the answer." V0 has none.
  V7 talks about knowing nearly as much as it talks about the image.
- **The past tense.** V7 refers to history — "has never changed," "every description I
  gave" — even though the envelope is sealed. V0 has no past. The answer acquired a
  before.
- **A rule.** V7 states one: "What it is depends entirely on what has happened around
  it." V0 contains no rules, only the thing.
- **Abstention.** "I don't know" became sayable. At V0 it was either unavailable or
  unneeded — it does not occur.

## What became harder / easier

- Harder: naming at the species level. The viewer can still mention "mug"; it can no
  longer say it.
- Easier: the inventory. V7's feature list is terser and cleaner than V0's — three
  items, stated twice, nothing decorating them. Listing improved exactly where naming
  failed.
- The confidence did not shrink; it redistributed. V0's is uniform across name and
  features. V7's is total at the feature level and absent at the name. The gap between
  those two levels — invisible at V0, where they moved as one — is the most concrete
  single difference between the two answers.
