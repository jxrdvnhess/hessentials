## Observations

1. **The dose never reached the Sonnet — +1 at all four steps.** Embedded, released,
   salient, dominant: six, every time. This diagonal two-cluster family is simply
   a +1 field for that cohort — eight lifetime Sonnet over-counts now, all on
   half-claimed cluster/orbit/drift readings. The fifth member's position and size
   were irrelevant to an error that was never about the fifth member.

2. **The Haiku's perfect record broke — at the DOMINANT dose.** The cohort that had
   counted correctly through every prior assignment reported six when the fifth
   became the largest, most separate element — and placed the surplus in the FAR
   cluster: "a group of three smaller on the left" (the left group is a pair), with
   the near cluster hedged ("two to three"). The most salient member did not get
   miscounted; the error settled at the quietest site in the field, on the opposite
   side from the attention-grabbing blob.

3. **The embedded cell exposed the symmetrization mechanism nakedly.** "Two
   clusters... each roughly three" — the same equal-clusters claim that DELETED a
   member in A26 ("two clusters, each a pair") here INFLATED the pair to match the
   triple. One claim, two directions, both toward equality. The adjustment's sign
   is not a property of the claim; the claim demands symmetry and the field pays
   wherever payment is cheapest.

4. **A28 versus A29 is the real result.** Same five blobs, same cohorts, same
   question. A28's symmetric 2+2+1 with a schema available (face — slots for
   everything): 8/8 correct censuses. A29's schema-less diagonal cluster field:
   5/8 erroneous. The protective factor in A28 was evidently not salience, as I
   proposed — it was the SCHEMA. Salience, dosed directly, protected nothing here;
   at maximum it broke the second cohort. The A28 explanation is half-overturned
   by its own follow-up: the fifth was promoted there because a schema had a seat
   for it, not because it was unmissable.

5. **Reframe forced by the data:** "absorbable member" was the wrong unit. The
   census error does not live ON a member; it lives in the FIELD, at its least
   guarded location — absorbed into the far cluster, the overlapping pair, the
   crowd. Members are not absorbed; adjustments are. Salience doesn't shield a
   member from deletion — it pulls the audit away from wherever the books are
   being balanced. (The brief's own terms — absorbed / retained / promoted —
   turned out to describe sites, not members.)

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Combining A26–A29: a claim with an arithmetic demand (equal clusters, symmetric
  pattern) collects its adjustment at the field's lowest-attention site, in
  whichever direction satisfies the demand. A schema that seats every element
  leaves no site unguarded — hence A28's perfection. A field with a dominant
  attractor leaves the rest of the field UNDER-guarded — hence the Haiku's first
  error arriving with the dominant blob: the fifth didn't get miscounted; it
  stole the audit. If this holds, the illustrator's census lever is attention
  allocation, not element design.
- The Sonnet's flat +1 across doses suggests its cluster-reading of this family
  carries a fixed surplus independent of layout detail — consistent with the
  constructed-relations account (A25) and untouched by this manipulation, which
  never altered the reading, only the fifth.

## Open questions

- The schema-protection account now needs its direct test: the A29 field WITH a
  seatable schema overlaid (can a five-slot reading of this diagonal layout be
  invited — and does the census heal?).
- Does a dominant attractor degrade censuses generally (add a dominant element to
  a previously well-counted row)?
- The symmetrization direction: what picks inflate vs deflate? (A26 deflated a
  triple; A29 inflated a pair. Both equalized. Smallest-change? Site-availability?)
- The Sonnet's +1 family: is there ANY five-blob two-cluster diagonal field that
  cohort counts correctly? (A definitional control for "half-claimed.")

*No findings. The dose missed the Sonnet entirely, broke the Haiku at maximum
salience, and the member turned out never to be the unit — the field balances its
books wherever no one is looking.*
