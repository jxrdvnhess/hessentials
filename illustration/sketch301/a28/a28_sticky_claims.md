four steps; the fifth element fixed (top-center, isolated) in every image. Target:
the point at which the pair claim releases the fifth.

## Observations

1. **The pair claim is not sticky — it released everything at the first loosening.**
   Pair language: 2/2 at the clean dose, 0/6 at every dose after. The release point
   sits between ~70px and ~115px of pair separation, and the release was total — no
   hedged pair-talk, no residue. On this gradient the pair claim behaved like the
   radial in A27: fully fluid. The assignment's title hypothesis takes a direct
   hit: the claim the record nominated as sticky bent immediately.

2. **The census was perfect at every dose — 8/8 — including clean pairs.** A26's
   −1 did NOT replicate under this layout. The deletion event now has a boundary
   condition visible in the comparison: A26's fifth element sat INSIDE a diagonal
   cluster of three — absorbable, non-salient; A28's fifth stands alone at
   top-center — maximally salient. **The salient remainder was never deleted; it
   was PROMOTED.** Every viewer gave it a role: forehead, nose, head, "mother,"
   "leader," focal point. Roster-deletion appears to require an absorbable member;
   salience buys immunity.

3. **The clean symmetric layout recruited a schema that had slots for everything.**
   The Sonnet read the 2+2+1 as a FACE — pairs as eyes ("an iris and a highlight"),
   the fifth as forehead — and the face-schema's roster matched the page exactly,
   so the census was right for schema reasons, not counting reasons. A claim with
   five slots counts five. (The same machinery that deleted A26's fifth protected
   A28's: the difference is only whether the recruited structure has a seat for the
   odd member.)

4. **Social vocabulary arrived without agents.** "Almost like a mother and children,
   or a leader and followers" — the first social-role reading in the blob series,
   produced by pure arrangement: one-above, four-below, alone-versus-grouped. A22
   found that blobs can be alone but not lonely; A28 adds that they can apparently
   be PARENTS and LEADERS once the geometry supplies hierarchy. Noted and parked
   per the warning — narrative remains off-limits — but the record should hold
   this: relational ROLES crossed the line that emotional states did not.

5. **Hedges tracked the claim-shift zone again** (b and c: "rough arc or cluster,"
   "could also simply be") and were absent at the schema-claimed clean dose —
   consistent with every instrument reading since A19: fluent where a claim grips,
   disjunctive where claims compete.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Stickiness, as currently observable, is not a property of claim TYPE (pairs were
  nominated and acquitted). The record's two deletion/inflation events shared
  something else: a misfit member that could be absorbed or invented WITHOUT
  perceptual protest — A26's fifth hidden in a triple, A21/A25's phantom amid an
  overlapping size-hierarchy. Census error may need claim-misfit PLUS a
  low-salience site for the adjustment. Sticky claims may not exist; what exists
  may be quiet places to put the error.
- The face event suggests schema recruitment trumps sub-structure: given a
  symmetric 2+2+1, "two pairs" survived only as the face's internal anatomy. The
  strongest available schema doesn't fight smaller claims — it hires them.

## Open questions

- Direct test of the salience account: A26's exact diagonal 2+3 with the absorbed
  fifth PULLED OUT of the triple by 60px — does the −1 vanish as the member gains
  salience? (The reciprocal of this assignment; one cell.)
- Does the face-schema's protection generalize — does any 5-slot schema (a hand?
  a quincunx die-face?) guarantee correct census wherever it is recruited?
- The social-role line (Obs 4) is now crossed at pure-arrangement level. What
  geometry is minimal for it — is one-above-many sufficient, or did size hierarchy
  carry it?
- Pair separation released between 70 and 115px: is the release threshold a
  proximity constant, or relative to element size/frame?

*No findings. The nominated sticky claim bent at the first touch; the fifth element
was promoted, not deleted; and the only thing that has ever absorbed a census error
is a place where no one was looking.*
