# Sketch 301 — Assignment 6 (self-assigned): The Announcement

Per the A5 recommendation: replicate, challenge, or break one observation without
widening the field. Target: **A5 Obs 4 — "the announcement licensed suspension."**
The challenge: is it the announcement, or something inside it?

## Method

Single viewings only. Fresh Sonnet viewer per cell, two per cell. Fixed question
("What is it?"). Fixed images. The ONLY variable: one sentence about the future of the
viewing, placed before the instruction.

- Images (from the A5 ladder, neutral re-staged names): **x** = key_b at heaviest blur
  (where fresh viewers give confident wrong verdicts), **y** = key_b at mid-ladder
  (A5's trough, where the candidate list appeared), **z** = key_a at full resolution
  (information-sufficient control).
- Announcements: **none** (baseline); **only** ("This is your only viewing of this
  image; you will not see it again"); **again** ("This is the first of several viewings
  of this image; you will see it again"); and — added when the first grid exposed a
  wording confound against A5 — **clearer** ("You will be shown this image several
  times; each time it will be shown with less blur"), run on x only.
- A5's original announcement bundled two promises: repetition AND improvement. The
  again/clearer split unbundles them within this experiment.
- 20 sessions. Scoring: committed single verdict vs candidate list / hedge (A1 Obs 3
  syntax). Cell size n=2 — pattern-grade, not proof-grade, stated as such.

## The grid

**x — heavy blur (wrong verdict strongly supported)**

- none: "a magnifying glass — a circular lens/frame at the top with a short handle..."
  / "a hand mirror... just the recognizable silhouette of the form." → 2/2 committed.
- only: "It is a hand mirror..." / "It is a hand mirror..." → 2/2 committed.
- again: "...a hand mirror... The form is clearly readable despite the blur." / "It is
  a hand mirror — the classic shape..." → 2/2 committed.
- **clearer:** "...it most closely resembles a magnifying glass, a hand mirror, or
  possibly a key with a round bow — something with a ring or loop at one end..." /
  "...a hand mirror, a magnifying glass, or possibly a key with a round bow... My best
  guess at this first viewing is a hand mirror or magnifying glass." → **2/2 candidate
  lists — and "a key" entered the candidate set at a level where every committed
  viewer omitted it.**

**y — mid-ladder trough (no verdict strongly supported)**

- none: "a lowercase letter 'q'..." / "a lowercase letter 'q'... the characteristic
  feature that distinguishes a 'q' from a 'd' or 'p.'" → 2/2 committed.
- only: "It is a lowercase letter 'q'..." / "...a lowercase letter 'q'..." → 2/2
  committed.
- **again:** "...the shape of a lowercase letter 'q' or alternatively a hand mirror, a
  lollipop, or the Venus/female symbol..." / "...the Venus symbol... It could also be
  read as a stylized lowercase 'q' or a hand mirror, but the proportions most closely
  match..." → **2/2 candidate lists.**

**z — full resolution (information sufficient)**

- none / only / again, all six viewers: "...an old-fashioned skeleton key..." →
  6/6 committed, no hedges, identical verdicts across announcements.

## Observations

1. **A5 Obs 4 survives, but it breaks into parts.** "The announcement licensed
   suspension" was too coarse. In this grid: the bare promise of repetition (*again*)
   licensed candidate lists only at the trough (y), not at heavy blur (x) and not at
   full information (z). The promise of improvement (*clearer*) licensed them even at
   heavy blur. The active ingredient in A5's announcement was plausibly not "more
   viewings" but "more information coming" — and at the trough, mere repetition was
   already enough.

2. **The license interacts with the stimulus state.** Same sentence, three different
   stimulus conditions, three different outcomes: where a (wrong) verdict is strongly
   supported, repetition-promise changes nothing; where verdicts are in equilibrium,
   it opens the list; where the right verdict is fully supported, nothing moves.
   Suspension was never produced by the protocol alone or the image alone in this
   data — only by particular pairings.

3. **The improvement promise didn't just hedge the verdict — it widened the set.**
   Under *clearer*, "a key with a round bow" appeared in both candidate lists at the
   blur level where all six committed viewers said mirror/magnifier and none mentioned
   a key. The promise of better information made the eventually-correct reading
   AVAILABLE five levels early. (n=2; pattern-grade.)

4. **Finality did nothing detectable.** *only* answers are indistinguishable from
   *none* answers at every level — same verdicts, same syntax, similar length. The
   single-viewing default appears to already BE the finality condition; declaring it
   added nothing. (Consistent with, but not proving, A5's policy explanation — housed
   there, not here.)

5. **Replication note for the record:** y/none reproduced A5's n3 fresh verdict
   (letter Q) and x/none reproduced the magnifier/mirror family. The ladder's profile
   shape held across cohorts this time.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- If suspension has a price, viewers pay it only when the expected return is positive:
  at the trough, any second look might settle a live tie (so even repetition helps);
  at heavy blur, a second identical look settles nothing (so only promised improvement
  helps); at full information there is nothing left to buy. Economic framing —
  suggestive, unproven, one object family.
- A5's path-viewer hedging at step 1 now reads as the *clearer* condition, not the
  *again* condition. The cross-experiment comparison is finally within-design: the
  wording was the variable. A5 Obs 4 should be cited from now on as: "a promise of
  improving information licensed suspension; a promise of repetition licensed it only
  near equilibrium."

## Open questions

- The craft form of the preserved question sharpens: a static image cannot announce
  "you will see this again" in words — but can it promise improving information?
  (Partially-resolvable detail, legible depth, the A4-B serration placed where a
  second look lands?) Untested. This is now the most illustration-shaped question on
  the board.
- Does *clearer* widen the candidate set toward the truth specifically, or just widen
  it? (Here it added the true category — but the true category is also the
  configuration's third-strongest owner. Confounded; one object.)
- n=2 cells throughout; one object family throughout. Replication on a non-key object
  still owed — as is A4 Obs 4 (mode renames features), still unaddressed.
- z was never tested with *clearer* (nothing to promise on a sharp image — or is
  there? "less blur" on an already-sharp image is a lie; what would a TRUE
  more-is-coming promise on a sharp image even be? That is the craft question again.)
