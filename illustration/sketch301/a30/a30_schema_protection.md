error? Same five blob identities throughout; only the arrangement's schema-ability
varied, from the A29 +1 field up to a clean quincunx. (The field cannot stay fixed
AND change schema — the standing tension since A23; identities held, positions

## Observations

1. **Schema protection confirmed — and it healed the cohort that has never been
   healed.** The Sonnet over-counted the unprotected field (six, the now-standard +1)
   and counted five everywhere a schema was available — including, decisively, the
   strong quincunx, which it named outright as "the five-side of a die." This is the
   FIRST time in the entire blob series that the Sonnet has counted this element-
   identity set correctly. The +1 that survived position, separation, salience, and
   dominance (A29) was dissolved by a recruitable schema. Schema is the lever the
   A29 dose failed to find.

2. **The repair is graded, and the gradient is in the right order.** Unprotected →
   six. Weak → five but schema-free ("no obvious pattern" — counted right by
   declining to construct, the candidate-zone route, not by recruiting). Strong →
   five via the die-schema, named. The protection arrived precisely with the
   recruitable schema, not before; the weak cell counted right for the OTHER reason
   (no claim, so nothing to misfit), making the two correct cells diagnostically
   different — important for not over-reading "strong schema fixed it."

3. **The strong schema is the first claim in the series with FIXED, CORRECT
   arithmetic.** Every prior arithmetic-bearing claim demanded a number that
   diverged from the page (pairs → 4, equal-clusters → 6, 4+4 → 8). The die-five
   demands exactly five and the page has exactly five — so the recital, for once,
   recites the truth. Schema protection may be the same recital mechanism running
   on a schema whose roster happens to match. (Not protection BY structure — coincidence
   OF roster. Held; it is the A28-face result again, now isolated from salience.)

4. **Competing schemas did not destabilize the census — both schemas seat five.**
   The 2+2+1/quincunx cell: Sonnet held the fork open (face / dice), Haiku committed
   to die-five; both counted five. When the alternatives AGREE on the roster, the
   competition is invisible to the count. (The interesting competing case — two
   schemas with DIFFERENT rosters — was not buildable from five symmetric blobs;
   flagged.)

5. **The cohort lock, restated precisely after thirty assignments:** the Haiku
   never miscounted here (4/4) and the Sonnet miscounted only the schema-less field
   (1/4). The two cohorts converge wherever a schema is present and diverge only on
   bare scatter — consistent with the A27 revision-policy reading (Haiku files
   genre and counts the page; Sonnet constructs relations and pays their roster)
   AND with A29's field account (the schema removes the unguarded site).

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The whole census arc (A24–A30) now reduces to one statement, offered for the
  professor to break: **the reported count is the roster of the strongest recruited
  structure; error is the roster's divergence from the page.** No structure → the
  cohort that constructs one pays its surplus (Sonnet +1), the cohort that doesn't
  reports the page (Haiku). Misfitting structure → divergence in the structure's
  direction (pairs −1, clusters +1, symmetry rounding). FITTING structure (rows, 1+4
  radial, the quincunx, the face) → roster equals page, census correct. "Schema
  protection" is the third case; it is not a separate mechanism, it is roster-match.
- If that is right, the protection is not that schema STABILIZES counting — it is
  that a five-slot schema laid over five elements cannot produce a divergent roster.
  The professor's warning holds and sharpens: this schema protected this count
  because its roster was five; a five-slot schema over six elements should DELETE
  one. That is the clean next test.

## Open questions

- The roster-match prediction, directly: a die-five quincunx schema invited over SIX
  elements — does the schema delete the sixth to keep its five-slot roster? (Falsifies
  or confirms "schema = roster-match," and would be the strongest census result yet.)
- Competing schemas with DIFFERENT rosters (a layout readable as 2+2+1=5 OR as
  3+3=6 overlapping) — does the census flicker between rosters, or pick one?
- Weak-cell correctness was no-claim, not schema — is there a dose where the schema
  is strong enough to recruit but its roster is wrong, isolating recital-on-schema
  from no-claim-counting?
- Thirty assignments of cohort lock: the single cleanest test remaining is a third
  cohort (a human, or a third model) on the bare scatter — does +1 belong to Sonnet
  or to relational reading as such?

*No findings. The schema the field could not refuse counted it correctly for the
first time — and the likeliest reason is the dullest: a five-slot schema over five
dots has no spare seat to fill and no leftover to drop.*
