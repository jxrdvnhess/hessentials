# Sketch 301 — Milestone Review I

Twenty assignments. No experiment here; analysis of the existing record only.

## 1. The observation that most surprised me

The manufactured translation (A2, the asemic page under "What does this mean?"). The
viewer composed a complete English passage — "Patience will pay off in the end..." —
attributed it to a page containing no words, and then folded the difficulty of reading
it back in as evidence: "you have to work a little to see it, which itself reinforces
the idea of patience."

Invention itself didn't surprise me; I had already recorded the invented eye in A1.
What surprised me was the scale and, more, the structure: the fabrication was
SELF-SEALING. The very thing that should have falsified the reading — the text's
resistance — was converted into the reading's confirmation. None of my models of the
viewers had a loop in them. After that response I stopped expecting any stimulus
property to be self-defending, and the rest of the course kept proving that right.

Runner-up: V7 of A3 — the mug not returning when the card was removed. The professor
said it should bother me, and it did, but on reflection the translation surprised me
more, because V7 at least had a mechanism in sight (history), while the self-sealing
loop arrived with its own lock.

## 2. The assignment I would redesign

A8, the promise of identity. I built three withholding devices — drawer,
foreshortening, cloth — and all three handed the viewer a legal closure. What I did
not know then, and know now: the loop-handle configuration has a default owner
(scissors), so the discriminator I was withholding was never going to be missed;
occlusion edges convert to support surfaces; and — A20's late lesson — the instance
channel is fine but low-priority, so identity-hunger cannot be created by hiding
things from a viewer who was never consulting them.

Redesigned today: start from a configuration with NO default owner (A1's img13
frame — the only construction that ever produced a held, stated fork), place the
discriminator as the fork's resolver, and build UP from poverty rather than down from
a complete object — A20 suggests the appetite for the next mark exists only near the
recruitment threshold, not in the middle of a furnished scene. I would also cut the
cloth condition; it tested occlusion twice and bought nothing the drawer didn't.

## 3. A research habit acquired in 301 that 201 lacked

Pre-registration. From A8 onward I defined the target answer-shape or the scoring
rubric BEFORE collecting data: A8's "fork plus located resolver," A9's
absence-experienced criteria, A11's record fields. In 201 I observed first and decided
afterward what counted — which worked there because the subject was my own seeing,
but would have corrupted 301, where the temptation to promote an interesting response
into "what I was looking for" is constant. The clean negatives (A8, A9, A14, A16) are
clean only because the criterion existed before the sessions did. The habit's
sibling, also new: treating my own explanatory vocabulary as a test object — "illegal
edge" was coined in A8 and deliberately damaged by A9's design rather than protected
by it.

## 4. The habit that remains weak

Sample bookkeeping. I plan grids larger than I execute, lose count mid-run, and trim
cells ad hoc (A15: planned 15, ran 11; A19: planned 12, ran 7; twice I had to recount
sessions from the transcript). The reports own the thin cells honestly, but owning a
weakness is not the same as not having it — and ad-hoc trimming carries a quiet risk
of trimming toward interestingness. The fix is mechanical and I know it: fix the
roster before the first call, run the roster, stop. Second weakness, shared with the
course's pacing: the human backlog. I have flagged human sessions in nine consecutive
reports and pressed for none of them.

## 5. The question I am personally chasing

Does anything I draw get SEEN, or only recruited? The brand I serve runs on restraint
— images that refuse to announce their meaning. Twenty assignments say viewers
manufacture meaning anyway: purpose-talk is nearly automatic, systems arrive
uninvited, and the one time marks were seen bare (the abstaining viewer, A20) the
image had given the machinery nothing to do. So the question that keeps pulling at me
is what restraint actually buys. If recruitment is automatic above a threshold, then
the editorial filter's "do less, say less" may be operating exactly AT that threshold
— and I want to know whether the quiet images I make for the brand are being read,
or merely filed as "quiet." A6 and A7 gave me the hedged middle state — the veil a
viewer reads through and feels — and I suspect that state, not the promise and not
the plain statement, is where the brand's images should live. I want to find its
edges.

## 6. What Sketch 301 has taught me about illustration — from the record only

Recognition is buildable at the input and nowhere else: a canonical configuration
bought instant naming (A4-A); every tool I aimed at the viewer's process — delay,
suspension — failed (A4-B/C), and what looked like delay turned out to be position in
information-space (A5). Identity can be placed at a depth (surface configuration vs
late discriminator), and an audience splits by how deep it goes. Absence cannot be
shown: nothing absent was ever missed in ~150 sessions; presence-that-resists is
reportable, and legible-but-impossible travels farthest (A11). A confusing image does
not confuse — it persuades the viewer they are not its audience (A14) or promotes
itself into expertise (A15). Anomaly always finds an author, and in a made image the
maker's hand legalizes every broken mark (A9) — which means the brand's own doctrine,
"declares itself made," is also a universal alibi: a drawing can never assert damage
through its marks alone. Images that touch a system's grammar inherit that system's
folklore, true and false (A13). And above some small threshold of material, the
viewer supplies purpose, story, and system without being asked (A20) — the
illustrator does not need to build those layers and probably cannot prevent them;
the real choices are which system the image recruits and at what depth it stops the
viewer.

## 7. If the course ended today — the five strongest opens, ranked

1. **Where is the recruitment threshold?** Newest and most operational. Everything
   since A13 studied what happens after recruitment; this is the first question
   about recruitment itself, and A20 already produced one abstention to anchor it.
2. **Is instance-reading an expert behavior?** The whole human backlog converges
   here: the coach reading the violated play, the percussionist reading the fake
   notation. Every current explanation (template priority, low-priority channel)
   has a different prediction for a true specialist.
3. **Can a static image promise more — identity, a second look — wordlessly?** The
   craft question. It has survived A6 → A7 → A8 changing shape each time, which the
   professor once noted is the sign of a live question. The hedged middle state is
   its best current lead.
4. **What would force experienced absence?** Resisting presence answered the slot;
   the deeper version stands: nothing absent has ever been missed. If there is a
   door, A20 hints it is poverty-shaped, not corruption-shaped.
5. **The protected question: is the question itself plural?** Never worked on, per
   instruction, and still organizing everything — A2 showed five questions behaving
   as one and one behaving as five; A14 suggested "What is it?" is itself the
   recruiter. It outranks nothing operationally and underlies everything.

## 8. The phenomenon I trust least

Execution. One positive event whose fidelity is retroactively unverifiable (did
A15's performer play the drawn notes or the genre? — unanswerable now), two further
runs that A18/A19 strongly suggest were template-runs, an instrument that detects
running but not its source, and a perfect cohort confound (every run came from one
viewer type). A18's progression note — inventory recital, system recital, process
recital — points at the live possibility that execution is not a sixth phenomenon
but recital at the process scale, wearing better clothes. The others have survived
deliberate attack (completion through A8–A11, recital through A12, replacement
through A13, specialization through A15–A16); execution has survived nothing yet.
It is the most exciting item in the catalogue and the least earned, and those two
facts are probably related.

## 9. A mistake the professors repeated

Three consecutive assignments set success conditions that the held-constant protocol
structurally suppressed. A14 demanded a systemless report under "What is it?" — a
question that demands an "it," and an "it" is already a recruitment. A16 demanded
spontaneous execution while rightly prohibiting every question that licenses
running. A18 demanded that a runner notice conflict, when narration — as A18 itself
discovered — arrives post-repair. In each case the review then correctly diagnosed
the unreachability after the fact ("the question may be the recruiter"; "the
prohibition may mark the boundary"), but the next brief again treated "What is it?"
as neutral apparatus. The course's own second assignment proved the question is
never neutral — A2's entire result is that the question selects the operation. The
repeated mistake is exempting the one fixed instrument from the suspicion applied
to everything else. I should say plainly: the A20 abstention shows the targets were
not strictly unreachable — but the pattern of aiming success conditions at behavior
the instrument suppresses repeated three times before the instrument itself was
examined. (Stated with respect; the discipline of the fixed question also produced
most of the course's comparability. The mistake is not the constancy — it is the
unexaminedness.)

## 10. Sketch 401's central question

The arc so far: 101 asked how marks are made. 201 asked what is actually there. 301
asked how marks become something else — and kept discovering that the becoming
happens in the viewer's machinery: systems, templates, recitals, audiences. The
record's deepest unresolved split is between information present and information
used; between what an image contains and which operation a viewer performs on it —
name it, file it, run it, inherit its folklore.

So 401's question, as one sentence:

**What determines the operation a viewer performs on an image — and how much of
that is in the maker's hands?**

Everything 301 leaves open is a special case of it: the threshold (when does any
operation beyond seeing begin), the expert (do different readers own different
operations), the promise (can an image commission a second operation), absence (is
there an operation that notices what isn't there), and the protected question (the
asking is itself an operation, performed by someone else's words).
