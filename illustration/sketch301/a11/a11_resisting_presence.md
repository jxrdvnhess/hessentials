## Observations

1. **The slot entered the report if and only if something occupied it.** Presence:
   7 of 8 viewers mentioned the mark. Absence: 0 of 3 (0 of 6 with A10). The
   resisting-presence hypothesis survived its direct test, on the design the
   hypothesis itself proposed.

2. **Absence behaves differently from presence; and wrong presence behaves differently
   from resistant presence.** The three conditions produced three distinct report
   shapes: nothing (B); an occupant — classified or held with hedges (C); an anomaly
   judgment — "doesn't belong" (D). The brief's three comparisons all discriminated.

3. **Absence survived inside a reported anomaly.** D's viewers saw the 13, called it
   wrong, located it — and still asserted "numerals 1 through 12." Wrongness was
   reportable; the absence it implied was not. Substitution read as surplus.

4. **The category recital ran under every condition.** "1 through 12" was asserted
   over a missing 7 (B), beside a smudge (C, implicitly), and around a reported 13
   (D). It behaves like a recital, not a description — recited even while its
   falsifier is being pointed at.

5. **Resistance is viewer-relative** (A7 Obs 2, replicated on a new mechanism): the
   same smudge was confidently completed into "a stack of books" by both Sonnets and
   held open as "a mark or symbol" by both Haikus. What resists one viewer's
   classifier yields to another's.

6. **Every reported anomaly received an author.** Books were "decorative... an
   app-icon element"; the 13 was "a deliberate joke," "a quirk"; the smudge "a maker's
   mark." A9's attribution machinery, running unchanged: presence gets filed under
   intent. The clock's own identity never flickered — 14 of 14 complete. Across
   A9–A11 the count now stands at **zero absence reports in 47 altered-image
   sessions** — in this course so far.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Wrongness is a property of a present mark; absence is a property of an expectation.
  These viewers report marks. The expectation channel appears to be serviced by the
  category recital, which is generated, not checked — so absence never finds a
  reporter: the mark-channel has nothing to carry, and the expectation-channel always
  says "complete."
- Refinement of resisting presence: the smudge resists CATEGORIALLY (unclassifiable)
  and was hedged or converted; the 13 resists SEMANTICALLY (classifiable, impermissible)
  and was reported as wrong by every viewer who saw it. Semantic resistance traveled
  furthest. If the craft goal is a mark the viewer must report, this run says: make it
  legible and impossible, not illegible.

## Open questions

- Can the recital itself be broken? A clock with nine evenly spaced numerals — correct
  spacing, wrong count, no slot to occupy: does "1 through 12" still get recited over
  a structure with no lacuna at all?
- Substitution-blindness: every numeral replaced by well-formed wrong glyphs — at what
  density does the recital fail?
- D's misser (Haiku) and C's two completers: the resistance threshold moved with
  viewer acuity both times. Where is the mark that resists EVERY cohort — or is
  resistance always relative?
