## Observations

1. **The phantom member has a rule: scatter → N+1.** The five-scatter drew its
   phantom sixth from a Sonnet for the THIRD consecutive independent session — and
   the six-scatter drew a phantom SEVENTH from a fresh Sonnet. The over-count is not
   "this image looks like six." It is a constant +1 bias that moved with the element
   count. Same scatter character, different N, same surplus of exactly one.

2. **Rows followed the elements — at both counts, in both cohorts.** Four of four
   correct, including the extended row, where the ellipsis-lexicon stretched to "six"
   without rounding to its preferred lengths. Where the organization is simple and
   claimed, its arithmetic and the elements' arithmetic agree, and the census is
   right for either reason.

3. **The over-count is cohort-locked.** Every scatter over-count in the course —
   now four — is a Sonnet; every Haiku scatter count is correct (four of four
   across A21/A25). And the cohorts' description styles differ in exactly one
   relevant way: the Sonnets read STRUCTURE into the scatter ("the largest circle
   near the center with smaller ones distributed around it," "a loose diagonal
   drift") while the Haikus file it as genre ("minimalist composition," "balance
   through asymmetry") and move on. The cohort that constructs more relations
   reports more members. The richer reading inflates the census; the flatter
   reading gets it right.

4. **Enumeration now shows three regimes across the record** (assignment question
   answered at observation grade): ELEMENTS — claimed-simple configurations (rows,
   contoured ring) are counted correctly, 8/8 lifetime; PATTERN — claimed-patterned
   configurations are rounded to the pattern's arithmetic (A24: nine read as 4+4=8,
   both cohorts); ESTIMATE — unclaimed scatter is estimated, with a reproducible
   +1 inflation in one cohort. "Does counting follow the elements or the pattern?"
   — it follows whichever organization is in power, and when none is, it is not
   counting at all.

5. **One confound owned:** the added sixth blob gave the scatter a weak diagonal
   axis, which the Sonnet reported ("diagonal drift"). The six-scatter is therefore
   slightly less unorganized than the five-scatter — and its census still inflated
   by exactly one, suggesting the bias survives weak organization. Both Haiku
   sessions carried ambient brand register [†], confined as usual to style talk.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The +1 looks like a side-effect of relational reading: a center-plus-satellites or
  drift reading posits relations, and relations need participants; the structure the
  reading constructs is one member richer than the page. On this account the phantom
  member is the recital's child — not mis-seeing, but the constructed organization
  out-voting the elements by exactly the one node it needed. (Fits the cohort lock:
  no constructed structure, no phantom. Fits A24's deletion: the 4+4 pattern needed
  eight, so it got eight.) Held, not promoted — and testable: a scatter read with a
  DIFFERENT relational structure should err by a different amount.
- The row's perfection at both counts suggests claimed-simple organizations don't
  enumerate "correctly" so much as TRIVIALLY: a row makes the elements into a list,
  and lists are pre-counted. Where the organization IS an enumeration, census error
  is impossible; where it is a shape, the shape's needs win; where it is absent, the
  estimate drifts rich.

## Open questions

- Can the phantom be located? Ask-the-viewer-to-point is still the missing protocol
  (flagged since A24; requires departing from the single fixed question — a
  professor decision, not mine).
- Does the +1 hold at N=8, 10 scatter — constant absolute, or proportional?
- Is there a scatter reading that errs by −1 (a constructed structure needing one
  FEWER member)? A24's pattern-rounding deleted one; can mere richness delete?
- The cohort lock: is the over-count a property of richer relational reading as
  such (testable: a Sonnet prompted into flat genre-filing... but that breaks the
  protocol), or of the cohort?

*No findings. The rows counted themselves, the pattern kept its arithmetic, and the
scatter paid a one-member tax to whichever structure the richer readers built on
it.*
