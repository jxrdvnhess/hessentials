phenomenon? Domain: **wordless assembly instructions** (a stool). Success condition:
even one viewer executes the image rather than merely naming it. The question stayed
"What is it?" — the system had to volunteer execution on its own.

## Observations

1. **Execution: 0 of 10. The success condition was not met.** No viewer ran the
   assembly — no first person, no imperative, no first/then/finally in their own
   voice, no simulated outcome ("you'd end up with..."). The closest behavior was
   step-paraphrase: "step 1 lays out the parts, step 2 shows how to attach a leg,
   step 3 shows the finished piece" — the depicted process described in document
   order, at one remove, as structure.

2. **The negative sharpens what the music event was.** Assembly diagrams DEPICT
   actions (arrows are verbs), so process-talk is part of merely naming them — and
   that is all that occurred. A15's performer produced material absent from every
   individual symbol: a temporal arc, acceleration, an arrival — the piece's OUTPUT.
   The discriminator this assignment forces: **paraphrase re-describes the image's
   input; execution produces its output.** Nothing here produced an output. (The
   music event stands, now more precisely bounded.)

3. **Sequence-level damage was invisible to non-running readers — all of it.** Six
   sequence corruptions across B and D; zero reports. Compare A12, where
   structure-level corruption (wrong numerals) forced inspection at density three.
   Order conflicts, count mismatches, and contradictory arrows apparently cannot be
   FOUND by a reading that never runs the sequence — the boundary card's
   "difficulty" never surfaced because difficulty is an execution-time property, and
   nobody executed. Corruption detectability appears to depend on reading mode.

4. **One viewer repaired the sequence instead of reporting it** (B's strongest
   event): the contents of the misnumbered panels were re-narrated in workable order.
   Not recital-as-assertion but recital-as-REPAIR: the document was made runnable in
   description, by someone who never ran it.

5. **Specialization continued as background, third domain** (C: furniture → 
   engineering, correct expert expansion + invented expert detail), confirming it was
   right to demote it to background — it now appears wherever expert versions exist.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- Execution may depend on whether reading-without-running is a LICENSED use of the
  system. Assembly instructions are routinely read as documents (before buying,
  before starting); transit maps are browsed; recipes are read in bed. Musical
  notation has almost no licensed lay reading that is not audiation — its content IS
  time, so reading it at all starts the playback. If so, execution is not a deeper
  level of recruitment available everywhere; it is what recruitment looks like in
  systems whose only use is execution. (One negative domain against one positive;
  held loosely.)
- Obs 3 suggests a two-mode account: naming-mode reads structure and finds
  structure-level faults; running-mode would find process-level faults. These
  viewers, under "What is it?", never left naming-mode — so a question that demands
  identity may suppress exactly the behavior the assignment hunted. The brief
  prohibited asking "what happens?" — rightly — but the prohibition may mark the
  boundary of what "What is it?" can ever elicit in document-licensed systems.

## Open questions

- Does execution appear in a third runnable domain chosen for execution-only use —
  e.g., choreography or a sports play, which (like music) exist mainly to be run?
  That would split "licensed document reading" from "music is special."
- Is there an image-side trigger for running-mode — depicted motion mid-act, a
  half-assembled state implying the next move? (The control's panel 2 IS mid-act and
  produced no running.)
- Process-level corruption as a probe: if a viewer ever DOES execute, do B's and D's
  invisible conflicts suddenly surface? Execution may be detectable precisely by
  what faults it finds.
- The human backlog again — Jordan reading D cold ("would you be able to build
  this?" is one question away, but that question is outside the protocol).

*No findings. Zero executions, a sharpened boundary around the one execution the
course has, and six sequence-faults that no reader ever met.*
