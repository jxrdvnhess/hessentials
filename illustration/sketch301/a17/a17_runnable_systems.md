# Sketch 301 — Assignment 17: Runnable Systems

As assigned: does execution depend on the type of system being viewed? Domain chosen
to satisfy "exists primarily to be run": **basketball play diagrams** — a play is a
plan for an execution and nothing else. No success condition this time; the course is
measuring conditions, not chasing outcomes. Name / describe / run kept as separate
categories throughout.

## Materials

- **A — control:** half-court give-and-go: 1 passes to 2 (dashed), cuts to the rim
  off 5's screen. Clearly runnable.
- **B — damaged:** grammar intact, member-level corruptions: a **duplicate "1"**, an
  illegal **"6"** on the floor, one cut arrow exiting the court entirely.
- **C — specialist:** a sideline out-of-bounds set labeled **SLOB — BOX**: box
  formation, X defenders, screens, numbered sequence tags, a switch arrow.
- **D — boundary (runnable-looking, hard to run):** two passes converging on one
  empty spot, two cuts crossing to that same spot, a route that loops back on itself,
  a screen set across a teammate's own path, sequence tags out of order.

Fresh viewers: 11 sessions (A×2, B×3, C×3, D×3 per record). "What is it?" only.

### The two executions (both on the damaged card, both Sonnet)

- "**Player 1 starts at the bottom with the ball, passes to player 5** near the elbow
  (shown with a dashed arrow), **player 5 cuts toward the basket, and player 6 cuts
  from the left wing toward the corner or beyond.** A solid arrow shows **player 1
  also moving or cutting hard toward the left corner after the pass.**"
- "...the arrows show the movement: **player 1 passes to 5..., 5 receives and drives
  or cuts toward the basket, and 1 cuts hard to the left corner**... the kind of
  diagram you'd see in a coaching playbook or a play-design app like FastDraw or
  HomeCourt."

Temporal connectives in the viewer's own voice ("starts," "after the pass"), agents
acting in order, and output-material no symbol depicts: "**receives and drives**" (no
drive is drawn), "**cuts hard**" (no intensity is drawn). By A16's discriminator —
paraphrase re-describes input, execution produces output — these are runs.

### What the runs did to the damage

Both executions ran straight THROUGH the member-level corruption: the illegal 6
played its role in both narrations ("player 6 cuts from the left wing"); the
out-of-bounds route was softened to "toward the corner **or beyond**"; the duplicate
1 was silently merged or recast. No anomaly was reported by anyone, runner or namer.

### The boundary card

Nobody ran it — and so nobody met its conflicts. The converging passes, crossing
routes, and self-looping cut were read as "players mov[ing] toward the basket **in a
coordinated sequence**." Difficulty, again, never surfaced in naming-mode (A16 Obs 3
holding: difficulty is an execution-time property).

## Observations

1. **Execution reappeared — in the run-first domain.** 2 clear runs in 11 sessions
   here; 0 in 10 sessions on assembly instructions; 1 in 3 specialist-sheet sessions
   on music. The condition hypothesis ("execution occurs where the system's primary
   purpose is performance") gains its first discriminating support: same protocol,
   same question, different domains, different outcomes. (Three domains, small n —
   carried, not promoted.)

2. **The A16 discriminator worked as an instrument.** Output-production separated the
   two runs from nine namings cleanly: "receives and drives," "cuts hard," "after the
   pass," and (soft cases) "to get open for a scoring opportunity," "to disrupt the
   opposing team's offensive flow" — purpose and intensity that exist nowhere in the
   ink. Naming responses, including expert ones, never exceeded the diagram.

3. **Execution did not surface member-level damage.** Both runs incorporated the
   illegal sixth player without friction and re-legalized the out-of-bounds route.
   Refinement of A16 Obs 3 forced by data: running exposes SEQUENCE faults (in
   principle), not roster faults — a six-player give-and-go runs fine. The strong
   test — someone running the conflict-laden boundary card — has still never
   happened; the two runners drew the runnable card, the boundary card drew namers.

4. **Both executions came from the same viewer type** (Sonnet), as did A15's
   performer. Across three domains Haiku has named and recited but never run.
   Viewer-capacity may be a second coordinate of the execution condition, alongside
   domain. (Cohort observation, n tiny.)

5. **Expert wrongness continued on schedule:** one specialist-card viewer read the
   offense as a DEFENSIVE alignment — "defenders positioned to pressure and limit
   inbound passing options... commonly employed in tight game situations" — fluent,
   detailed, inverted. And the play-name recital ran in the control ("give-and-go,"
   "pick-and-roll" — the second one wrong). Whatever system is in power recites
   (A12–A16, unbroken).

6. **The domain's vocabulary leans toward running even in namers:** "during game
   execution," "to create scoring opportunities," "designed play." Run-first systems
   may keep execution near the surface of even their naming reads — the soft-outcome
   clauses appeared here and never in assembly.

## Explanations (held separately, tentative)

- The execution condition now has two candidate coordinates: the SYSTEM's primary use
  (run-first vs document-licensed) and the VIEWER's capacity or habit (one cohort
  runs, one doesn't). The data cannot yet separate their weights — a run-first image
  before many viewers of both kinds, or a document-system before known runners, would
  begin to.
- Why both runs landed on the damaged card: possibly noise (n=3 per card); possibly
  the damaged card is the SIMPLEST (three labeled players vs five) — runnability may
  track low cast-count more than correctness. If so, the illustrator's lever for
  inviting execution is economy of agents, not cleanliness of grammar. (Unearned;
  flagged for design rather than belief.)

## Open questions

- Does anyone, ever, run the boundary card — and if so, do its conflicts finally
  surface as experienced difficulty? This remains the missing cell across two

- Cast-count vs correctness (the explanation above): a clean 3-player play vs a clean

- Haiku has never executed anything. Capacity, style, or chance?
- The inverted expert reading (offense as defense): are run-first systems MORE
  vulnerable to polarity errors because every diagram implies an opponent?
- The human backlog, standing: a human who plays basketball reading p4 would be the
  richest single session the course could buy.
