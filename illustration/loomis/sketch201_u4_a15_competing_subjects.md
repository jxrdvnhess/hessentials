# Sketch 201 · Unit 4 · A15 — The Competing Subjects

One photograph: a grey-bearded man perched on a carved pale-stone seat with a spiral-scrolled
end, against a plain sunlit plaster wall. He wears a brimmed hat, a red bow tie, suspenders
over a white shirt, brown trousers; he looks off to the side. Three elements could each be
called the subject. For each: the strongest evidence that it is, the strongest evidence that it
is not, and the evidence that would force the call. No psychology, no story, no symbolism — only
what is visible. I am not crowning a winner; the point is the competition.

---

## Subject A — The man

**Strongest case for:** he is the only living thing and the largest single mass in the frame;
his head sits in the upper third with the most internal detail (beard, hat, face); his body
sets the frame's diagonal, and his off-to-the-side gaze throws a line across the picture that
nothing else answers. Everything else holds still while he is the only thing oriented.

**Strongest case against:** he is pushed to the right and up, ceding the entire bright lower-left
to the stone; his face is turned away, engaging neither camera nor any element in the frame; the
stone equals him in brightness and exceeds him in sculptural contrast. The crop deliberately
keeps an object as large and lit as he is — a portrait would not surrender half the frame.

**What would force the call:** a tighter crop from hat to waist, dropping the stone, would settle
him as the subject. If the chosen frame keeps the full scrolled stone at equal weight (as it
does), the photograph is declining to make him the sole subject.

---

## Subject B — The carved stone seat

**Strongest case for:** it is the brightest, highest-contrast form in the frame and the only
carved/sculptural one; the spiral scroll is lit to show its volume and fills the lower-left
quadrant; the man is arranged *onto* it, so the composition is literally built around it as its
base. In pure visual weight it rivals the figure.

**Strongest case against:** it is cropped — its full shape is cut off — and a subject is rarely
shown incomplete; it performs the job of a seat (a support for the figure) rather than standing
alone; nothing in the frame attends to it, and the eye, once on the man's head and gaze, travels
up and out, away from it.

**What would force the call:** remove the man. If the stone still holds the frame as a composed,
complete image, it was the subject. If removing the man leaves a cropped, purposeless fragment
(which the tight crop suggests it would), the stone was structure, not subject.

---

## Subject C — The assembled wardrobe

**Strongest case for:** the garments are coordinated and set against a plain plaster wall that
isolates them — brimmed hat, the red bow tie (the single saturated accent and strongest colour
note in an otherwise warm-neutral frame), suspenders over white, brown trousers. The blank
backdrop and the deliberate coordination are how a clothing image is staged.

**Strongest case against:** the garments are spread across the whole body, not displayed or
isolated; no single piece is framed as a product; the beard and the stone both pull attention
off the clothing; the bow tie, the strongest wardrobe cue, is small relative to the figure and
the stone.

**What would force the call:** a crop to the torso — hat-to-waist, bow tie and suspenders against
the bare wall, stone excluded — would make the wardrobe the subject. In the full frame, with the
face and the stone competing, the clothing reads as an attribute of the man, not the subject.

---

## What the contest comes down to

The deciding evidence is the **crop**, because all three claims are real and the frame as given
refuses to resolve them: it keeps the man, the whole scrolled stone, and the full coordinated
outfit all at comparable weight. Tighten to the face and it is a portrait; tighten to the torso
and it is a clothing image; remove the man and test whether the stone survives as a whole — and
it does not. On the visible evidence the strongest single claim is the man (only he survives
every crop that still reads as a complete picture), but the frame as composed is genuinely shared
— which is the condition the assignment is about: the subject here is chosen, not found.
