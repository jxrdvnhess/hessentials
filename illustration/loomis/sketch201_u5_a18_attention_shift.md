# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A18 — The Attention Shift

One photograph: a lean, shirtless man standing on a dry rocky hillside, one hand raised to
his brow (sunglasses on), patterned swim shorts, white sneakers; sparse dead brush, exposed
dirt, a band of hot sky. Unit 5 rule observed: no beauty, no style, no "like" — only what the
image is doing. Evidence is salience only: contrast, chroma, size, position, edges, orientation.

---

**Part 1 — The first thing a viewer notices: the sunlit bare torso.**
It is the brightest large area in the frame — lit skin against a dull mid-brown hillside — and it
sits dead center. It carries the highest local contrast in the picture: bright skin meets the
dark patterned shorts at the waist in a hard light-to-dark edge, and the same lit skin stands off
the low-chroma ground. It is the only vertical mass in a frame whose landscape runs horizontal.
Brightest + highest-contrast + central + largest coherent mass: it is taken first.

**Part 2 — The second thing: the patterned swim shorts.**
The rest of the frame is browns, tans, pale dirt, and pale-blue sky — all low chroma. The shorts
are the single high-chroma, hard-patterned block in it (saturated blue-and-teal stripes), and
they sit center-frame directly beneath the brightest mass, so the eye drops from torso to shorts.
Their stripe edges are the busiest small-scale contrast anywhere in the picture. Color and edge
density, not size, earn them second.

**Part 3 — If the torso disappeared, what becomes visible: the hillside itself.**
Remove the lit figure and the frame resolves into its actual setting — sparse dead brush, exposed
rock and dirt, a high horizon, a band of hot sky. What currently reads only as "backdrop" would
be revealed as an arid, sun-struck slope. The place is fully present in the frame now; it is just
not being looked at.

**Part 4 — What is hard to notice because the torso exists.**
Two things, both losing to the bright center:

- *The raised hand at the brow and the sunglasses* — the one action in the frame. They are small,
skin-toned, and high in the picture, set against the equally lit face and arm, so they hold almost
no contrast of their own. The brightest mass and the saturated shorts keep the eye at the body's
center, and the gesture — the only thing the figure is *doing* — arrives late, if at all.
- *The character of the landscape* — the dryness, the dead brush, the exposed rock. It is low in
contrast and pushed to the edges; the lit torso anchors the eye centrally, so the setting's
specifics stay unread.

**The cost.** The torso's brightness buys instant legibility — "a person, here" — and pays for it
by suppressing the gesture and the place: the one action the figure performs and the one
environment it stands in. Attention spent on the body is attention not spent on what it is doing
or where it is.
