# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A38 — The Necessary Detail

Base = the back-arc alone (A36's strongest structure). Add one detail at a time — not to identify
the calf, but to help the structure do its job (read as a reclining body). Five attempts. Image:
`loomis/sketch201_u5_a38_necessary_detail.png` (generator `_a38_necessary_detail.py`). Run, not
predicted.

The five: arc + **ground**, arc + **belly** (close the body), arc + **head & ear**, arc + **folded
foreleg**, arc + **hump**.

## Which detail helped the structure most → least

1. **Belly — most.** The arc is a top edge; the belly supplies the bottom, and the two close into a
**volume**. The line stops being a line and becomes a reclining body — with no head, legs, or ground.
It completed the relationship the arc was already implying.
2. **Head & ear.** Orients the arc — front vs rear — and declares "animal." It does not build the
body, but it tells the structure which way it faces and what it is.
3. **Ground.** Establishes "resting above a surface," which the arc can't say alone — but it leaves
the body hollow (a back-line hovering over a line), so it helps less than closing the volume did.
4. **Folded foreleg.** A leg, but it floats below the front with a gap; with no belly to attach to,
it reads as a stray mark more than a grounding limb. Modest.
5. **Hump — least.** The identity-mark from A33 contributes the least *to the structure's job*. It
names the breed (zebu) but does not make the arc more of a body or more clearly reclining.

## What this says

The details that helped the structure are the ones that **completed a relationship it implied** —
the belly closing the volume, the head supplying orientation. The detail that helped least is the
pure **naming** mark (the hump): it identifies without organizing, so it does a different job than
the structure needs done.

This refines "a detail may fail alone and succeed in company." It does — but the *kind* of success
depends on the detail. The belly would have floated alone (as things did in A37); in company with
the arc it is transformative, because it finishes the body. The hump in company merely rides along —
it labels, it does not strengthen. So the **necessary** detail, once structure exists, is the one
that completes the structure's relationships, not the one that names the thing.

(And, against my habit: I'd have guessed the ground would help as much as the belly — it helped less,
because "above a surface" is weaker than "a closed body." The experiment corrected the guess. Kept to
this calf and these five; organizing-completion details *helped most here* — not yet a law.)
