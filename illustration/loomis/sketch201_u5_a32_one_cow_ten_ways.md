# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A32 — The One Cow, Ten Ways

One cow held constant — the zebu calf from A31, studied from a single photograph (facing right,
weight settled back on the left hip, head lifted and turned slightly toward us, a small withers
hump, dewlap, near foreleg folded forward, hind tucked). Drawn ten times, each answering one
question. Depth, not variation. Image: `loomis/sketch201_u5_a32_one_cow.png` (one shared model,
ten lenses; generator `_a32_one_cow.py`).

## What each lens surfaced about *this* calf

1. **Mass** — the body is bottom-heavy and rearward: the hindquarter and barrel hold almost all
   the dark; the head/neck is a small mass cantilevered off the front.
2. **Outline** — the contour is two long curves: a humped topline (poll, dip, hip) and a low
   belly line, pinched together at the folded foreleg.
3. **Weight** — the load sits *back and low*: the brisket and folded legs press the ground, the
   hip carries the heaviest mass; the head reaches forward as light, unweighted cantilever.
4. **Balance** — the centre of gravity drops well inside a long base of support (foreleg-hoof to
   rump); this animal is in its most stable possible configuration — it cannot tip.
5. **Joints** — under the calm exterior the legs are sharply folded: the foreleg jackknifes
   (shoulder→elbow→carpus→hoof) and the hind is doubled under (hip→stifle→hock→hoof). Resting is
   not relaxation of the joints; it is acute flexion of them.
6. **Negative space** — the shapes *around* the calf are as specific as the calf: the wedge under
   the lifted neck, the gap between folded foreleg and belly. The pose is legible from its holes.
7. **Major volumes** — one connected chain: head-ovoid → neck-tube → shoulder/hump → barrel-ovoid
   → hindquarter-sphere. The animal is these solids flowing into each other, not parts abutting.
8. **Directional flow** — a single arc carries the whole figure, from the muzzle up over the poll,
   down the neck, along the back to the hip and around the rump. Everything else hangs off that line.
9. **Posture** — the attitude is *lifted-front over settled-rear*: an alert head on a long, planted
   ground-line. Watchful and at rest at once.
10. **Fewest marks** — five strokes identify it: the humped back, the head tilt, the forward-folded
    foreleg, the hindquarter to ground, the brisket on the ground. Below five it stops being *this* cow.

## The lesson

A31 found that ten cows differ. A32 found that one cow contains ten — that mass, weight, balance,
joints, volume, flow are not the same statement seen twice but six different true things about the
same animal. And it answers the A31 note directly: the flow, volume, and balance lenses only work
by seeing relationships — how the hip flows into the barrel, how the head's weight is carried by
the base — not by locating parts. (Left open, per the last critique: whether richer rendering would
deepen these further. It would; the silhouette was my use of the medium, not the medium's ceiling.)
