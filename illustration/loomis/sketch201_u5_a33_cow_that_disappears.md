# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A33 — The Cow That Disappears

The same zebu calf, reduced. Ten line drawings, each with strictly fewer marks than the last
(12 → 3), removal only, down to the minimum that still preserves *this* calf — not "a cow."

logo, not styling — an observational test of what survives reduction.

## The removal order (what collapsed first → what refused to leave)

Dropped earliest: the **ground line**, the **muzzle**, the **tucked hind hoof**, the **far ear**,
the **dewlap**, the **under-neck line**, the **belly-on-ground line**. Then the **head-tilt** mark.
Last to go: the **rump-to-ground** curve. The final three that remained: the **humped back-arc**,
the **big ear**, the **folded foreleg**.

## What this showed

- **What collapses first is locating and finishing detail.** The ground, the muzzle, the dewlap,
  the far ear, the belly line — these place the calf and finish it, but the drawing keeps reading
  as the calf without them. Local, isolated features die early.
- **What survives longest is relational.** The back-arc is not a part; it is a *relationship* — the
  whole topline as one connected sweep (the same line A32's flow lens found carrying the figure).
  The folded foreleg is a relationship (leg to body to ground). These outlast every isolated detail.
- **The "this cow / a cow" line runs through the hump.** The single bump at the withers — a small
  deflection in one arc — is what keeps the figure a *zebu*. Flatten that arc and the drawing slides
  from this calf toward a generic cow, even with the same number of marks. A great deal of the
  identity rides on a very small, very specific deviation.
- **The big ear carries the head once the head-tilt is gone.** At four marks the explicit head
  direction drops, yet the ear's position still reports where the head is. One mark stands in for a
  whole region.

## The minimum, stated plainly

Three marks hold it: a humped topline arc, a big ear at the front, a foreleg folded forward. Read
together they say *zebu* (hump, ear) and *resting* (folded foreleg, low arc). Remove any one and it
falls toward ambiguity — not instantly to nothing, but out of "this calf." I'll put it no more
strongly than that: these three are the last to survive *in this reduction*, which is evidence
about what the identity leans on, not a claim that three marks are the whole of the animal.

A30 asked what survived the article; A31, what survived observation; A32, what survived analysis.
A33: what survives reduction — and the answer here is the relationships (the arc, the fold) and the
one small breed-specific deviation (the hump), not the catalogue of parts.
