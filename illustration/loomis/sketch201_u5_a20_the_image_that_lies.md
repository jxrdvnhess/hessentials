# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A20 — The Image That Lies

One ordinary photograph: a man reclining on a grassy bank at the edge of a calm lake, barefoot,
ankles crossed, trees across the water, bright daylight — holding a phone up in both hands and
looking at the screen. No tricks, no illusion. Evidence is visible fact only; the question is
what the frame permits versus what the viewer supplies.

---

**1. What the image encourages the viewer to believe first.** A person at ease in nature — a
peaceful lakeside, the kind of scene read as unplugged escape, being present outdoors. The cues
are loud and large: calm water fills the foreground, green trees stand across it, grass and a
tree trunk frame him, the light is bright, and he is barefoot and reclined with ankles crossed.
Every dominant element says *a body relaxed in a natural place.*

**2. Visible evidence that complicates that belief.** He holds a phone up in both hands, and his
head and eyes are directed at the screen — not at the water, not at the trees. His attention
terminates on the device. The reclining posture with a raised phone is the posture of looking at
a screen in any location; the landscape sits around and behind him, unregarded. The body is in
the lake setting; the attention is on the phone.

**3. Did the image lie, or did the viewer arrive too quickly?** The viewer arrived too quickly.
The image lied about nothing: the phone is fully visible, held centrally in both hands, and the
gaze fixed on it is plain. The frame asserts only *a person reclining by a lake* — which is true.
The part that was wrong, "present in nature / escaping the screen," was never in the frame; the
viewer supplied it from the loud setting cues and skipped the small grey phone. Nothing is hidden.
The evidence for the correct reading and the evidence that defeats the first reading are the same
object, equally present.

---

**Why the false reading forms (sequence, from A18–A19).** It is an order effect. The setting is
large, colorful, and foreground — it is seen first and installs the belief before anything else
is read. The phone is small, grey, and held close to a similarly-toned body — it is seen second,
and by the time it arrives the "nature escape" reading is already set, so the phone reads as a
detail inside that scene rather than as the thing that overturns it. The image did not deceive;
the loudness of the setting simply got there first. Same recurring split, noted not theorized:
attention (the setting) and subject (where the man's own attention goes) point in opposite
directions.
