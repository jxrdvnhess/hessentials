# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A27 — The Illustration That Survives

Article: **"What the Cows Know"** (Practice / Inner, by Jordan Hess —
`content/practice/what-the-cows-know.md`). Read completely. Below is *residue* at three reading
durations — not summary, not theme, not lesson — then three illustrations matched to the three
residues, and the one that survives all three, defended from the residue and the article's
structure rather than from taste. First image-making assignment of the course.

---

## Residue

**1. After a one-minute read.** A herd of cows lying down in a field on a drive between Houston
and Austin. Two ways of knowing — one in the head, one "lower and faster." The body examples: you
walk into a room and something is wrong; your stomach drops before you finish the sentence. Birds
leaving before the storm. The phrase "the cows aren't guessing. Neither are you." And the closing
turn: you knew too, you just stopped listening.

**2. After a one-hour delay.** The cows lying down, and one proposition: the body reads something
before the mind can name it. The specific examples (the room, the message, the birds, micro-
expressions) have thinned to a single residue — *your body knows first* — still attached to the
image of the settled herd. The mechanism and the list are mostly gone; the cows and the claim remain.

**3. The next day.** A field of cows lying down. That is nearly all that is left — the opening
picture, carrying by itself the one line that survives with it: *they knew before you did; so did
you.* The argument, the structure, the examples have compressed back into the single image the
piece began and ended with.

---

## Three illustrations

- **A — serves the one-minute residue.** A person stopped just inside a doorway, the body checking
before the mind does — the article's vivid human example ("you walk into a room and something is
wrong"). It serves the rich immediate read, when the human moments are still held.

- **B — serves the one-hour residue.** The herd lying in the field beneath a low, heavy sky — the
sign and the thing it reads (weather coming). It serves the hour-old residue, *cows down = something
is coming.*

- **C — serves the next-day residue.** The field of cows lying down, sky plain, nothing added — the
bare image that remains a day later.

---

## Which illustration survives all three?

**C — the herd lying down in the field.**

The defense is in the article's own structure, not preference. The cows are the only concrete image
the piece carries, and it is *bookended*: the article opens on them ("a field where the cows were
lying down… a whole herd, settled into the grass," line 9) and closes on them as the takeaway image
("Watch the field next time you pass one. If the cows are down, something is coming. They knew before
you did," lines 33–35), naming them mid-way as the through-line ("The cows aren't guessing. Neither
are you," line 29). Everything else — the two ways of knowing, the room, the message, the birds, the
erosion of self-trust — is abstraction hung on that one image.

In the residue test the course has run since A10, the abstractions and the inventory fade first; the
concrete bookending image is what persists. So the lying-down herd is present at every duration: it
is the one-minute opening, it is still attached to the hour-old proposition, and it is the whole of
what remains the next day. Illustration **A** serves only the immediate layer — its doorway moment is
one mid-article example among several, not bookended, and by the next day it is gone. Illustration
**B** adds a storm sky, but the rain is the article's *explanation* of what the cows sense, not the
residue; the residue is the readable posture (cows down), not the weather. Only **C** sits in all
three residues, because the article built the herd to be exactly that — the thing left standing when
everything said about it has fallen away.
