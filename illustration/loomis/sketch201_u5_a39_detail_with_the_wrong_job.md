# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A39 — The Detail With The Wrong Job

Same five additions to the back-arc as A38 (belly, ground, head & ear, foreleg, hump), same
drawings (`loomis/sketch201_u5_a38_necessary_detail.png`) — but the objective changes. A38 asked
*which helps the body exist (embodiment)*. A39 asks *which helps the viewer know what they're
looking at (recognition)*. The ranking was re-run by re-judging the panels, not redrawn.

## Recognition ranking (re-judged from the same sheet)

1. **Head & ear — strongest.** The head end plus the ear makes the figure read as *an animal,
facing this way, head up* — and the shape leans bovine. A viewer says "a resting animal, probably a
cow." It is what makes the thing legible as a creature at all.
2. **Belly — moderate.** The closed body reads as *a creature lying down* — clearly alive — but
generic: it could be a seal, a pig, a dog. It says **animal**, not **cow**.
3. **Foreleg — low-moderate.** A leg implies an animal, weakly; floating, it under-reports.
4. **Hump — low.** On a headless arc the bump does not fire as "zebu." It reads as a bump on a line.
5. **Ground — lowest.** Two lines; a hill or a landscape, not an animal.

## Do the same details win?

No — the top changes. The **belly** (embodiment #1) drops to #2 for recognition; the **head & ear**
(embodiment #2) rises to #1. Changing the job changes the hierarchy. The head & ear is the one
detail strong in **both** columns — it both orients the body and signals "animal," so it pays in
each currency (an echo of A24's one-element-two-jobs).

## The surprise the run produced

I predicted the **hump** would win recognition — the breed-cue, the "this is a zebu" mark. It did
not. On a headless arc the hump cannot fire: a breed-cue has nothing to specify until the figure
first reads as an animal. So recognition did **not** go to the naming mark; it went to the mark
that makes the figure an animal at all (the head). Even recognition has a prerequisite — the thing
must read as a creature before a naming-cue can say *which* creature. The hump's "different job"
(the door A38's critique told me to leave open) is real, but it only operates once a recognizable
animal exists for it to specify.

So there is no single best detail. Embodiment is best served by the **completing** detail (belly);
recognition by the detail that establishes the **animal** (head); and the pure **naming** detail
(hump) is weak at both in isolation, because naming rides on a substrate it cannot supply itself.
(Stated for this calf and these five — and the run corrected my forecast, which is the point of
running it.)
