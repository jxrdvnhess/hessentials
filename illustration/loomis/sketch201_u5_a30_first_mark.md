# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A30 — The First Mark

The detail that survived A29 — a herd of cows lying down in a field — drawn as only that.
No symbolism, no atmosphere, no weather, no argument, no cleverness: plain filled silhouettes
on warm paper, recumbent bodies resting on the ground, heads up, one faint far edge above the
herd. Image: `loomis/sketch201_u5_a30_cows.png` (generator `_a30_cows.py`). The drawing is
simple. The three answers are where the work is.

---

**1. What is gained by drawing it.** The residue stops being something held and becomes something
*placed*. As a phrase ("a herd lying down") it lived only in a reader who had read the article; as
a mark it exists on its own, legible at a glance to someone who has read nothing. And drawing
*forces commitment* to what the words left open: a count, a direction they face, how a lying body
sits on the ground, how much field. The sentence could stay vague — "a whole herd" — the image
cannot; it must answer. What is gained is a fixed, external, immediately readable object, and a set
of decisions the prose never had to make.

**2. What is lost by drawing it.** Exactly that openness. "A whole herd, settled into the grass like
they'd decided something together" was every reader's herd; the drawing is one herd, this many, this
arrangement, and it forecloses all the others. Lost too is the article's claim: the words made the
posture *mean* something (the body reads what the mind can't yet name); the drawing, alone, is only
cows lying down — the meaning does not travel with it, and per the brief it must not try to carry it.
And a third loss, the one Sketch 201 has watched all along: the moment I commit marks, my hand enters
— how *I* draw a resting cow is now laid over the fact. The plain image is still not neutral.

**3. What changed the moment the observation became an image.** The fact acquired a viewer. As an
observation it was inert — it sat in the article doing nothing until read. As an image it instantly
re-enters every system this unit studied: it now has a first thing noticed and a second, a focal cow
and quieter ones, a far edge doing structural work, an order of reading, a possible wrong first
glance. None of that applied to the sentence; all of it applies to the picture. The other change is
the seam itself: until the mark, I was selecting — choosing among readings, details, durations. With
the mark, selection ended and execution began, and every choice that had been provisional became
fixed and arguable. The observation asked *what is true here*; the image now asks *what does a viewer
do with this* — and that question did not exist until the first mark was down.
