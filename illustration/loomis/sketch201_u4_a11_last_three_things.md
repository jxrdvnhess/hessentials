# Sketch 201 · Unit 4 · A11 — The Last Three Things

Unit 4 is selection: of what survives, what *deserves* to remain. Nothing added — only
removal. Method as before: one careful look, then the photo is closed; the three are written
from what refused to leave, before reopening. Evidence first; reflection kept shorter than the
evidence.

## The three that remain  *(written with the photo closed)*

1. Two old men sharing one bench, side by side.
2. Both eating — each with his head down over his own food, not turned to the other.
3. A harbour behind them: moored boats and masts on grey water.

---

## After reopening — four answers

**On reopening:** all three are accurate. Left man — bald, dark zip jacket, grey trousers,
white sneakers, a green bag on the bench beside him, eating from both hands. Right man — pale
flat cap, cream jacket, grey trousers, brown shoes, legs crossed, eating. Behind: moored
boats, masts, a motorboat, grey water. A blurred dark foreground (a rail or heads) frames the
bottom.

**Q1 — Why did these survive?** They are the load-bearing facts, not the detailed ones. The two
men are the largest, most central, highest-contrast masses in the frame. Eating is the only
action present — the one thing *happening*. The harbour is the entire reason two old men are
sitting there. Who / what they are doing / where: remove any one and the picture stops making
sense. Everything that didn't survive was an attribute hung on these three.

**Q2 — What survived that surprised me?** Two things. First, they survived as a *pair* — "two old
men," not two individuals; neither man's identity came back, only the pairing. Second, a
relationship survived as one of the three elements in its own right: *each over his own food, not
turned to the other* — together but separate. That is not an object; it is a relation, and it
held as firmly as the bench did.

**Q3 — What disappeared completely?** Their individuality — the bald head versus the capped head,
the dark jacket versus the cream one, all of it collapsed into "two old men." Also gone: the green
bag, every clothing and shoe specific, the crossed leg, the blurred foreground, the particular
boats, and all colour beyond "grey." None of it came back, and the cap — which I did register in
the look — did not make the three.

**Q4 — Which of the three becomes the single black-and-white image?** The second. Two seated dark
masses side by side, heads inclined over their hands, a thin band of masts behind — the bench and
the harbour reduce to a few marks, and the picture is the togetherness-yet-separateness of two men
each eating alone beside the other.

---

**Reflection.** Selection kept the structure and discarded the attributes: who, the one action, and
the place that explains them; nothing else earned its place. What made the cut was never the most
detailed thing — it was the most load-bearing, and one of the three was a relationship rather than a
thing. The harbour staying (when A10's staging fell away) is worth only noticing, not explaining:
there, location was navigation and left; here it is the reason, and stayed.
