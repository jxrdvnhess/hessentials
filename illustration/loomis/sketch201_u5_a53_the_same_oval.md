# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A53 — The Same Oval

Tests A52's banked claim: can a relationship change the *kind* of thing the viewer believes they are
looking at? One filled oval, identical in shape, size, and position, in six panels; only the
relationship differs. Judged blind — captionless, shuffled, each object named before labels. Page:

## Blind readings (recorded before labels)

On the small table: *sculpture, an exhibit.* On the line with ripple ticks: *a buoy, floating.*
Hanging by a curved line from the bough: *fruit — a plum.* On the plain line: *a stone.* At the end
of the straight vertical line: *a plumb bob.* In the crossing sticks: *an egg in a nest.*

## Writeup (≤150 words)

**Observations.** Six panels, six nouns: sculpture, buoy, fruit, stone, plumb bob, egg. The object
never changed. Finer: the bough's *curved* line read grown (fruit); the straight vertical line read
manufactured (plumb) — same topology, different noun, so the character of the connecting mark also
names. The nest named most decisively. Two ripple ticks were enough to make water, and water enough
to make a buoy. The plinth assigned not a species but a status: a made thing, displayed.

**Explanations (separate).** The claim survives this page: the relationship reached inside the
object and renamed it. Boundary, though: A52's stone stayed a stone in five panels; the oval changed
kind in all six. Candidate: a relationship names the object only as far as the object leaves the
question open — ambiguity is the opening. Caveats: one reader, who also built the page. *(~150
words)*
