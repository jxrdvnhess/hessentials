# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A44 — The Honest Expert

A43 studied bad marks a novice adds. A44 studies good marks an expert adds. Start from the coalition
calf and add five marks that each make it *better* — not richer, not prettier. The restriction: each
mark must solve a weakness the calf **actually has**, not a generic virtue or a hypothetical. So the
work began by looking at the bare calf and naming what was genuinely missing, then solving only that.

## What the bare calf was actually missing

Looked at first, no marks added. Real weaknesses, not wished-for additions: it is a quadruped
standing on one leg (no hind leg); the head joins the body only along the topline (the neck has no
underside, so the head hangs off a wire); the legs taper to points at the ground (no weight); the
head is two stacked dark balls with no front (no face plane); and the single foreleg makes the front
read as a thin tripod.

## The five expert marks

**Hind leg**
1. *Problem the drawing had:* a four-legged animal with no rear leg — the rump dives into the ground
and the body looks unsupported behind.
2. *What it solves:* gives the rear a means of standing; the calf becomes a quadruped.
3. *New debt:* it commits the stance and gait, and it must hang from the hip and land on the ground;
a wrong hind leg reads worse than none.
4. *Why worth it:* rear support is the single most genuinely missing element — without it the animal
isn't standing, it's collapsing. The largest real fix in the set.

**Throat / neck underside**
1. *Problem:* the head connected to the body only along the top; the neck had no bottom edge, so the
head floated on the topline.
2. *What it solves:* closes the neck into a tapering mass and binds the head to the body as volume.
It ends exactly where the foreleg begins, so jaw → chest → leg becomes one continuous front edge.
3. *New debt:* an underside line risks becoming a dewlap (A42 found that marginal) or a second
contour (A43's interior-line danger); it must stay taut and land on the brisket.
4. *Why worth it:* the head was the drawing's most-attended element and it was attached by a single
wire. Giving the neck two edges turns an appendage into an animal.

**Far foreleg**
1. *Problem:* one front leg read as a thin tripod; the chest had no depth.
2. *What it solves:* the implied second front leg, set behind and lighter, gives the front two points
of contact and a little dimension.
3. *New debt:* it must stay subordinate (lighter weight, clear overlap) or it competes with the near
leg — the exact failure A43's spots showed.
4. *Why worth it:* a standing profile quadruped reads as flat and unstable on a single front leg.
The smallest of the five gains, but real — and it pays its debt by staying grey and behind.

**Hooves**
1. *Problem:* the legs tapered to points where they met the ground; the animal looked tip-toe.
2. *What it solves:* small masses at the three contacts give the legs weight and a place to land.
3. *New debt:* they must sit exactly on the ground line, or they read as beads.
4. *Why worth it:* weight at the contact is what makes the legs bear the body rather than dangle.
Cheap fix, clear gain.

**Muzzle (nostril + mouth)**
1. *Problem:* the head was two overlapping dark balls — a lump, not a face; the eye alone couldn't
carry it.
2. *What it solves:* a nostril knockout and a mouth slit give the muzzle a front, so the head reads
as facing and breathing rather than as a blob with an eye.
3. *New debt:* it commits the expression and the exact facing; it must sit correctly on the muzzle.
4. *Why worth it:* the head is where attention goes (A42); resolving it from lump to face is a large
perceived gain for two small marks.

## What the run found

Every one of these is an addition — the same *act* A43 condemned — yet the after is clearly the
better drawing: a supported quadruped with a face and weight, where the before was a topline, a
belly, one leg, and a blob. So the difference between A43's lies and A44's improvements is not the
number of marks or the presence of detail. It is whether the mark answers a question the drawing was
already asking. A43's marks answered questions the calf never posed (illumination, texture, a second
surface) and so introduced rivals. A44's marks answered questions the calf was visibly failing —
*how does the rear stand, where does the neck end, what does the head face, what bears the weight* —
and so they completed rather than competed.

That is the companion to the warning: detail is not the enemy and minimalism is not the rule. The
test is the same accounting from A41–A42, run honestly from the weakness side first: find what the
drawing is already failing to do, and add only the mark that does it. (Said of this calf and these
five — the distinction observed here, not a doctrine of when detail is allowed.)
