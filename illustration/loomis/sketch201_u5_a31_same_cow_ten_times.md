# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A31 — The Same Cow, Ten Times

Ten resting cows, each drawn from a different real photograph (Unsplash, "cow lying down,"
observed live through the browser). No field, no atmosphere, no herd, no meaning. Only: what
does a resting cow actually look like? Image: `loomis/sketch201_u5_a31_cow_studies.png`
(generator `_a31_cow_studies.py`). Reconstructed from observation, not traced.

## What the real photographs showed — that A30 did not

A30's cows were a symbol I already held: a symmetric loaf with a small head and ears as two
top-triangles. Looking at real resting cows corrected almost every part of that:

- A resting cow bears its weight on the **brisket** (the sternum), body settled to the ground —
not on hidden legs. The belly is a heavy rounded barrel near the grass.
- The **near foreleg folds forward** and the lower leg and hoof reach out along the ground; the
knee bends. A30 had no legs at all.
- The **hind legs tuck to one side** — the cow lies slightly on one hip, so the body is
*asymmetric*, not a mirrored loaf; the upper hind/stifle is a big rounded mass and the hock and
hoof fold forward beside the belly.
- The topline is **two masses, not one dome**: the withers/shoulder, a dip at the loin, then the
**high hip** (the hooks) — most pronounced on the angular Holstein, rounded on the beef breeds.
- The **neck is short and low** when the head is up; the head is a **forehead plus a projecting
muzzle** (two lobes), not a knob.
- The **ears are large and out to the side** — a strong cattle cue — not little triangles on top.
- A **dewlap** of loose skin hangs under the throat (heaviest on the zebu).
- Breed changes the silhouette: the zebu's **withers hump** and pendulous ears; the Highland's
**horns and shaggy outline** and low-carried head; the Holstein's **bony high hip**; the beef
breeds' rounded bulk.

## The ten

Each is a configuration I actually saw, varied so the looking shows: 1 zebu calf (hump, big ears,
deep dewlap, leggy); 2 Highland, head low, horns, shaggy; 3 Highland in profile, horns, head up;
4 a plain cow head-up on a slope; 5 Holstein, angular high hip, short tucked foreleg; 6 a black
calf, leggier; 7 a head-low "grazing rest," neck stretched forward and down; 8 head low with
horns; 9 a heavy Limousin, horns, high hip, foreleg well forward; 10 a relaxed meadow cow.

## Note on the gap this exposed

The drawings are still economical silhouettes, not full tonal studies — that is the medium's
limit, not the observation's. What changed from A30 is that the structure is now *read from real
cows* (brisket, folded foreleg, tucked hind, two-mass topline, big ears, dewlap) and the ten
genuinely differ because real resting cows genuinely differ. Selection found that the cow
survived; this is the first work that went and looked at what survived.
