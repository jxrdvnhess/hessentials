# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A22 — The Thing That Changed Jobs

One photograph: an older man at the near-left end of a long wooden table that recedes
diagonally back to a bright window wall full of plants; he leans toward an open laptop, hands at
the keyboard; the table carries a glass, a phone, papers, cables and spans the lower half of the
frame. The task names *function* only — what each element does for the image — not importance,
subject, or meaning. Evidence only; no aesthetics.

---

**1. The most obvious element.** The man. He is the only figure and the nearest large form, set
at the left foreground; the highest local contrasts in the frame sit on him (the glasses and
face, the hands on the pale keyboard). Among a surface of small objects, a human form is what the
eye lands on. He is seen first.

**2. The element that organizes the image.** The table. It is a single surface running from the
lower-left foreground diagonally into the right background; it spans the entire lower half, carries
every object in the frame, and sets the room's depth through its recession. It splits the picture
into two bands — the working surface and its objects below, the windows and room above — and its
receding edge is the dominant line everything is arranged along.

**3. Are they the same thing?** No. The man is the obvious element; the table is the organizing
one. The thing seen first is not the thing structuring the frame.

---

**What job each performs.**

- *The man — focal mass and terminus.* His function is to be the point the eye arrives at and
rests on: the single figure, the near anchor, the local-contrast peak. He holds the near-left
corner of the composition. He does not arrange anything else in the frame; the objects and the
room do not align to him.

- *The table — structural spine and route.* Its function is to build the space and move the eye
through it: it establishes the perspective and depth, supports and aligns every object (the man
included, placed at its end), and partitions the frame into surface and room. Its recession runs
from the bright background down to the near end — so the organizer's job includes *delivering* the
eye to the focal mass. It is what the image is built on; it is not what is looked at first.

**The functional point.** The two cooperate without overlapping: the table routes attention to the
man, and the man terminates the route the table lays down. Neither does the other's job. The most
obvious element is not organizing the frame, and the element organizing the frame is not the one
seen first — which is the A21 observation made concrete: the thing that carries the structure can
sit entirely apart from the thing that carries the eye.
