# Sketch 201 · Unit 4 · A17 — The Illustration Choice

Article: **"The single object you carry."** (Practice / Daily, by Jordan Hess —
`content/practice/the-single-object-you-carry.md`). The task is not to draw, and not to style:
only to decide which illustration deserves to exist, defended from the article's own evidence.
No aesthetics, palette, or symbolism.

---

**1. What the article appears to be about.** Choosing a meaningful object to keep on your
person — a talisman, an heirloom, a ring you bought yourself, a pocket knife. On the surface it
reads as a piece about *what to carry*: the right personal object, selected well.

**2. What the article is actually about.** Attaching a *named decision* to a physical object so
the body can recall that decision through weight and touch at the moment the mind is most likely
to forget it. The object is explicitly interchangeable — *"What it is doesn't matter. That you
chose it on purpose, for a reason you can name, does."* The subject is the decision and the
contact, not the object: *"The object is a memory device… The object is just where you parked the
memory."*

**3. What survives after the article ends.** One concrete moment: *"When you're at the meeting
that's going badly and you reach into your pocket and feel the object, you have, for an instant, a
non-verbal reminder of who you said you were."* What stays is the act of reaching and feeling —
*"the contact is the practice"* — and the line that the object is only where the memory is parked.
The residue is a hand finding the object, not the object.

**4. Three plausible illustrations.**

- **A — The object alone.** The chosen thing by itself: a worn pocket knife, a folded photograph, a ring.
- **B — The hand closing around the object in the pocket.** The moment of contact — a hand slipped in, finding and pressing the object, the object itself barely seen.
- **C — The full scene.** A person in the failing meeting, hand drifting to the pocket — the practice shown inside the public pressure that triggers it.

**5. Eliminate two.**

- **Eliminate A.** The article defeats it outright: *"What it is doesn't matter."* An illustration of
the object makes the object the subject — exactly the *"aesthetic accessorizing"* the article names
as the error it is correcting. A would illustrate the misreading, not the piece.
- **Eliminate C.** The article forbids the scene: *"Don't talk about it. Once the object becomes a
conversation piece, it stops being a private anchor and becomes a story… The point is that it's
just for you."* A meeting, other people, a setting — these turn the practice into narrative and make
the *situation* the subject. C externalizes what the article spends its last paragraphs making private.

**6. The survivor, defended from the article — B, the hand closing around the object.**

- It depicts the practice itself, not its props: *"The contact is the practice."* The image is the touch.
- It honors the object's irrelevance the text demands: with the object barely seen inside a closed
hand, identity stays incidental — *"What it is doesn't matter."*
- It is the one action the article actually stages: *"you reach into your pocket and feel the object…
a non-verbal reminder."* B is that sentence, and nothing more.
- It keeps the practice private, as required: a hand in a pocket says nothing aloud — it satisfies
*"it's just for you"* where the scene (C) would betray it.
- It locates the memory where the article puts it: *"the object is just where you parked the memory"* —
the remembering happens in the reaching, which is the only thing B shows.

B is the sole illustration that depicts the practice (contact), respects the object's
interchangeability, and stays private — the three claims the article is actually built on.
