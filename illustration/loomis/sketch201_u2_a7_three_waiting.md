# Sketch 201 · Unit 2 · A7 — Three People Waiting

Three men, the same action: sitting and waiting on a bench. No drawing, no rig, no
construction. Observations are behavioural — not *what fact exists* but *what the fact is
doing*: where attention goes, what the hands do with the empty time, how the body holds the
wait, which direction the person points.

- **Person 1 — brock** (young, jacket open, park bench)
- **Person 2 — dali** (older, hooded, autumn light)
- **Person 3 — podmatch** (blazer, metal bench, B&W)

---

## Person 1 — thirty observations

1. He gives his full weight to the backrest rather than holding himself up.
2. One ankle is crossed onto the opposite knee — a figure-four that opens him outward.
3. The raised foot juts past the line of his body, taking extra space without apology.
4. His hands lace together and rise to his mouth, parking his attention behind them.
5. The clasped hands occupy the wait without reaching for any device.
6. His gaze sits level and outward, grazing the scene rather than fixing on one thing.
7. The whole torso angles back from vertical — he is in no hurry to stand.
8. The crossed leg locks the pose; rising would take two moves, not one.
9. His shoulders sit down and loose, not lifted toward the ears.
10. The open jacket falls away from the chest; nothing is held closed or guarded.
11. He occupies the middle of the bench, not its edge.
12. The planted foot lies flat and relaxed, carrying little weight.
13. His chin tips lightly toward the laced hands, the head a few degrees off vertical.
14. No object sits in his lap and nothing beside him is watched — the hands entertain themselves.
15. His attention is ambient, not aimed — a person passing time by watching time pass.
16. The pose would read the same at one minute or twenty; it is built for either.
17. Nothing about him is braced — the bench holds him, he does not hold the bench.
18. The knees fall open, the crossed leg widening the base.
19. The hands at the mouth quiet him without hiding him; the eyes stay engaged above them.
20. He has surrendered verticality — the spine curves back into the slats.
21. He sits deep enough that the bench, not his legs, sets his height.
22. No part of him points toward an exit or a line of arrival.
23. The raised ankle and the clasped hands make two relaxed locks, both saying "staying."
24. His weight rests back over the hips, not forward over the feet.
25. The pose is composed above the waist and casually crossed below.
26. He never checks a time or scans for arrival; the wait is not an interruption.
27. The body is comfortable being seen waiting, unbothered by stillness.
28. He treats the bench as a place to be, not a place to wait.
29. His ease is expansive — he spends space rather than conserving it.
30. The waiting is incidental to him; he would sit like this with nothing to wait for.

---

## Person 2 — thirty observations

1. He folds forward and to one side, curving in around a single supporting hand.
2. One hand rises to cover the lower face, the wait turned inward.
3. The supporting elbow props on the knee, so the hand bears the head's weight.
4. His gaze leaves the scene, fixed off to the side at nothing in particular.
5. The covered mouth and the distant gaze together close him off from anyone near.
6. The free hand is not busy — no device fills the time.
7. His shoulders round forward, the chest slightly collapsed over the propping arm.
8. The head is given to the hand rather than held up — fatigue, not alertness.
9. He looks through the middle distance, not toward any point of arrival.
10. The whole posture contracts inward; he takes less space than he could.
11. There is no readiness to rise — the weight has sunk into the seat and the arm.
12. The collar stands up around a lowered neck, narrowing the body further.
13. His stillness is heavy, not poised — the wait seems to be lasting.
14. The hand at the face is self-soothing, done as if unobserved.
15. He is the oldest of the three, and the wait reads as endured rather than passed.
16. The eyes are soft and unfocused, attention turned to thought, not surroundings.
17. Nothing in the body tracks the environment — he has gone somewhere internal.
18. The supporting hand braces the head; without it the head would drop further.
19. His back-curve and forward fold make a closed, comma-shaped silhouette.
20. He performs no ease for the camera; the pose is unselfconscious.
21. The weight pools down and forward at the propping elbow.
22. The free arm rests low and adds nothing to the pose — disengaged.
23. He is alone inside the wait, not watching others move through it.
24. The lowered head shortens the neck and sinks it into the collar.
25. The hidden mouth keeps whatever the wait costs him off the face.
26. Time appears to weigh on him; the body has settled past patience.
27. There is no object of attention ahead — he is not waiting *toward* anything visible.
28. A single hand does all the work of the pose; the rest has given up effort.
29. His occupancy is interior — present in body, departed in attention.
30. The same bench, but the wait has become a place to disappear into.

---

## Person 3 — thirty observations

1. He leans forward off the backrest, holding his torso up instead of giving it away.
2. His elbows plant on his thighs, bracing the upper body in a ready set.
3. The hands clasp low between the knees — occupied, but aimed down and forward, not at the face.
4. His feet plant flat and wide, set to take his weight the instant he rises.
5. The forward lean points his whole front toward something ahead.
6. He holds his gaze outward and level — present to the scene, not escaping it.
7. The knees spread to make room for the dropped, clasped hands — an open base.
8. Nothing reclines; the spine stays lifted through the forward fold.
9. He could stand in a single motion — the pose is perched on the edge of rising.
10. His weight carries forward over the feet, not back over the hips.
11. The blazer stays squared on the shoulders; he holds a presentable line.
12. The clasped hands are still but ready — not fidgeting, not hiding anything.
13. He sits toward the front of the bench, not sunk into its depth.
14. His attention is directed and steady, as if waiting *for* someone expected.
15. The forward posture reads as anticipation, not fatigue.
16. He neither slumps nor sprawls; the body stays composed and contained.
17. The head is carried above the shoulders, not propped or dropped.
18. His stillness is held, not collapsed — alert stillness.
19. The elbow brace lets him hold the lean indefinitely without slumping.
20. He claims only the space his frame needs — neither expansive nor shrunken.
21. The joined hands hang in the gap between the knees, relaxed but linked.
22. He faces forward squarely; the body has a clear front and a clear direction.
23. Readiness sits in the planted feet and the forward weight — built to move.
24. He performs a little composure for the camera, and is tidy about it.
25. The wait has not slackened his posture; he holds the line he would hold standing.
26. His gaze anticipates an arrival rather than wandering the surroundings.
27. The shoulders are level and set — not dropped in ease, not rounded in retreat.
28. He uses the bench fully as a waiting place, perched to leave it when the wait ends.
29. The whole shape leans toward the coming moment, not into the present one.
30. His waiting is active and forward-aimed — the same stillness, pointed entirely ahead.

---

## How can three people perform the same action and reveal three different identities?

Because waiting is *empty* time, and the action gives them nothing to do — so what fills the
emptiness is the person, undisguised. A man running for a train is mostly the running; a man
with nothing to do but wait is mostly himself. The bench hands each of them the same blank,
and each fills it in his own grammar.

Person 1 spends the blank on ease: he gives his weight to the bench, crosses a leg out into
the world, laces his hands at his mouth and watches time pass. The wait is incidental to him —
he would sit the same way with nothing coming. Person 2 spends the blank by leaving: he folds
inward around a propping hand, hides the mouth, sends his eyes off into the middle distance,
and disappears into thought. The wait weighs on him; it is endured, not passed. Person 3
spends the blank on readiness: he holds himself forward off the backrest, plants his feet,
clasps his hands low, and aims his whole front at the arrival he expects. The wait is a poised
pause before motion.

So the identity is not in the action — all three are "waiting," and at thumbnail that label
would erase them into one. The identity is in the **relationship each body strikes with the
empty time, and the direction that relationship points**: outward into the scene, inward away
from it, forward toward what's coming. Ease, withdrawal, readiness. When the action is *doing
nothing*, the action can hide no one — the blank is identical, so only the person shows. That
is why behaviour outruns posture: posture is the shape a body takes; behaviour is what the
person does with the situation the shape is in.
