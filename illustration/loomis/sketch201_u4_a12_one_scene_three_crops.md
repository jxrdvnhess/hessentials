# Sketch 201 · Unit 4 · A12 — One Scene, Three Crops

One photograph, looked at as long as needed (this is a framing test, not a memory test):
a lean young man, shirtless in patterned swim shorts, stands on a dry rocky hillside under
hard sun, one arm raised so his hand shades his eyes while he looks off to the side into the
brightness. Three crops of the same frame. No symbolism; descriptions held to content and
emphasis, not motive or meaning.

## Crop 1 — What the photograph is about

A young man on bare, sun-struck ground, shading his eyes with a raised hand as he looks off
into bright light. The weight of the frame sits on three things together: the standing figure,
the hand lifted to the brow, and the glare-lit dry hillside around him. Stripped to its core,
the image is a person meeting strong light in an open, arid place — the lifted hand is the one
action, the harsh light and barren ground the condition it answers.

## Crop 2 — What the photograph appears to be about

At first read it is a fit, tanned, shirtless young man in patterned trunks, posed on a sunny
hillside — a lifestyle or fashion frame in which the body is the subject and the land is
backdrop. The physique, the tan, and the easy stance take the glance first; the setting reads
as "summer," and the raised hand reads as a casual model's gesture rather than a response to
anything. The surface subject is the man's body; the bright dry place is décor behind it.

## Crop 3 — What disappears if the most important element is removed

The most important element is the raised, eye-shading arm — not the body, though the body is
larger. Drop that hand to his side and the only thing happening in the frame is gone. What
remains is a shirtless man standing still on a hill. What disappears with the hand: the single
action, the looking-into-distance, and the figure's relation to the bright light — the glare
stops being something he answers and becomes plain backdrop. The physique stays intact; the
event leaves. The picture goes from *a person under hard light* to *a man posing outdoors.*

---

**Reflection.** Same pixels, selected three ways. The element that turned out to carry the
frame was not the largest thing in it (the body) but the smallest moving part (the hand at the
brow): remove it and the subject changes even though almost all the image remains. Noted as a
thing that happened here — not built into a rule.
