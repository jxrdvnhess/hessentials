# Sketch 201 · Unit 4 · A13 — The Wrong Subject

Three photographs. For each: what most viewers would say it is about, what I read as the
actual subject, and a defence built only from what is visible in the frame. No psychology, no
motive, no story, no biography — only evidence (gaze, hands, orientation, what is lit, filled
versus empty space, what the body squares to). "Actual subject" is a claim about where the
image's content and attention sit, not about meaning.

---

## 1 — The muscular man in the produce aisle

**What most viewers would say:** a fitness or physique image — a heavily built, braided man,
the body itself the subject; gym-and-lifestyle.

**What I read as the actual subject:** the act of one man closely inspecting a single ordinary
pineapple.

**Defence (visible evidence):** his head and eyes are turned down and inward onto the fruit;
the pineapple is raised in one hand to his eye level; his gaze line ends on it, not outward at
the camera. The body is static — no flex, no turn toward the lens; his shoulders square to the
shelf, not to us. Produce fills the shelves around him, placing him as a shopper. The muscle is
present but inert: nothing in the frame is doing anything except the looking-at-the-fruit. The
one action and the one point of attention both sit on the pineapple, not on the physique.

---

## 2 — The older man in the sunlit room

**What most viewers would say:** an older man relaxing in a beautiful, light-filled room — a
serene retirement or interiors image, the room the subject.

**What I read as the actual subject:** a man working, with the bright room shut out.

**Defence (visible evidence):** he leans forward into an open laptop; both hands rest on the
keyboard; his head is dropped toward the screen; his face is turned away from the windows and
the foliage beyond them. His back curves toward the work. The cleared table holds only the
laptop. The room's light and greenery are beside and behind him, unlooked-at. Every visible
vector of his body — head, hands, spine, gaze — points at the screen; nothing in him attends to
the room the surface read calls the subject.

---

## 3 — The young man in the armchair

**What most viewers would say:** a stylish young man posed in a designed interior — an
interiors or fashion frame, the man-in-the-room as a styled object.

**What I read as the actual subject:** a seated figure turned to the window light, with the
room left open around him — the outward look and the empty space.

**Defence (visible evidence):** his face and gaze are directed out the window, away from the
camera; his hands are empty and at rest, arranging and holding nothing. Large areas of floor
and wall are unoccupied. His body angles toward the glass, which is the brightest part of the
frame. A tall plant shares the corner as an equal upright form. Nothing in the frame is staged
toward the lens; his attention visibly leaves through the window, and the room is kept open
rather than filled — neither of which a "styled object in a set" would do.
