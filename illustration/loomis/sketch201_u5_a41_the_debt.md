# Sketch 201 · Unit 5 · A41 — The Debt

The same coalition sequence as A40 (belly → head → foreleg → ground → hump), re-run as
accounting. Each addition is treated not as a gift but as a **loan**: it grants a capability and,
in the same stroke, incurs obligations the other marks must service. Visual: the A40 build,
`loomis/sketch201_u5_a40_coalition.png`. The question for each: what became possible, what became
harder, what does this mark now *owe*?

## The ledger

**Belly** (closes the arc into a volume)
- *Possible:* a body exists — everything downstream can now attach to something.
- *Harder:* the body now has a fixed scale and proportion; the arc can no longer be re-read as a
hill — it is committed as *this* body's topline; and a chest gap opens at the front that something
must later close.
- *Owes / obligates:* it owes the arc agreement (top and bottom must report the same body), and it
obligates every later mark to fit the volume it set — a head must be scaled to it, a leg must reach
its ground.

**Head & ear** (body → animal, oriented)
- *Possible:* recognition; a front and a direction; the head-to-hip gesture completes.
- *Harder:* the body must now *support* a head — a believable neck attachment and correct scale
(too big reads cartoon, too small reads wrong); the open chest must be resolved because the head
points to it; the figure now has a definite front, so later marks can *contradict* the anatomy in a
way a featureless blob could not.
- *Owes / obligates:* the head owes the body a credible attachment and scale; it commits the
orientation, so every later mark must agree the animal faces this way.
- *What it did to an older mark:* it taxed the **belly** — the belly was "a body"; it is now "a body
of the right size and facing for *this* head," and must answer for that.

**Foreleg** (pose: resting, grounded)
- *Possible:* the recumbent pose becomes legible; a weight-bearing limb.
- *Harder:* the leg points at a surface that does not exist yet, so it reads as reaching into nothing;
it commits the figure to "lying" (it can no longer be read as standing); it sets a limb-scale the
other (implied) legs must match.
- *Owes / obligates:* it owes the body a shoulder/chest to emerge from — and it creates a debt the
drawing must pay next: *a ground to rest on.*

**Ground** (placement: weight, balance)
- *Possible:* the animal rests on a surface; balance is anchored; the foreleg's debt is paid.
- *Harder:* the ground commits the figure to gravity — every height above the line is now a claim
about anatomy and weight; a floating belly or a non-touching leg, neutral before, now reads as an
**error**. It fixes an eye-level that later spatial marks must obey.
- *Owes / obligates:* it pays the foreleg's debt but levies a new tax — every low mark must now
either touch it or justify the gap.
- *What it did to an older mark:* it changed the **foreleg** — "reaching into space" became "reaching
to the ground," resolving its meaning while demanding it actually make contact.

**Hump** (identity: zebu)
- *Possible:* breed identity — the hump finally fires.
- *Harder:* it must sit exactly at the withers or it reads as a deformity; it commits the breed, so
no later mark may contradict it; it invites the viewer to audit "is this really a zebu?", raising the
anatomical bar.
- *Owes / obligates:* it owes the back its placement and the head its existence — *a hump is only a
hump relative to a back, behind a head.* Its debt is the heaviest in proportion to its size: a tiny
mark that requires the whole anatomy to vouch for it.

## The accounting view

Every addition here is a loan against the rest of the drawing: a new capability bought on credit
the other marks must service. Read this way, the A40 order is not only the order of greatest gain —
it is the order in which each debt is **payable**: the head's "I need a believable body" was
pre-paid by the belly; the foreleg's "I need a ground" was paid by the ground that followed. And it
re-reads A37's failure precisely: the spots and the face, added to bare structure, *borrowed against
a body that did not exist* — the loan defaulted, and the marks floated. A mark in the wrong order is
a mark incurring a debt nothing can pay.

(Kept to this build: I'm describing the obligations these marks created here, not asserting a
universal law of marks — the recurring tax I'm trying not to pay. The smaller claim is the one the
sheet earned: *each mark, as it arrives, converts the freedoms of the marks around it into
obligations.*)
