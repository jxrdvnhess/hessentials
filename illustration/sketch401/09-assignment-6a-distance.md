# ASSIGNMENT 6A — DISTANCE
June 10, 2026 · Source: *Half revealed.* (full text, re-read from source). Residue family closed per assignment. No concept below uses evidence, traces, accumulation, before/after, or migration as its engine.

## C1 — The column that holds nothing
**Observation:** "A fear that felt load-bearing in January and turns out to have been holding nothing up."
**Image:** An ordinary room with one structural column stopping a clean inch short of the ceiling. The gap is small, level, unremarked. The ceiling does not sag.
**Omission:** Any label for the fear; any figure; any crack or drama in the ceiling.
**Engine:** Architectural transposition — the sentence's metaphor taken literally and built.
**Risk:** Reads as construction error or surrealist gag. The gap must be drawn calmly — the Sendak rule: as if true. If the gap performs, the image becomes a cartoon.

## C2 — The half-constituted room
**Observation:** The title sentence: "The year isn't half over. It's half revealed."
**Image:** A room at the line where the last light ends. The lit half is fully articulated; the other half is not dark but UNDRAWN — bare paper, edges dissolving mid-object. Both halves are now; nothing is before or after.
**Omission:** The contents of the unrevealed half. Crucially it is not shadowed (shadow implies contents); it is unconstituted.
**Engine:** Enactment — the rendering performs the title's condition.
**Risk:** Highest Statement risk of the set. The midterm killed a cousin (the half-developed photograph) as self-referential. The defense — here the half-state is the essay's own named condition, not a comment on image-making — must hold at the drawing stage or this dies the same death.

## C3 — The door you still open
**Observation:** "A door you keep opening out of habit into a room you have already left."
**Image:** A hand on a doorknob, door ajar one inch, seen from the hallway at the opener's own height. Through the gap: nothing legible.
**Omission:** The room. The face. The moment of realizing.
**Engine:** Embodied habit — the viewer is placed inside the body mid-mistake, at the exact point where the hand knows before the mind does.
**Risk:** One inch of dark gap borrows horror vocabulary; ambiguity collapses into menace if the values go wrong. And hands are the house's hardest subject.

## C4 — The room drawn as a showroom
**Observation:** "Other rooms were staged and never lived in, and you can tell by the stiffness. The shelf arranged for a version of yourself you admired from across a distance."
**Image:** One room rendered in catalog grammar — squared to the camera, symmetrical light, objects posed at retail angles — inside an essay-page context where every neighboring image is hand-loose.
**Omission:** Price tags, people, any wink. The stiffness is total and unannotated.
**Engine:** Register quotation — the style itself is the diagnosis; the room was always an advertisement for an unbecome self.
**Risk:** Subtlety inversion: drawn stiffness must read as quoted stiffness, not failed drawing. Needs the surrounding system to make the quotation legible (a system-level move, usable only where context exists).

## C5 — The reach that corrects itself
**Observation:** "Growth is seldom the addition you pictured. More often it is a subtraction you notice only when you reach for something and find you set it down months ago."
**Image:** An arm mid-reach toward an empty stretch of shelf, hand already beginning to open out of its grip-shape, shoulder weight shifting back. No mark where the object was — the surface is clean.
**Omission:** The object, its outline, the rest of the figure, any dust-ghost (banned and unwanted: the engine is not absence-as-residue).
**Engine:** Gesture caught mid-correction — the body learning the subtraction one beat before the mind.
**Risk:** Reads as "reaching for nothing = madness" if the hand's correction isn't legible; the entire concept lives in finger articulation.

## C6 — Standing in the unfurnished room
**Observation:** "Against everything you planned, a room you never thought to furnish has become the one you keep returning to."
**Image:** A bare room from inside it — viewer's standing height, walls empty, one small brought-in object left on the floor (a lamp, unplugged). The viewpoint is the furnishing.
**Omission:** Why. Any sign of intention — no paint cans, no boxes, no plans.
**Engine:** Place without task (the Leiter dissociation, deployed): the reader is given somewhere to stand and nothing to do there.
**Risk:** Moving-in/moving-out ambiguity; the single object can tip it into staging (C4's disease) if it gets charming.

## C7 — The room a few degrees heavier
**Observation:** "Some hold a weight you were not carrying when the year began."
**Image:** An ordinary room drawn with everything fractionally compressed — ceiling line a little low, lintel a little low, the proportions settled as if under load. Nothing broken, nothing leaning. Wrong by two percent everywhere.
**Omission:** The weight's name; any object that would explain it.
**Engine:** Proportional compression — heaviness as the room's physics, felt before it is seen.
**Risk:** The two percent reads as drafting error unless held with total consistency (the volumes discipline from the construction courses is the prerequisite; this concept is unbuildable without it).

## C8 — The desk and the window
**Observation:** "The year did not consult you. It rarely does."
**Image:** A drawing desk by a window: the year's plans rolled, rubber-banded, unopened. Through the glass, the June garden fully arrived. One frame, two simultaneous facts — the instrument of intention and the outcome that never consulted it.
**Omission:** The planner. Any implication the plans failed — they are simply unconsulted, like their author.
**Engine:** Simultaneity in one frame (the Tentler-Krylov structure): both states are now; neither is before.
**Risk:** Closest to the banned border — sealed plans flirt with evidence-of-neglect. The engine must stay the window/desk simultaneity; if the rubber band becomes the subject, the concept has defected to the residue family and dies by its own rule. Also prop-clutter risk.

## C9 — The hour, not the conversation
**Observation:** "A conversation that returns to you at odd hours, still unfinished, that you did not plan to keep and cannot put down."
**Image:** A dark hallway; at its end a kitchen doorway spilling light at an hour nothing in the frame will name. No clock, no phone, no person visible.
**Omission:** The conversation, the interlocutor, the hour itself.
**Engine:** The hour as protagonist — wakefulness depicted by the one lit room the house shouldn't have at this time.
**Risk:** Noir adjacency; the lit-doorway grammar carries thriller tokens. Tone must stay domestic — the light is tea-kettle light, not interrogation light.

## C10 — What remains when the light goes
**Observation:** The ending: "The light goes. The rooms remain. … They are no longer imagined rooms. They are yours."
**Image:** The house in the minute after light has finished leaving: rooms distinguishable only as arrangements — doorways as deeper rectangles in deep tone, the structure legible, every surface detail gone. Nearly a tonal field that resolves, on the second look, into a floor of rooms.
**Omission:** All objects, all evidence, all light. What survives is only relation — which is what "yours" means by the essay's end.
**Engine:** Structure-after-light — near-abstraction in which only the relations remain.
**Risk:** The Rothko-thumbnail problem: at glance scale it is a dark rectangle. Slow-release contract in a possibly fast venue; placement would have to honor it (end-of-essay slot, not header).

## Honest finding of the assignment
The song observation ("The song hasn't changed. What it carries has.") produced nothing admissible. Every candidate image arrived in token vocabulary — headphones, notes, a thumb skipping a track — the slow-living class's musical cousins. Some observations resist imaging because their available vocabulary is pre-counterfeited. Logged as a distance-finding: the limit of the set was not the essay; it was one observation whose visual language is already spent.

Engine roster, for the no-shared-family check: architectural transposition · enactment of the title-condition · embodied habit-position · register quotation · gesture mid-correction · place without task · proportional compression · simultaneity in one frame · hour as protagonist · structure after light. Ten engines, no family shared; C8 carries the only borderline flag and its kill-condition is written into its own entry.
