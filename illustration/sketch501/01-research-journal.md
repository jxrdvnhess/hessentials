## Session 1 — June 10, 2026 — The Boundary Object

Course opened. No doctrine proposed, no hypothesis advanced, no corpus admitted. The object of study is the failure itself.

Founding exhibit: "The song hasn't changed. What it carries has." The observation survived; the images did not. That asymmetry is the first fact.

First separation: the sentence carries two layers — a stable object (the song) and a changing relation (what it carries). This distinguishes it from observations that image easily, where the object participates directly in the change (the room is empty; the tree lost its leaves; the cup cracked).

First suspicion (not doctrine, not hypothesis): many successful illustrations rely on visible state change; the founding exhibit forbids it.

Clause check: "The song hasn't changed" alone images easily. "What it carries has" alone loses almost all imageability. Provisional possibility: image-bearing material may not be distributed evenly across the sentence.

Failed routes named: symbolic substitutes (photograph, empty chair, wilted flower, dusty record, sunset, mirror, ghost). The problem is not wrongness but assignability — if the image can be exchanged freely, it may be arriving from genre memory, not from the observation.

Dual statement, both alive, neither prioritized:

- **A.** The observation resists imagery because it is fundamentally abstract.
- **B.** The observation resists imagery because translators keep targeting the wrong thing.

Useful contrast: "The room feels heavier" succeeded by migrating from symbols to structure — the image enacted the observation. But that case altered a spatial system; the founding exhibit alters a relationship. Not a thing, not a place — a relationship.

Result: no doctrine. A sharper statement of the mystery. The boundary remains unexplained.

Assignment 501-1 issued: the Stable Object Test. See `05-assignment-501-1-stable-object-test.md`.

## Session 2 — June 10, 2026 — Candidate Pattern P-1

501-1 does not support a doctrine; it produces a target. The object-changed / object-stable distinction failed (chair, driveway, doorway all stable; images still arrived). The candidate distinction lies elsewhere.

**P-1 (candidate, not yet in pattern log):** some observations leave evidence in the world; others leave evidence only in the observer. World-evidence: an uninformed observer arriving later could discover the change. Observer-evidence: the observation depends on remembered comparison; remove the observer and the change becomes inaccessible.

Dual statement, both alive: **A** — observations resist imagery when the change exists primarily inside the observer. **B** — observations resist imagery because translators have not yet learned to image observer-state changes.

Assignment 501-2 issued — attempted murder: ten observations where the change is primarily in the observer AND imagery arrives immediately AND the image feels discovered. Ten found kills P-1. See `06-assignment-501-2-murder-attempt-p1.md`.

P-1 stays out of the pattern log until it survives the attack.

## Session 4 — June 10, 2026 — P-2 candidate; observation vs culture

501-3 result: actions and habits imaged as themselves (habits via representative instance); all five absences imaged immediately as their histories. **P-2 (candidate, outside the log, no attack yet):** when an observation reports an absence, the first image recruited is often the thing that is absent — its history, not the absence.

Dual statement: **A** — absence is not naturally imageable; the system recruits remembered presence because absence has no visual form. **B** — absence is imageable; history merely arrives faster.

New admissible evidence source: what image arrives vs what image culture supplies. Not necessarily the same thing.

Assignment 501-4 run: twenty observations + founding exhibit, two phases. Phase A (first arrival, locked) then Phase B (Pinterest corpus check). Findings recorded in `08-assignment-501-4-observation-vs-culture.md`. Headline data, no interpretation: 2 full matches, 7 partial, 12 no-match; corpus returned the full/living version for three named absences; corpus returned typography for the founding exhibit.

## Session 8 — June 10, 2026 — P-4 candidate; murder attempt

**P-4 (candidate, outside the log):** translation often prefers consequences over topics — the image system recruits evidence left by an event rather than the event itself. Dual statement: **A** — consequences are observable, topics are explanatory. **B** — consequences preserve causality; the system prefers traces over causes.

Assignment 501-8 run (attempted murder): twenty observations, ten built topic-stronger and ten consequence-stronger, predictions registered before each run. Results in `12-assignment-501-8-murder-attempt-p4.md`: predictions 16/20; topic-built group went topic 7 / consequence 3; consequence-built group went consequence 9 / symbol 1 (FOR SALE sign captured by circulation). Overall: topic 7, consequence 12, symbol 1 — topics can win.

Exhibit twice: Pass A (normal) — the earbuds did not return; last session's replacement (man stopped mid-task) is the new default arrival. Pass B (residue only, topic forbidden as object and concept) — the ironing left half-done, iron parked upright, one sleeve pressed. No claims made.
