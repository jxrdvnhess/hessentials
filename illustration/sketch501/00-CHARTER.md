# SKETCH 501 — TRANSLATION

Authorized: June 10, 2026, by faculty order at the close of Sketch 401. Not yet begun: this charter opens the room; primary instruction starts at the first session.

## The path here

101 — structure precedes appearance. 201 — observation outranks schema. 301 — meaning is relational. 401 — selection is contract-sensitive; excellence honors and exceeds the venue's promise (D-1, entered, killable). 501 asks what 401 could not:

> **How does a thought become an image?**

## The founding exhibits

1. **The song that resisted imaging.** "The song hasn't changed. What it carries has." (*Half revealed.*) The observation survived; every candidate image arrived in pre-counterfeited vocabulary — headphones, notes, the thumb mid-skip. Some observations resist imaging entirely. Why?
2. **The ten engines of 6A.** One essay yielded ten genuinely different images through ten different engines — architectural transposition, enactment, embodied habit, register quotation, gesture correction, place without task, proportional compression, simultaneity, hour-as-protagonist, structure-after-light. Engines, not props, are the unit of invention. What is an engine, mechanically? Where do they come from? Is the roster finite?
3. **C7 as invention.** The room two percent heavier solved an emotional problem with spatial physics — it did not select a symbol; it built a mechanism. Selection chooses among images; translation makes image-possibilities exist.

## Opening questions

1. What happens between a thought and an image? (The faculty's formulation.)
2. Why do some observations have enormous unexplored visual territory while others arrive with their vocabulary already spent?
3. Are engines discovered or constructed? Can a depleted observation be re-opened by a new engine, or is depletion a property of the observation itself?
4. Is there a translation analogue of the counterfeit class — thoughts that appear translated but were only re-tokenized?
5. What does the maker's body contribute? (401's late finding: body relationships — habit, gesture, correction — were fertile and underexplored.)
