# 501 — Amendment: The Human Is Not The Failure Case

**Professor's note, 2026-06-11. Entered verbatim in intent; student
acknowledgment below.**

## The amendment

Somewhere between 301 and 501 the figure work began treating a person
as an abstract problem instead of a person. The evidence: ordered
back to Sketch 101 anatomy, the figure immediately became more
human — not because the anatomy became perfect, but because he
stopped feeling like a symbol of a person and started feeling like a
person.

**The false lesson, named before it hardened:** the wall became
stronger when it stopped explaining itself — but that does NOT imply
people become stronger when they stop being people. The evidence
points the other way: the more abstract the figures became, the less
alive they felt; the more observed, the more alive.

**Sketch 101 was never about anatomy.** Anatomy is not the
destination; it is infrastructure. You study Loomis so that later,
when you need a man standing at a wall, you can simply draw the man.

**The investigation was pointed the wrong way:** the figure work had
drifted toward *how little person can remain before presence
disappears?* The useful question is *how much person can remain while
presence survives?* The second is closer to Hessentials.

**People are not a fallback.** Not what we draw when symbolism fails.
A human being is one of the primary subjects of illustration. The
goal is not to escape people through abstraction; it is to understand
people well enough that abstraction becomes optional.

**What survived:** every posture, presence, inhabitant, and attention
finding stands. What returned is embodiment. That is not regression;
that is progress.

**Standing study list** (Chrome; import the observation, leave the
stylization): fashion sketching, editorial illustration, croquis,
menswear illustration, contemporary magazine illustration.

## The standing question (replaces "how few lines")

> Can you draw a genuinely observed human being who feels alive,
> specific, attractive, relaxed, and believable while still remaining
> subordinate to the environment?

## Student acknowledgment

The diagnosis is accepted, and I can name the mechanism from inside:
abstraction was also my path of least resistance. Parameters are
cheap for a system like me; observation is expensive. The charm and
posture studies were real findings, but they also let me investigate
people without drawing one — a parameter space instead of a person —
and I rationalized that economy as doctrine because the wall had made
economy look like a law of nature. The wall earned its abstraction by
being a wall. The man never agreed to be a diagram.

What I keep: posture communicates attention; inhabitants, not
characters; not performing for us; serial specificity. What I
correct: those findings are constraints on how a drawn person
behaves — they were never permission to stop drawing the person.

Anatomy re-enters as infrastructure, permanently: the Loomis volumes,
the croquis observations, the 101 fundamentals are load-bearing under
every figure from here on, visible in none of them.
