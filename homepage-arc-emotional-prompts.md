# Homepage image arc — the emotional reimagining (evidence only)

**The turn.** Same drawing language, same hacienda/interior world, same on-cream
treatment. What changes is what the rooms are *about*. Each scene carries one of the
emotional themes — growing older, wondering if you chose right, the almost, loving people
you can't keep, missing former selves, fear of the future, choosing a life — but carries
it as **evidence and light, never as subject**. No figures. No captions-as-pictures. The
feeling is in what a room holds, not in anything performing it for the viewer.

**Guardrails baked in:** no obvious symbols (no clocks, no wilting flowers, no
fork-in-the-road, no single tear), sincerity over performance, tenderness and complexity
over sentiment. If a scene could be a greeting card, it's failed. Specificity is the cure.

**The arc, in order** — leaving → wondering → the almost → the unkeepable → the former
self → the unlit future → the chosen life. Seven scenes; the homepage uses a few (a beat,
a vignette, the footer), the rest become rotation and in-article plates.

Production is identical to the Mérida set: even untextured cream ground; run each prompt on
its own for full resolution; drop the keepers in the folder and I remap to `#f8f6f3`,
feather, and slot them.

## 1 — Starting over / becoming someone new · "the room between lives"

*Slot: opener or soft-bleed beat. The day you are neither who you were nor who you're becoming.*

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream paper,
> invented from imagination, not based on any existing artwork or photograph and not in the
> manner of any particular artist. A quick observational sketch made directly on the page,
> visible construction lines, a few searching and doubled, light hatching for shadow, the
> drawing dissolving softly into the bare paper toward the edges. Subject: an emptied,
> high-ceilinged hacienda room in the middle of a move, early morning, tall louvered windows
> thrown open, the pale cement-tile floor bare. This is a drawing of evidence, not of
> occupancy, and there are no people. The story is in the surfaces: several brighter,
> un-faded rectangles on the floor where furniture stood for years; two or three clean
> picture-ghosts of paler plaster on the wall; a folded canvas drop-cloth in one corner; and
> a single stretch of wall freshly lime-washed pale over the older tone, the seam still
> showing. The room caught between one life and the next. Keep only a restrained breath of
> color — clean new morning light flooding low across the bare floor, a cool grey-blue still
> held in the corners; everything else graphite on cream. Quiet, still, tender, faintly
> hopeful. Wide horizontal composition with air around the drawing. Even untextured cream
> ground, no vignette, no border, the same tone edge to edge. Avoid photographic realism,
> oil-paint finish, a full painted background, heavy saturation, postcard prettiness, any
> figures, and any farmhouse or rustic elements.

## 2 — Wondering if you're making the right choices · "the worn landing"

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream paper,

## 3 — Relationships that almost worked / romantic ambiguity · "two cups, one cold"

*Slot: margin vignette or plate. The almost — told by the light that moved on.*

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream paper,
> invented from imagination, not based on any existing artwork or photograph and not in the
> manner of any particular artist. A small, intimate observational sketch made directly on the
> page, visible construction lines, light hatching, dissolving early into a large area of bare
> paper around it. Subject: a small plaster table near a tall window in the late afternoon,
> the light going to blue. This is a drawing of evidence, not of occupancy, and there are no
> people. Two cups are left on the table; the two chairs are pushed back at slightly different
> angles. One cup is drained to its ring, the other still nearly full and long gone cold. A
> pale band of sun that once fell across the table has crossed the room and climbed the wall,
> the afternoon spent. Nothing else on the table. Keep only a restrained breath of color — a
> last warm note where the light has moved to, cool grey-blue settling over the table left
> behind; everything else graphite on cream. Quiet, tender, unresolved, faintly melancholy.
> Intimate composition with generous bare paper around the drawing. Even untextured cream
> ground, no vignette, no border, the same tone edge to edge. Avoid photographic realism,
> oil-paint finish, a full painted background, heavy saturation, postcard prettiness, any
> figures, and any farmhouse or rustic elements.

## 5 — Missing former versions of yourself / growing older · "where the mirror hung"

## 6 — Fear of the future · "the last blue minute"

*Slot: soft-bleed beat. The known room warm; the rooms ahead unlit. Apprehension held tenderly, never ominous.*

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream paper,
> invented from imagination, not based on any existing artwork or photograph and not in the
> manner of any particular artist. A quick observational sketch made directly on the page,
> visible construction lines, a few searching and doubled, light hatching, dissolving softly
> into the bare paper toward the edges. Subject: a familiar, warm hacienda room at the last
> blue minute before dark, an interior doorway opening onto an enfilade of unlit rooms
> receding one beyond the next. This is a drawing of evidence, not of occupancy, and there are
> no people. One lamp stands unlit; the near room still holds the day's last warmth; the open
> door gives onto darker rooms beyond, each dimmer than the last, the farthest nearly gone to
> blue-black. Not threatening — simply unknown. The rooms you have not yet walked into. Keep
> only a restrained breath of color — a held warmth in the near room, deepening cool grey-blue
> in the rooms ahead; everything else graphite on cream. Quiet, still, tender, faintly
> apprehensive but calm. Wide horizontal composition with air around the drawing. Even
> untextured cream ground, no vignette, no border, the same tone edge to edge. Avoid
> photographic realism, oil-paint finish, a full painted background, heavy saturation, any
> ominous or threatening mood, postcard prettiness, any figures, and any farmhouse or rustic

## 7 — Choosing a life and living with the consequences · "the chosen room" (footer / resolution)

*Slot: footer, the closing note. "This is what stayed." Not staged, not regretted — inhabited and chosen.*

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream paper,
> invented from imagination, not based on any existing artwork or photograph and not in the
> manner of any particular artist. A quick observational sketch made directly on the page,
> visible construction lines, a few searching and doubled, light hatching for shadow, the
> drawing dissolving softly into the bare paper toward the edges. Subject: a fully lived-in
> hacienda room at dusk, settled and complete, a tall shutter open to a calm evening. This is
> a drawing of evidence, not of occupancy, and there are no people. Gathered quietly in one
> warm room are all the marks of a life actually lived here — a worn pale path across the
> tile, a brighter rectangle where a rug lay for years, a clean picture-ghost on the plaster,
> a door-handle polished bright by one hand — none of it staged, all of it chosen. The room
> that was decided on and inhabited. Keep only a restrained breath of color — a warm amber
> settling low across the floor from the open shutter, a soft cool blue in the evening beyond;
> everything else graphite on cream. Quiet, warm, tender, a peace held inside the melancholy.
> Wide horizontal composition with air around the drawing. Even untextured cream ground, no
> vignette, no border, the same tone edge to edge. Avoid photographic realism, oil-paint
> finish, a full painted background, heavy saturation, postcard prettiness, any figures, and
> any farmhouse or rustic elements.
