# Mérida illustration series — generation prompts

**Our spin.** Not a cinematic Mérida photo-reel. *Drawings of evidence* — a
sun-warmed hacienda seen as what living quietly leaves behind, graphite on cream
with a restrained breath of dusk, made to be woven into the reading rather than
to stop it. Same drawing language as the june piece, in the Mérida / Mediterranean
register: lime-plaster walls, tall louvered shutters, deep arched openings,
cement-tile (pasta) or pale stone floors, an interior courtyard. Warm melancholy,
felt not performed. Refuses to be a postcard.

Four scenes, each mapped to a real slot. The footer is the anchor.

---

## Production notes (read once, applies to all four)

- **Ground.** Each prompt asks for an *even, untextured pale warm-cream paper, no
  vignette, no border, the same tone edge to edge.* That evenness is the fix for
  the "slightly off" feeling — it lets me white-balance the paper to the exact
  brand cream (`#f8f6f3`) so every drawing sits on the page identically, as ink
  on the site rather than a sheet laid on top.
- **Generate 2–3 variations of each** and keep the ones that feel most like
  evidence and least like a set. Drop them in the hessentials folder; I'll do the
  cream remap + edge feather + placement. You don't process anything.
- **Orientation matters** — it's noted per scene (wide for the footer, bleed, and
  plate; upright for the margin vignette). Generate at the largest size offered.
- **The through-line:** no people, no faces, no styled still life, no cozy nook,
  nothing arranged to look lived-in. The room tells the story by what it carries.

## 1 — Footer · "morning in the courtyard" (the anchor)

*Slot: the closing note at the foot of the page. Wide horizontal.*

> An original loose architectural pencil drawing on plain, even, pale warm-cream
> paper, invented from imagination, not based on any existing artwork or photograph
> and not in the manner of any particular artist. A quick observational sketch made
> directly on the page, with visible construction lines, a few searching and doubled,
> light hatching for shadow, the drawing dissolving softly into the bare paper toward
> the edges rather than filling a frame. Subject: the interior courtyard of a calm,
> well-proportioned hacienda in the Mérida register at early morning — a colonnade of
> deep lime-plaster arches down one side, pale stone or cement-tile underfoot, tall
> louvered shutters standing open onto shadowed rooms, a single slender tree. This is
> a drawing of evidence, not of occupancy — the kind of thing you notice only after
> living somewhere for years. A pale worn path across the stones where feet have
> crossed; a faint ring where a large planter once stood and is gone; one plain chair
> against a column with nothing on it; a shutter that no longer closes flush. No people
> and no faces, no flowers, no styled still life, no decorative clutter, walls otherwise
> bare. Keep only a restrained breath of color where the first low light falls — a little
> amber and gold across the courtyard floor, a cool grey-blue still held in the deepest
> arch; everything else graphite on cream. Quiet, still, faintly melancholy, the long
> shadows of early morning. Wide horizontal composition with air around the drawing, the
> arches receding calmly. Even untextured cream ground, no vignette, no border, the same
> tone edge to edge. Avoid photographic realism, oil-paint finish, a full painted
> background, heavy saturation, postcard prettiness, and any farmhouse or rustic elements.

# Follow-up prompts (v2) — retire the chair, vary the evidence

The single chair has become a motif rather than a trace — it repeats across scenes. These
v2 prompts drop the chair entirely and give each scene its **own** distinct signature of
living, so the four read as four different rooms a real person left behind, not a set
dressed from the same kit. Same drawing language and same even, untextured cream ground as
before.
