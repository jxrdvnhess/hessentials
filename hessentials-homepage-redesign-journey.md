# Hessentials Homepage — The Redesign Journey

*A record of how the visual direction evolved, why it evolved, and what the brand is actually trying to become. Written so the thinking isn't lost before development begins.*

---

## Where it started: the Mérida cinematic concept

The original homepage carried its emotion through cinematic, AI-generated photographs — most memorably the "two men at a Mérida villa" set, paired with overlay headlines like *"Some moments hold."* It had a genuine emotional arc. Warm light, intimacy, a relationship, a real-feeling moment you could walk into. It worked on the level of *story* — and it worked because, underneath, it was drawn from something real: the feeling of a treasured romance, beautiful and ultimately released.

That arc was never the problem. It's worth being clear about that, because it's the part worth keeping.

## Why I changed my mind on it

The discomfort wasn't "this depicts people." It was that an **AI-generated photograph pretends to be a captured memory** — it takes something that deserves honesty and renders it as a slick fiction that poses as documentary truth. It counterfeits the register of a real memory. That's the violation: not the people, not the story, but the *medium pretending to be a real photograph of something that mattered.*

The brand doctrine that came out of this:

> **Hessentials does not counterfeit human experience.**

A drawing or a painting declares itself made — an interpretation, an act of remembering. A fake photograph pretends it happened. That distinction became the line.

---

## The branches: going literal from the poem

The reaction to "fake photos of people" was to remove the people entirely and find a non-human way to carry the arc. The direction became a painted **fine-linework wallpaper** — bare winter branches in a 19th-century botanical-engraving register — performing the arc of Mary Oliver's *The Journey* as you scroll: a dense "storm" at the top, clearing "little by little" through the middle, resolving to a sparse "calm" with emerging stars, then a dark, quiet domestic footer holding one small, unattributed line:

> *"You stayed."*

Enormous, careful work went into this. A locked illustrator's "hand," a generation pipeline, a tiling method, an asymmetric pacing plan, a full prototype. The craft was real and the hand was genuinely beautiful.

## Why the branches were wrong too

Built and rendered, it didn't move me — and I finally understood why:

> **The branches never moved me. The arc did.**

We took a poem about *internal* movement — recognizing your own voice — and rendered its literal nouns (branches, wind, stars). We illustrated the *scenery* of the poem instead of its *feeling*. That's the trap of adapting something you love: you protect the literal images because they're sacred, and you end up with a diorama of the poem instead of an evocation of it.

Bare branches can't carry a story arc. They have no before-and-after, no human stakes, no intimacy. They're texture — beautiful texture, but texture. "Storm → clearing → calm" was a metaphor layered *on top of* a botanical motif that never actually carried it. The names were writing a check the images couldn't cash.

The deeper realization, reading my own writing back (*An Ecosystem*): the truest things "barely photograph well. They look ordinary from the outside." Both the cinematic villa *and* the decorative branches were **beautiful surfaces** — the trip, not the Sunday.

But my actual belief is that the meaning lives in the ordinary, invisible, unglamorous part.

I had been trying to make Hessentials a "little world of perfect beauty" — understandable, after years of instability, wanting one place that was clean and controlled.

But:

> **Perfect beauty has no arc.**

Nothing is at stake, nothing was survived. The thing that moves people is the imperfection that got through anyway.

---

# What the redesign was actually about

The redesign was never really about branches or villas.

It was about finding my own visual voice.

And the through-line is the same belief that runs through everything I've written:

> *choose well, protect the living thing, the meaning is in the ordinary.*

The brand is the **ecosystem philosophy turned outward** — toward food, home, style, ritual, atmosphere, and daily life.

Not toward performance.

Not toward escapism.

Not toward perfection.

The deeper realization:

> Hessentials is not trying to create a perfect world.
> It is trying to create a living one.

---

# The third stance

The entire redesign ultimately clarified one governing philosophy:

> Every piece passes one test before it gets in:
> does it refuse a false binary and occupy a third stance with standards?
>
> If it does, it stays.
> If it doesn't, it doesn't.

This became the emotional and philosophical filter for the homepage and eventually the entire brand.

Examples:

- **Not** fake photographs that counterfeit memory.
- **Not** empty tasteful beauty that refuses to feel anything.
- **Instead:** emotional truth rendered in a medium that openly admits it is made.

—

- **Not** emotional numbness.
- **Not** emotional performance.
- **Instead:** feeling everything, but quietly.

—

- **Not** perfection.
- **Not** chaos.
- **Instead:** discernment inside real life.

—

- **Not** aspirational fantasy.
- **Not** cynical realism.
- **Instead:** affection for ordinary life.

This is the actual emotional operating system of Hessentials.

# The emotional realization: "feeling everything, but quietly"

An important detail: the playlist image and playlist were not found references pulled from the internet. They were made personally during this period of creative and emotional clarification.

That matters because the image wasn't functioning as aesthetic inspiration detached from the self. It was evidence of an emotional state already being lived.

The image became important not because it was "on-brand," but because it accidentally revealed the emotional frequency the brand had been searching for all along.

The room was:

- layered
- warm
- cluttered
- intimate
- restrained
- inhabited
- emotionally charged without spectacle

And the title:

> "feeling everything, but quietly"

ended up articulating the exact third stance Hessentials was struggling to name.

The realization was not:

> "this should become the aesthetic."

The realization was:

> "this is the emotional register that feels true."

That distinction matters enormously.



A playlist image unexpectedly clarified the emotional register more cleanly than almost anything else.

The image was dark, warm, lived-in, intimate, layered, emotionally charged — but restrained. Dogs asleep. Papers scattered. Laptop open. Uneven lighting. A room occupied by consciousness rather than staged for spectacle.

The feeling wasn't:

> "Look how emotional this is."

The feeling was:

> "Emotion exists here."

That distinction became critical.

The emotional tone of Hessentials is not dramatic vulnerability or aestheticized sadness.

It's:

> feeling everything, but quietly.

Deep feeling with containment.

Warmth without spectacle.

Sensitivity with discernment.

Presence without performance.

This clarified something important:

The homepage should not feel like:

- an art installation
- a cinematic short film
- a symbolic poem
- a luxury magazine spread

It should feel like:

> a moving editorial house.

Layered.
Warm.
Alive.
Accumulative.
Changing.
Seasonal.
Quietly human.

And most importantly:

> less "homepage reveal"
> more "you arrived mid-life."

That became one of the clearest articulations of the homepage direction.

# The major structural realization

The homepage is not:

> a sequence of scenes.

It is:

> a single emotional atmosphere the editorial world lives inside.

This changed everything.

The original Mérida concept behaved like cinematic sequencing:

- scene changes
- emotional escalation
- visual reveals
- transitions
- narrative pressure

But real homes do not behave like films.

A real home holds continuity.

You stay in the same emotional weather for awhile.
Morning slowly becomes afternoon.
Things accumulate.
Objects move.
A room gathers evidence of life.

That realization led to a major shift:

## One static full-bleed image is likely stronger than multiple cinematic images.

Because the image stops behaving like content.

It becomes:

> atmosphere.

The editorial modules, text, seasonal changes, fragments, and layers move *inside* the atmosphere instead of replacing it.

This is quieter, more mature, more sustainable, and more emotionally honest.

---

# The resolution: paintings, not photographs

The final intersection:

- Mérida had the arc but the wrong medium.
- The branches had an honest medium but no arc.
- The answer is the intersection:

> the true feeling, rendered in a medium that admits it's a rendering.

This is why the homepage must use paintings rather than AI-generated photographs.

A painting says:

> this is interpreted experience.

Not:

> this objectively happened.

The painting preserves emotional truth without counterfeiting documentary reality.

---

# What the paintings actually need to do

The paintings should not behave like:

- gallery pieces
- symbolic art objects
- moody fine-art studies
- perfume-ad emotionality
- precious compositions

The paintings are not there to "announce meaning."

They only need to do one thing:

> create believable emotional presence.

Not absence.
Not performance.
Not melancholy theater.

Presence.

The feeling should be:

> someone you love is in the next room.

Not:

> someone has vanished forever.

# The direction (as it stands)

## Paintings, not photographs

Paint declares itself made.
It cannot counterfeit a captured memory.

The brushwork itself becomes part of the honesty.

## Presence, not faces

You feel the person — a trace, a shadow, evidence of activity, the shape a life leaves in a room — but you never see an invented face.

Presence without pretense.

## Loose and gestural

Visible brushwork.
Areas that dissolve.
Imperfection built into the medium.

The looseness is philosophical, not decorative.

It says:

> this was interpreted.
> this was remembered.
> this was lived through.

## A living editorial house

Not one symbolic image doing all the emotional work.

Instead:

- rooms
- kitchens
- counters
- books
- flowers beginning to die
- coats over chairs
- receipts
- towels
- herbs
- afternoon light
- dishes
- notebooks
- clutter with affection

The humanity lives in the residue.

## Per-pillar emotional worlds

Each pillar becomes its own quietly inhabited world:

- Recipes: evidence someone just cooked
- Style: evidence someone just got dressed
- Living: domestic atmosphere and continuation
- Practice: stillness without ceremony

"Finding yourself" does not emerge from one profound symbolic image.

It emerges from accumulation.

---

# The duality to capture

Not "Instagram vs reality."
That framing is beneath the brand.

The real duality is:

> the considered result
> and the ordinary life it exists inside.

The beautiful outfit and the messy closet.
The incredible dinner and the wrecked kitchen.
The styled shelf and the receipts underneath it.

The mess is not irony.
The mess is not comedy.

The mess is:

> proof that something real happened.

And the brand has affection for that reality.

That is:

> "Life, edited well."

Not life perfected.
Life attended to.

---

# Anti-cliché rules (non-negotiable)

The failure mode is:

- A24 grief aesthetic
- meditation-app atmosphere
- moody tasteful stock
- lonely symbolic interiors
- over-signifying
- performative softness
- designer emptiness
- "sad beautiful room" syndrome

Guardrails:

## Ordinary daylight

Not dramatic golden hour.
Not theatrical shadows.
Not emotional lighting tricks.

Drama is where cliché lives.

## Occupied and a little messy

Not staged-empty.
Not pristine.

Presence implied through activity and evidence.

## Warm, not melancholy

This is not absence-as-grief.

This is:

> presence you cannot quite see.

## No single hero symbol

No one meaningful chair.
No lone cup.
No mirror revelation.
No "sad object carrying the whole philosophy."

That is signifying.
Reject it.

## The real trick

Gestural paint + ordinary domestic life.

Loose brushwork gives it soul.
Normal Tuesday content keeps it from becoming perfume-ad artifice.

Loose paint + dramatic symbolism = cliché.
Loose paint + a slightly cluttered kitchen in flat afternoon light = Hessentials.

## Anti-mystical

Domestic.
Not ceremonial.

No altars.
No glowing spiritual reverie.
No aestheticized transcendence.

Just:

> a real life, attended to carefully.

# The next concrete step

Do not build a full system yet.

Do not create an entire painting library.

Do not finalize all pillars.

First:

> prove the emotional language.

One painting.

The hardest one.
Probably Recipes.

A real working kitchen.
Warm.
Ordinary.
Slightly cluttered.
Alive.
Anti-performative.

And judge it with one ruthless question:

> Does this create emotional presence without emotional performance?

If yes:

the visual language exists.

If not:

refine before scaling.

The lesson from the branches:

> prove the core before committing the pipeline.
