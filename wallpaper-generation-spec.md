# Hessentials Homepage Wallpaper — AI Image Generation Spec

**Status:** Generation-ready. Continuity-locked. Derives from the "Wallpaper background: continuity-lock" reference doc. Companion to the (deferred) Cowork build brief, which will cover the scroll mechanic, footer build, and headline retirement.

**What this spec produces:** A curated set of fine-linework illustrations covering three vertical zones — storm, clearing, calm — plus the seam transitions between them, plus the wallpaper content entering the footer. Painted/drawn linework only. Same hand, three states. Generated by an external AI image tool, then curated by Jordan.

**Purpose of this spec:** Lock the parameters that must NOT vary across zones (the hand), explicitly name the parameters that DO vary (density, openness), and define per-zone targets so each pass produces work that can blend at the seams. Without continuity lock, the seam handoffs are impossible no matter how good the blend logic.

---

## 1 — The continuity lock (the hand)

These parameters are fixed across all three zones, all seams, and the footer entry. No exceptions. Every prompt sent to the generator must reassert them in full. "In the same style as the previous image" is unreliable across passes; the lock must be restated each time.

**Medium.** Fine ink linework. No fills, no color washes, no halftone, no stippling clouds. Tone is achieved entirely through line density — more lines = darker; fewer lines = lighter.

**Line character.** A confident, continuous pen line with slight organic taper. Neither stiff engraving nor sketchy crosshatching. Closer in spirit to a 19th-century botanical plate or copperplate engraving, but with breath in it. Lines are slightly imperfect, slightly alive, never mechanical.

**Line weight.** Single nominal weight with light variation at terminations and overlaps. Roughly equivalent to a 0.3–0.5mm technical pen. No heavy contour lines, no thick-thin brush variation. If a branch is fatter, it's because it's drawn with more parallel lines, not a thicker stroke.

**Ink color.** Black on the warm flat brand cream ground of the site (`#f8f6f3` — the canonical `--cream-bg` token in `src/app/globals.css`). The previous plaster-texture overlay was pulled site-wide on 2026-04-29; current body is flat brand cream with no texture. Tile sources are ink-only on transparent ground; the cream is provided by the body's `background-color`. No other colors. The footer treatment darkens the ground at build time via overlay; it does not change the ink.

**Subject vocabulary — only these elements may appear:**

- Bare branches and stems, cold-northern deciduous species register — bare oak, birch, windblown maple. New England winter character (the actual landscape of "The Journey"). No leaves on branches.
- Loose fallen leaves and twig debris on the ground.
- Wind lines, drawn as faint directional flow — not arrows, not motion lines from a comic.
- Stars — small, single-point or simple six-point, varying scale.
- Open negative space, treated as a subject in its own right.

Nothing else. No flowers, no fruit, no animals, no figures (human or otherwise), no furniture, no architecture, no calligraphy or text within the artwork. Text on the site appears only as live HTML type in the footer, never drawn into the image.

**Composition orientation.** Vertical. Each motif works as a tall tileable band. Top and bottom edges must match cleanly to themselves so vertical stacking is invisible.

**What the image is NOT:**

- Not photographic, not photoreal, not photo-traced.
- Not 3D-rendered, not CGI.
- Not watercolor, not gouache, not pencil sketch.
- Not vector flat illustration, not infographic style, not lineart-with-fills.
- Not stylized in an obvious illustration register: no fashion-illustration loose lines, no New Yorker cartoon, no Ghibli, no Moebius.
- Not Art Nouveau (no William Morris repeat, no Mucha curves).
- Not Japanese woodblock / ukiyo-e linework.

**Reference register.** 19th-century European botanical engraving is the anchor and only anchor — precise, controlled, bare-line, single weight. A single contemporary illustrator who can hold that same hand in a contemporary frame is acceptable as a secondary touchpoint. No gestural / loose / sketch register is permitted, even as influence — the single-hand promise the seams depend on cannot survive a contradictory pole. The "honest as a drawing" rule from the reference doc applies — visible hand, no attempt to fool the eye.

## 2 — The variable axis

Only two parameters change across zones:

1. **Density** — how many lines occupy a given area.
2. **Openness** — how widely elements are spaced, how much negative space dominates.

Everything in Section 1 stays locked. Same hand, three states.

## 3 — Per-zone guardrails

**Note on the density percentages below.** The percentages are curation rubrics for judging finished candidates, not prompt parameters. No generator renders "70% line occupation" reliably. When prompting, steer density by describing the scene — tangle, openness, count of branches in frame, presence or absence of stars. When curating, use the percentages to judge keeps.

### Zone 1 — STORM (top of page, hero area)

**Emotional target.** The wild night of the poem. Held breath. Suppression. The voices, the trembling house. Density at maximum without losing legibility of individual lines.

**Density.** ~70% line occupation. Tangled, overlapping, wind-thrown.

**Composition.** Bare branches at multiple scales crossing each other diagonally and upward, blown by an implied wind (pick a direction and hold it across the zone). Fallen debris suspended in motion. Faint wind lines threading through. No stars yet — the sky is choked. Negative space exists only as small pockets caught between branches.

**Feels like.** Looking into a thicket in a storm. You can see, but barely. The eye wants to find a way through and almost can't.

**Does NOT feel like.** Halloween-spooky. Tim Burton. Decorative gothic. Dramatic-illustration register. Just dense and weather-thrown.

**Generation count target.** 30–50 candidates **per variant**. The storm zone uses three tile variants (storm-A, storm-B, storm-C) stacked non-alternatingly so the reader reads weather instead of pattern. Three variants × 30–50 candidates = 90–150 storm candidates total before curation lands on the three keepers. The storm zone is the hardest to keep restrained; expect to reject most for tipping cinematic or decorative. The three keepers must each independently hold the hand AND share the hand with each other — that's the in-zone continuity check.

### Zone 2 — CLEARING (middle of page)

**Emotional target.** The poem's "little by little." Things start letting go. Space enters. Not yet calm — still in motion, but the resolution is beginning.

**Density.** ~35% line occupation. Branches thin out; debris is sparser; wind lines lighter.

**Composition.** Fewer branches, more vertical orientation (less wind-thrown), beginning to settle. First stars appear — faint, small, scattered, the way they look when clouds are breaking but haven't cleared. Negative space starts to dominate slightly over linework.

**Feels like.** A breath being let out. The held tension easing without yet having released.

**Does NOT feel like.** Already arrived. Resolved. Triumphant. Sunny. This is the moment between, not the destination.

**Generation count target.** 20–30 candidates. The middle zone is the easiest to over-resolve; reject anything that feels finished.

### Zone 3 — CALM (bottom of page, above footer)

**Emotional target.** After. The storm is past. Weight set down. **Deliberate in craft, accidental in feeling** — the wind stopped, the nervous system unclenched. Not a destination, not stillness-as-achievement. "The Journey" is about decision, not transcendence; the calm is the body finding it can breathe again, not the soul arriving.

**Density.** ~12% line occupation. A few clean vertical stems, very little debris, more stars, mostly open space.

**Composition.** Two or three vertical stems standing clean. Scattered stars (still small, still simple), more visible because the air is clear. Wind lines mostly gone — maybe one or two faint traces as memory of the storm.

**Feels like.** The wind stopped. A still room at night. The breath fully out without anyone deciding to exhale.

**Does NOT feel like.** Empty / undesigned. Decorative / pretty. Inspirational / wallpaper-poster. Resolved. Meaningful-on-purpose. "You have arrived at stillness." The restraint must read as deliberate in craft, accidental in feeling — never as a destination.

**Generation count target.** 20–30 candidates. The calm zone fails three ways: too sparse (reads as nothing), too pretty (reads as decoration), or too resolved (reads as destination). Reject all three. A candidate whose density and hand are correct still fails if the feeling is meaningful-on-purpose; cut it.

## 4 — Zone heights and page length (integration point with the build brief)

Tileable motifs repeat to fill whatever vertical space they're given. If the homepage's actual scroll length isn't mapped to the three zones deliberately, the storm overstays and the clearing arrives late on a long page. The arc cannot perform itself; the build has to allocate run-length per zone.

Pacing is **asymmetric**, deliberately, to read as weather rather than as a dimmer. The storm holds past comfort. The clearing breaks fast. The calm extends. Even pacing reads as a brightness gradient; uneven pacing reads as weather. The ratio is the chief instrument of this.

**Locked allocation (Addendum B, 2026-05-26):**

- **Storm 50% / Clearing 15% / Calm 35%** of the wallpapered scroll height. Mobile-tuned: if the storm at 50% reads as oppressive on 390px width by the second scroll gesture, drop to 45% and reallocate the 5% to the calm (not the clearing — the calm extension is the payoff, the clearing's job is to break fast).
- The storm zone uses **three tile variants** stacked non-alternatingly (storm-A, storm-B, storm-C) for in-zone variation. The reader perceives weather, not pattern.
- The clearing and calm zones each use one tile.
- Total wallpapered scroll height of the homepage (hero through above-footer) must still be measured against this ratio in situ. The Cowork build brief closes that integration point. Working estimate against the current photo-arc homepage scroll height (~6500–7000px desktop): storm ~3250–3500px, clearing ~975–1050px, calm ~2275–2450px. New homepage scroll height will be different once the cinematic frames retire — likely shorter.

The generator needs the ratio (to know how many storm variants to produce) and the tile aspect ratios (to know what to generate against). Both are pinned in this spec. The absolute pixel height per zone is a build concern, not a generation concern.

## 5 — The seams (highest-risk surface)

The transitions between zones are where the entire effect lives or dies. They must read as one wall clearing over time, not three wallpapers stacked on each other.

**Seam 1 — storm → clearing — AGGRESSIVE BREAK, not gentle blend (Addendum B).** The weather breaks here. Density drops from ~70% (storm) to ~35% (clearing) over a compressed vertical run — tight, fast, decisive. Storm motifs do not thin gradually; they cut off. Clearing motifs do not appear gradually; they enter cleanly. The eye reads a moment of release, not a slow dimming. Generated as a fixed-height non-tiling piece with the break composed into the middle third of its height.

**Seam 2 — clearing → calm — GENTLE SETTLE.** Different character from Seam 1, deliberately. Clearing-density motifs thin further; stars become slightly more frequent; remaining branches simplify and orient more vertically. Wind lines disappear across this seam. The "little by little" reaches its quiet resolution here. Generated as its own pass, fixed-height non-tiling.

The two seams are deliberately asymmetric in feeling — break (Seam 1) and settle (Seam 2). Symmetric seams would re-create the dimmer effect across the page.

**Seam generation approach.** Prompt the generator with the full continuity lock from Section 1, the subject vocabulary, and the density gradient described in scene language — e.g., "the top third of the image is densely tangled with bare branches crossing diagonally, debris suspended in motion, no stars; the bottom third is sparse, mostly negative space, two or three clean vertical stems, a few small scattered stars; the middle third transitions smoothly between the two states with no banding and no hard line." Seam tiles are taller than zone tiles to give the gradient room to breathe.

**Hand check during curation.** For each seam candidate, crop it in half. The top crop must read as the same hand as the storm zone; the bottom crop must read as the same hand as the clearing zone. If either crop drifts the hand — different line weight, different ink character, different botanical species register — reject the seam, not the zones. Same hand across all crops is non-negotiable.

## 6 — Footer treatment (wallpaper entry only)

The footer is a static styled state, handled in the Cowork build brief. But the wallpaper enters the footer, so this spec defines what enters.

**Background ground.** A deepened version of the calm-zone treatment. Same warm flat brand cream ground (`#f8f6f3`), rendered ~30% darker via overlay at build time — a deep warm shadow that reads as "a lamp is on, the rest of the room has gone quiet," not "the lights are off." This is not a separate generation pass; it is a CSS overlay over the calm zone's last tile and the footer extension.

**Wallpaper content entering the footer.** Very sparse. Maybe 5–8 stars across the full footer width. Possibly one or two of the calm-zone vertical stems carrying into the top edge of the footer. No new debris, no new branches, no additional ornament around the type.

**The footer text (live HTML, not generated):**

- The line is Jordan's own, unattributed: *You stayed.*
- Site's italic serif. Sentence case as written, with the period.
- **Physically tiny.** Small type, set low in the footer, modestly placed or slightly off-center. Not hero-sized, not dramatically centered. The smallness IS the meaning — the line should read as something discovered late, overheard rather than announced, like words someone pencilled on a wall for themselves. Hero-sized treatment would turn it into a slogan — the exact failure that retired the original overlay headlines and what `Refuses to signify` (Section 7) is built to catch.
- Color: warm off-white, slightly visible against the deep warm shadow. NOT glowing. No text-shadow halo. No backlight.
- Treatment register: a lamp on the line, not candlelight on a relic. House at night, not shrine. Per the brand-wide anti-mystical rule — domestic, not ceremonial. (Saved memory: `feedback_anti_mystical_register.md`.)
- **No attribution.** No credit line, no tracked-out small caps, no source acknowledgment of any kind. The line is unattributed because it is Jordan's — and because attribution on type this small would do exactly what the smallness is meant to prevent.

**The footer must NOT:**

- Tip into Statement. The line must read as overheard, not announced. Hero-sized type or dramatic centering breaks this on contact. (See `Refuses to signify` in Section 7.)
- Reveal the line via scroll trigger. (Saved memory: `feedback_scroll_listener_scar.md` — scroll-driven JS broke analytics before.)
- Glow, halo, candle-flicker, or otherwise tip ceremonial.
- Frame the line with decorative linework, ornament, or borders.
- Vignette the corners (tips cinematic, then ceremonial).
- Attribute the line in any way. No credit, no signature, no source caption.

## 7 — Hard constraints (carry into every generation pass and every curation pass)

1. **Footer line — original, unattributed.** The footer text is Jordan's own line: *You stayed.* Unattributed. No third-party copyrighted material appears anywhere in the wallpaper or footer treatment. Mary Oliver's "The Journey" remains the private source of the wall's emotional arc — referenced as such in this spec for the species register (Section 1) and the calm-zone emotional target (Section 3) — but no part of the poem surfaces on the site. The licensing question is retired by removal.
2. **Figureless.** No people, no animals, no faces, no hands. Not in the wallpaper, not in the footer, not anywhere.
3. **Honest as a drawing.** Must read as illustration on first glance. Reject any candidate that is ambiguous — could be photo-traced, could be photoreal-with-line-overlay, could be AI-rendered photorealism dressed as line art. If you have to look twice to confirm it's a drawing, it fails.
4. **Refuses to signify.** Reject any candidate that announces its own meaning. The curation question for every frame in every zone is twofold — *is the density and hand right* AND *is this reaching to be significant*. If it reads as a Statement, an Image-with-a-message, a frame that knows it is meaningful — cut it. Hardest test on the calm zone and the footer; applied whole-wall. Sits at the same weight as figureless and honest-as-a-drawing — non-negotiable.
5. **Subordinate to content.** Quiet zones must exist where homepage content sits. The Cowork build brief will define their positions; the generation spec must anticipate them. When in doubt about density, err lower. The wall yields to the words.
6. **Mobile-first.** Each zone tile must read correctly at 390px screen width. Storm zone especially — at narrow widths, tangle can collapse into mud. Test on phone before committing to a candidate.
7. **Performance budget and delivery format — Addendum B locks (2026-05-26):**
   - **Storm — WebP** (AVIF if the generator outputs it; AVIF needs a WebP fallback for Safari < 16). Auto-traced SVG balloons on tangled linework; raster is correct.
   - **Calm — SVG.** Sparse linework auto-traces small and scales cleanly. Trace from the PNG source via vtracer or potrace.
   - **Clearing and seams — per-tile decision.** Generate as PNG, export both WebP and SVG, pick the smaller-and-still-clean winner per tile.
   - **Source format from the generator (revised 2026-05-27):** high-quality PNG, generator's native dimensions acceptable (build-side rescales to target during WebP/SVG export), **cream-baked-in at `#f8f6f3`** — not transparent ground. The transparent-ground call dated 2026-05-26 was reversed: AI image generators read transparent-ground prompts as instructions to invert the composition (photo-negative white-on-black), which destroys the hand. Cream-baked-in is the realistic source format. Build-side chroma-keys the uniform cream to transparent during format conversion (cream is uniform off-image background; ink is not — clean keying problem). **sRGB** color profile. 8-bit per channel.
   - **Tile aspect ratios (locked):** Storm 3:2 (2400×1600). Clearing 2:1 (2400×1200). Calm 4:3 (2400×1800). Seams 2:1 (2400×1200), each one fixed-height (does not repeat).
   - **Vertical tiling rule:** zone tiles (storm × 3, clearing × 1, calm × 1) tile top-to-bottom — top and bottom edges must match seamlessly. Test by stacking two copies vertically; the seam must be invisible. Seams (Seam 1, Seam 2) do not tile — they are placed once at the zone boundary.
   - The full wallpaper budget across all zones and seams must not exceed the budget the retiring cinematic photos were occupying. The launch optimization work cannot be undone.

## 8 — Curation method (the marathon)

Order and discipline matter. This is the sequence:

1. **Generate the hand first.** Before any zone, generate 10–15 test images of a single small motif — a bare oak or birch branch against open ground, no density direction, just the hand. Lock the hand from these. The chosen image becomes the reference attached to every subsequent generation prompt.
2. **Storm zone second.** Hardest to keep restrained, so do it while the hand is freshest. Generate to count, curate hard, pick one.
3. **Calm zone third — before clearing.** The two endpoints define the arc. If calm and storm hold the same hand, the clearing has room to land between them. Doing clearing before calm risks anchoring on a middle that doesn't reconcile to either end.
4. **Clearing zone fourth.** It now has two fixed endpoints to sit between.
5. **Seams fifth.** With all three zones locked, generate each seam against the hand and the two adjacent densities.
6. **Footer treatment.** Live HTML/CSS work, handled in the Cowork build brief. No generation pass.

**Rejection criteria — reject any candidate that:**

- Drifts the hand (different line weight, ink character, species register).
- Reaches to be significant — reads as a Statement, an Image-with-a-message, a frame that knows it is meaningful. Applied at every frame in every zone (see `Refuses to signify` in Section 7). Hardest test on the calm zone and the footer.
- Introduces a vocabulary element not on the Section 1 list.
- Reads ceremonial (altar, shrine, ritual lighting, reverent framing).
- Reads decorative (greeting-card pretty, Pinterest-pretty, William Morris repeat).
- Reads cinematic (vignetting, dramatic depth, depth-of-field implication).
- Reads as an obvious illustration register (Ghibli, New Yorker, fashion illustration, etc.).
- Cannot tile cleanly at the top/bottom edges of its zone.

When in doubt, reject. Most candidates will fail. That is the marathon.

**Build-side seamless conversion (added 2026-05-27).** AI image generators compose like framed pictures — density toward the center, taper to empty at the edges. Native seamless tiling is a known failure mode; prompt-level fixes hit diminishing returns fast. Don't chase native seamlessness in generation; solve it on the build side. The workflow:

- **All zones (storm, clearing, calm) — offset-and-blend with inpainting (locked 2026-05-27).** The earlier two-track approach (mirror for dense, offset-and-blend for sparse) is retired. Mirror was tested on the first storm trio and the symmetry was visible even at heavy density — the "dense tangle hides the mirror" claim didn't hold in practice, and mirror reads as a build shortcut rather than as art. The whole point of this direction is AI producing actual artwork, not clever-engineering-as-aesthetic. So:
  - **Workflow:** shift the source image vertically by half its height (Photoshop offset / `np.roll`). This moves the previously-invisible discontinuity (where original top edge meets original bottom edge) from the tile-to-tile boundary into the middle of the tile. The new top/bottom edges are former middle rows of the source — naturally adjacent in the original, so they match seamlessly when the tile repeats. Result: tile boundary is invisible by construction; in-tile seam is now the inpaint target.
  - **Inpaint pass:** the discontinuity band in the middle goes back to the generator. Build-side provides a clean offset PNG plus a marked reference showing the inpaint zone (band roughly 180px tall centered on the seam). Generator inpaints branch reconnection across the band using the surrounding tangle as context (a task generators handle well — small, scoped, with style context everywhere except the gap).
  - **Final pass:** build-side receives the inpainted result, applies cream chroma-key to RGBA, exports as WebP. The tile now tiles seamlessly with no symmetry artifact.
  - **Why this works:** generators are bad at native tiling and bad at composing whole seamless wallpapers, but they ARE good at targeted inpainting where they have visual context on every side of a small unknown region. Offset + inpaint plays to that strength.
- **Sparser zones (clearing, calm) — offset-and-blend.** Standard textile method: shift the source image vertically by half its height (Photoshop offset filter or PIL/NumPy equivalent), then inpaint the discontinuity that's now visible across the middle. The inpainting goes back to the generator (generators paint branches well; they just can't seam them well — a small targeted inpainting region is what they're good at). Build-side does the offset and the cream knockout; the inpaint pass stays artist-side.
- **Generation prompt note.** Include "edge-bleed: branches continue past all four edges with uniform density" in zone tile prompts. Candidates with dense centers and empty edges are not failures — they are inputs to build-side seamless conversion. The hand transfers; the seam is a separate problem solved on a separate surface.

This is reality winning over the brief: prompt-level seamlessness is not where the generator is strong. The hand and the weather are where it's strong. Let the generator do those; let the build do the seam.

## 10 — Known issues / deferred decisions

Issues surfaced in-context that have been deliberately deferred rather than fixed in isolation. Fixing them now would risk solving twice if surrounding context changes.

### 10.1 — Hero copy legibility against storm zone (logged 2026-05-27)

**Observed.** First in-context check (storm tiles mounted on `/wallpaper-test` route, desktop viewport ~1920px) showed the hero standfirst italic copy crossed by storm branches at the same visual weight as the letterforms. The H1 survived because the type is large enough. The standfirst did not. Density and hand are correct for storm; the problem is specifically that the densest art sits behind the most important text.

**Framing (Jordan's direction).** Fix is **separation, not dilution.** Do not lighten the wall — that would compromise the storm character. Give the hero copy its own quiet pocket.

**Three candidate approaches**, ranked by editorial preference:

1. **Sparser-density band in the tile region behind the hero.** Compose the first storm tile (or a dedicated hero-zone tile) with reduced density in the top region so the headline area sits over more cream. Keeps the storm character below; gives the type room above.
2. **Low-opacity cream gradient/scrim behind the text block only** — not the full hero. A localized soft cream glow under the type block lifts the wallpaper just where the eye needs to read. Implemented at build (CSS gradient or backdrop-blur), not at generation.
3. **Compositional rule: top of storm zone stays less dense where headline and standfirst sit.** Cropping/positioning the first storm tile so the densest area falls below the hero. Build-side, no regeneration.

**Resolution deferred.** The hero composition may shift once the real descent exists (clearing and calm in place, real arc assembled) and type treatment is decided against the actual wall (sr-only H1 question, standfirst placement). Solving legibility now risks fixing it twice — once for the current state, once for the final state. Apply only the cheap mitigation now: **modest global scale-down on storm tiles** (background-size 85% in `HomepageWallpaper.tsx`) to reduce branch thickness ~15% behind type. Real fix waits for the full arc.

**Re-evaluate when.** Clearing and calm tiles locked, full storm-clearing-calm arc mounted on the test route, type treatment for hero decided.
