## AMENDMENT II — THE HOMEPAGE IS THE COVER

**Principle.** The homepage is not a landing page. It is the current issue's cover.
Hessentials behaves as a publication, not a website. A publication does not
redesign itself to stay current — it changes its cover. The masthead, the voice,
and the standards remain.

**The two layers.** The site is two systems, and they must not be confused.

The masthead — permanent. The wordmark, the navigation, the editorial categories,
the brand promise, the typography, the structure. These are recognizable, stable,
and slow to change. *The standards stay.*

The cover — per issue. One illustration, one cover story, one theme, one
atmosphere. This is where editorial judgment lives, and it changes on a monthly
cadence. *The selections change.*

**One cover.** Each issue has one cover and one cover illustration. No rotation, no
sequence, no slideshow, no narrative arc, no scene cycling. One issue, one face.

**What the cover illustrates.** The cover illustrates the question, not the article.
Its job is to make a reader curious enough to enter — *something happened here*,
without revealing what. It sets atmosphere and theme; it never summarizes.
Explaining weakens it. The strongest cover is not the best illustration of the
story; it is the best illustration of the question the story asks.

**Evidence of process.** Visible evidence of process must originate from an actual
process. A construction line stays if a hand drew it. A ghost stays if something
was actually moved. Notes stay if they were actually made. Drafting marks invented
to look authentic — process that never happened — are decorative nostalgia, and are
forbidden. This is the line the whole brand is built on: honest making, never
counterfeited making.

**The paper.** The cover is on paper. The site is not paper. The interface stays
warm ivory, generous space, editorial typography, restraint. No site-wide paper
texture and no sketchbook surface. The paper belongs to the artwork, not the
interface.

**The archive.** Past covers are kept. Over time they become a record of what
Hessentials judged worth attention — a history of selections, not a backlog of
content.

**In one line.** The standards stay. The selections change. That is the
publication. That is the homepage. That is the cover.
